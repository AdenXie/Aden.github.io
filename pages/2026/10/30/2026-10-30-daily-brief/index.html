Daily Briefing | 30 October 2026: Alphabet plummets 12.6 per cent in a single day; US Q3 GDP grows by 3.4 per cent; PCE inflation rises to 3.6 per cent; Iran rejects Qatar’s ceasefire proposal

AdenXie
  2026-10-30 09:00 2026-10-30 09:00 Created  
  • Daily Briefing 
 
  • News

Today's main theme

This issue covers Thursday 29 October; the section on Chinese policy includes news from 26 to 29 October. The main theme of the day was ‘growth is strong, but money is being spent too quickly’. US third-quarter GDP grew by 3.4 per cent year-on-year, driven primarily by AI-related capital expenditure; however, PCE inflation rose to 3.6 per cent in September, with core PCE inflation rising to 3.1 per cent, whilst the futures market continues to price in an approximately 80 per cent probability of a Fed rate rise in December. In the stock market, investors began to scrutinise the cash flow statements relating to AI investment: Alphabet fell 12.6 per cent after its free cash flow turned negative, marking its largest single-day decline since its IPO in 2004; in after-hours trading, although Amazon’s AWS division reported 39 per cent growth, its free cash flow for the past 12 months turned negative at US$42.4 billion, causing its share price to fall by around 10 per cent at one point; Intel fell by approximately 17 per cent in after-hours trading due to weaker fourth-quarter guidance. The European Central Bank kept its deposit rate at 2.5 per cent, warning that the energy shock would push up inflation and weigh on growth. In the Middle East, Iranian officials described Qatar’s proposal as merely an “unacceptable idea”, whilst news that US Vice President Vance had written to Iranian security officials caused oil prices to fall slightly. Domestically, the 800 billion yuan in new policy-based financial instruments has been fully deployed, and the central bank had previously introduced new tools to stabilise the bond market.

Key News

1. US GDP grew by 3.4 per cent on an annualised basis in the third quarter, whilst PCE inflation rose to 3.6 per cent in September

The US Department of Commerce announced on 29 October that preliminary figures for real GDP in the third quarter showed an annualised growth rate of 3.4 per cent, higher than the 2.2 per cent recorded in the second quarter and also above market expectations. According to data cited by the Public Broadcasting Service (PBS), consumer spending rose by 3.8 per cent; business investment, excluding housing, grew by 12.7 per cent, with investment in information processing equipment rising by 37.4 per cent, software investment by 9.6 per cent and research and development investment by 7.5 per cent, with AI infrastructure being the primary driver. Residential investment fell by 3.3 per cent, marking the sixth decline in the last seven quarters, with high mortgage rates continuing to weigh on the property market.

Inflation figures released on the same day came in on the high side. According to Fox Business, the PCE price index rose by 3.6 per cent year-on-year in September, up from 3.4 per cent in August, and by 0.5 per cent month-on-month; the core PCE index, excluding food and energy, rose by 3.1 per cent year-on-year, up from 3.0 per cent in August, and by 0.3 per cent month-on-month, both in line with market expectations. Goods prices rose by 3.9 per cent year-on-year, whilst service prices rose by 3.5 per cent. The personal savings rate fell to 3.8 per cent, indicating that households are relying on fewer savings to sustain their consumption.

Taken together, these two sets of data indicate that the US economy remains robust despite high oil prices and high interest rates, but there are no signs that inflation is easing. According to Fox Business, citing CME FedWatch, the market is pricing in a probability of around 80 per cent for a 25-basis-point rate rise in December. When the Federal Reserve kept interest rates on hold the previous day, there were already two votes in favour of a rate rise; this data will give the hawks greater confidence. Key developments to watch: the October non-farm payrolls figures due in early November, political pressure following the mid-term elections on 3 November, and the October CPI figures ahead of the December monetary policy meeting.

Source:Public Broadcasting Service (PBS)、Fox Business

2. Alphabet closed down 12.6 per cent, marking its biggest one-day fall since going public, whilst Amazon and Intel plummeted in after-hours trading

Alphabet, which had released its financial results after the market closed the previous day, saw its share price fall sharply during regular trading on 29 October. According to StockTitan, Alphabet’s share price fell from US$324.89 to US$283.82, a drop of 12.64 per cent; According to Yahoo Finance, this was the largest single-day fall since its IPO in 2004, wiping nearly $500 billion off its market capitalisation. Investor concerns centred on cash flow: Alphabet’s capital expenditure for the third quarter stood at $51.4 billion, whilst free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion, and growth in both search and YouTube advertising was slowing. Microsoft, by contrast, fell only marginally by 0.16 per cent on the same day, with the market clearly distinguishing between companies where ‘investments are paying off’ and those where ‘investments are still burning through cash’. Software shares also came under selling pressure; according to Yahoo Finance, ServiceNow fell by approximately 25 per cent following its earnings report, Fortinet fell by approximately 21 per cent, and the software ETF (IGV) fell by 4 per cent.

Amazon announced its third-quarter results after the market closed. According to the company’s announcement, revenue stood at US$201.9 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 12 per cent (the company stated that bringing forward Prime Day to the second quarter had reduced third-quarter growth by approximately four percentage points); operating profit stood at US$28.1 billion, with an operating margin of 13.9 per cent—a record high; earnings per share were US$1.89, down from US$1.95 in the same period last year, primarily due to a US$9.5 billion gain on the investment in Anthropic recorded in the corresponding period last year. AWS revenue totalled $46.0 billion, up 39 per cent—the fastest growth in the past five years—with an operating profit of $17.8 billion. However, free cash flow for the past 12 months stood at negative $42.4 billion, compared with a positive $14.8 billion in the same period last year, due to a net increase of $94.8 billion in capital expenditure; The company is maintaining its capital expenditure plan of approximately $220 billion for this year. CEO Jassy stated that AWS is expected to grow by over 40 per cent in both 2027 and 2028. According to Yahoo Finance, Amazon’s share price fell by around 10 per cent in after-hours trading before the decline narrowed.

Intel announced after the market closed on the same day that its third-quarter revenue stood at US$16.5 billion, up 21 per cent year-on-year, with revenue from its data centre and AI business totalling US$7.3 billion, a rise of 78 per cent; however, its revenue guidance for the fourth quarter was set at between US$15.0 billion and US$16.0 billion, below market expectations of approximately US$17.0 billion. CFO Zinsner stated that the slowdown in the PC market would continue at least until the first half of next year. According to Yahoo Finance, Intel’s share price fell by approximately 17 per cent in after-hours trading.

This week’s earnings reports from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon have shifted the market’s focus from ‘Is demand for AI strong enough?’ to ‘Who can sustain their investment with cash flow?’. Key points to watch going forward include: the upcoming earnings reports from Apple and other companies; whether Alphabet and Amazon will adjust their capital expenditure plans for 2027; and whether more companies will turn to equity financing.

Source:StockTitan、Yahoo Finance、Amazon Announcement、Intel Filing (SEC)

3. The European Central Bank has kept interest rates unchanged, stating that the energy shock will push up inflation and weigh on growth

On 29 October, the European Central Bank announced that it was keeping its three key policy interest rates unchanged: the deposit facility rate at 2.50 per cent, the main refinancing rate at 2.65 per cent and the marginal lending rate at 2.90 per cent. The statement accompanying the decision noted that the continued rise in energy prices poses an upside risk to inflation and a downside risk to growth, and that these effects are still being felt; whilst core inflation remains broadly stable and wages have not yet shown a significant reaction, a larger and more persistent energy shock, along with its indirect and second-round effects, is expected to push up inflation and weigh on growth. The statement also noted that financial conditions have tightened since the last meeting, which will dampen both inflation and growth.

The European Central Bank reiterated that it would make decisions on a meeting-by-meeting basis in line with the data, without committing in advance to a specific interest rate path, and emphasised that it stands ready to adjust all instruments within its remit and, where necessary, to deploy the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) to counter disorderly market volatility. Holdings under the asset purchase programmes (APP and PEPP) continue to be reduced as planned.

Compared with the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank faces a more classic ‘stagflationary’ dilemma: the eurozone is more reliant on energy imports, and high oil prices have a more direct impact on growth; consequently, it has opted to adopt a wait-and-see approach for the time being, rather than considering interest rate rises as the Federal Reserve has done. Key points to watch: the preliminary eurozone inflation figures for November; whether energy prices will fall; and whether the latest round of economic forecasts at the December meeting will see inflation revised upwards and growth revised downwards. The Bank of Japan will announce the outcome of its monetary policy meeting on 30 October.

Source:European Central Bank

4. Iranian officials have described the Qatari proposal as “unacceptable”; Vance has written to Iranian security officials

According to Reuters (as reported by Iran International), on 25 October, Qatari mediators presented Iran with a draft agreement to end the war in Tehran. The proposal outlines a seven-day process in which both sides would take daily confidence-building measures, guaranteed by several countries, with the process set to begin as early as next week. However, according to Israel’s Kan News, a senior Iranian official directly involved in the negotiations stated that what Qatar had proposed was merely “an unacceptable idea, not a proposal”; Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi did not reject it outright, stating that Iran was maintaining close contact with the mediators and that Iran’s own seven-day plan could be implemented within a week. The disagreement centres on the sequence of events: Iran is demanding that the blockade of its ports and sanctions on oil exports be lifted first, whilst the US insists on addressing the nuclear issue from the outset.

Signals from the US have also been mixed. According to *The National*, citing *The Wall Street Journal*, Vice-President Vance wrote to Rezaei, head of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, expressing a desire to resume peace talks; Trump subsequently stated that he had not instructed Vance to contact Iran, claiming the letter concerned humanitarian aid. Speaking at a rally in Texas, Trump said he would ‘absolutely’ strike Iran again, and that the war would end ‘when I want it to end’. The *Wall Street Journal* had previously reported that the US had warned Gulf states to prepare for possible short-term, intensive military operations following the mid-term elections.

In Israel, according to *Israel Today*, the Central Elections Committee has completed the count of the double-envelope ballots, with Netanyahu’s camp securing 53 seats, the opposition 48 and Arab parties 19. The political landscape remains unchanged, with Likud retaining its position as the largest party with 27 seats; the official results are expected to be announced next Wednesday. Likud has accused Arab parties of ‘stealing the election’, to which the police have responded that there is no evidence to support this claim. Key developments to watch include Iran’s formal response to the Qatar draft resolution, US military decisions following the mid-term elections, and the mandate granted to Israeli President Herzog to form a government.

Source:Iran International (citing Reuters)、Israel National News、The National、Israel Today

5. The full 800 billion yuan of new policy-based financial instruments has been fully deployed, and the central bank has introduced a ‘redemption swap facility’ for the bond market

According to a report in the Shanghai Securities News on 29 October, as of 28 October, the 800 billion yuan in new policy-based financial instruments had been fully disbursed, taking less than two months from the time the first tranche of funds was released in September. Of this, the China Development Bank (CDB) disbursed 365 billion yuan, corresponding to a total project investment of approximately 5.5 trillion yuan; the Export-Import Bank of China disbursed 120 billion yuan, stimulating total investment of over 1.7 trillion yuan; and the Agricultural Development Bank of China disbursed 315 billion yuan, corresponding to a total project investment of approximately 4.39 trillion yuan. Ming Ming, Chief Economist at CITIC Securities, estimates that this batch of instruments is expected to drive investment exceeding 11 trillion yuan. Liao Bo, an analyst at Northeast Securities, pointed out that new loans to enterprises and public institutions totalled 70 billion yuan in September, reversing the negative growth seen since June, which may be linked to the commencement of the funding programme.

With regard to financial stability, according to a report republished by East Money, the central bank announced on 26 October the establishment of the ‘Bond Market Non-Bank Large-Scale Redemption Swap Facility’ (BNSF). When asset management products face concentrated redemptions and systemic pressure arises in the bond market, eligible bank wealth management subsidiaries, public mutual funds and their parent companies may borrow central bank bills from the central bank, raise funds through repurchase agreements in the market, and use these to acquire eligible bonds from the redeemed products, which are then pledged as collateral to the central bank. Together with the equity market swap facility launched in 2024, this tool forms an emergency liquidity mechanism for non-bank institutions covering both the equity and bond markets. Market participants emphasise that this is a tool designed to address extreme situations, aimed at breaking the ‘redemption–sell-off’ cycle; it is not intended as routine liquidity injection. Institutions must bear their own profits and losses, and it does not constitute a guarantee of principal repayment; specific limits and rates are yet to be announced.

On the consumption front, the Ministry of Commerce convened a national meeting on 29 October to advance the ‘trade-in’ scheme for consumer goods, stating that the scheme had driven sales of relevant product categories to over 2 trillion yuan so far this year, and calling for the acceleration of subsidy payments. Taken together, these measures indicate that the policy focus is on using policy-based funds to stabilise investment in the fourth quarter and on stabilising consumption through the ‘trade-in’ scheme, whilst simultaneously preparing tools in advance to address potential redemption shocks in the bond market. Key points to watch going forward include: the October PMI figures due to be released on 31 October; whether supporting loans will stimulate the commencement of projects; and the detailed rules regarding the BNSF scheme.

Source:Sina Finance (Shanghai Securities News)、East Money、Sina Finance (Xinhua Finance Morning Report)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

US shares fell on 29 October. According to Yahoo Finance, the S&P 500 fell by 0.30 per cent to 7,720.39 points, ending a three-day winning streak; the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by 0.25 per cent to 52,342.56 points; the Nasdaq fell by 1.38 per cent to 26,184.73 points, with Alphabet and software stocks proving the main drags. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note fell by approximately 5.6 basis points to 4.989 per cent. Regarding oil prices, according to *The National*, Brent crude closed at US$115.32 on 28 October and retreated to around US$114 during early Asian trading on 29 October; News of Vance’s letter to Iran raised hopes for negotiations, but attacks by the Houthis on Saudi Arabia and Iran’s lukewarm response to the Qatar proposal limited the extent of the decline. According to HDFC SKY, citing data from the US Energy Information Administration, US crude oil stocks fell by 1.9 million barrels last week, whilst petrol stocks rose by 3.9 million barrels.

Looking at several news stories together: the strength of the US economy stems largely from investment in AI, with capital expenditure accounting for nearly 40 per cent of GDP growth; however, this very investment is pushing Alphabet and Amazon’s free cash flow into negative territory, and the stock market’s tolerance for this has clearly diminished. On the inflation front, high oil prices are making it difficult for both the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank to ease policy; the Federal Reserve is considering raising interest rates, whilst the European Central Bank is weighing up the balance between growth and inflation. For China, with the deployment of policy-based financial instruments now complete and the renminbi strengthening, the focus of domestic policy is on stabilising investment and consumption; the technology sector on the A-share market will continue to be affected by the AI valuation adjustments in the US stock market.

Checklist:

  • The Bank of Japan’s monetary policy decision on 30 October, and the yen exchange rate
  • Upcoming financial results from Apple and others, and whether Alphabet and Amazon’s share prices can stabilise
  • China’s October PMI (31 October), as well as the BNSF guidelines and progress on the associated loans
  • Iran’s formal response to the Qatar draft; decisions regarding Iran following the US mid-term elections
  • US October non-farm payrolls and expectations for a December interest rate rise
  • Official Israeli election results and mandate to form a government

Source:Yahoo Finance、The National、HDFC SKY
