Daily Briefing | 28 October 2026: Neither of the two main blocs secured a majority in the Israeli general election; China’s industrial profits rose by 14.4 per cent in September; OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Terra

Today's main theme

This edition covers Tuesday 27 October, with some technology news dating from 26 October. The main themes of the day were ‘voting and negotiations’. Israel held its first parliamentary election since the attacks of 7 October. Exit polls from three television stations all indicated that neither Netanyahu’s camp nor the opposition led by Eisenkot would secure a majority of 61 seats; Likud remains the largest party, and the process of forming a government is likely to drag on for several weeks. In Iran, Pezeshchiyan met with Putin and the Emir of Qatar in Moscow, whilst Tehran put forward ‘seven conditions’ for negotiations; however, the Houthi rebels continued to fire missiles at Saudi Arabia and claimed to have attacked the Rastanura refinery. The diplomatic developments caused Brent crude to fall by approximately $2 during trading, whilst WTI dipped below $100. Domestically, the National Bureau of Statistics reported that profits at industrial enterprises above a specified scale rose by 14.4 per cent year-on-year in September, an acceleration of around 10 percentage points compared with August, with the electronics and raw materials sectors providing the main support. In the technology sector, OpenAI launched GPT-6.1 Terra, designed primarily for computer operations, at half the price of the previous generation of similar models. Brazilian assets rebounded slightly following the previous day’s sharp fall, with the Finance Minister pledging to uphold fiscal discipline.

Key News

1. Exit polls from the Israeli general election: Likud remains the largest party, with neither bloc securing a majority

Israel held parliamentary elections on 27 October, the first general election since the attacks of 7 October 2023. According to a compilation by *Israel Today*, exit poll results from three television stations were similar: Netanyahu’s Likud party secured 24 to 25 seats, remaining the largest party; Yashar!, led by former IDF Chief of Staff Eizenkot, secured between 21 and 23 seats, whilst Bennett’s B’Yachad secured between 12 and 14 seats. According to data from the Israeli public broadcaster Kan 11, Eisenkot’s bloc is projected to win approximately 53 seats, Netanyahu’s bloc approximately 50, and Arab parties a combined total of approximately 17; neither side is expected to secure the 61-seat majority required in the 120-seat Knesset. The *Israel Times* summarised the situation as follows: “The anti-Netanyahu camp is in the lead, Likud remains the largest party, and neither side has a clear path to a majority.”

Voter turnout was very high. According to the Jewish Telegraphic Agency, citing figures from the Central Elections Committee, by 8.00 pm approximately 74.3 per cent of voters had cast their ballots, compared with 66.3 per cent at the same stage in the 2022 general election—the highest figure in decades. The atmosphere during the election was tense: Netanyahu claimed that Arab voters were “rushing to the polling stations” and accused the Arab party Ra’am of “attempting to steal the election”; Eizenkot, meanwhile, accused Netanyahu of laying the groundwork for refusing to recognise the results. Prior to the election, Netanyahu also stated that, as long as he remained in power, Trump was willing to take action to “completely eliminate the Iranian threat”.

Exit polls are only preliminary results. According to *Time* magazine, the final count is expected to be completed by 30 October, with the official results to be announced on 4 November. President Herzog will then consult with the various parties and authorise the most promising candidate to form a government within 42 days; Should multiple rounds of coalition-building efforts fail, the current Likud government will continue to govern as a caretaker government until the next election. Key points to watch: which side the Arab parties and Lieberman’s Yisrael Beiteinu party will align with; whether Netanyahu will contest the results; and the impact of the deadlock over forming a government on Israel’s decision-making regarding Iran.

Source:Israel Today、The Israel Times、Jewish Telegraphic Agency、Time Magazine

2. The Iranian President met with Putin in Moscow, whilst Tehran put forward ‘seven conditions’ for negotiations; the Houthi rebels continued their attacks on Saudi Arabia

Iranian President Pezeshigian met with Putin on 27 October whilst attending the Gas Exporting Countries Forum summit in Moscow. According to TASS, citing the Iranian President’s Office, the two sides discussed oil and gas cooperation and the expansion of economic and trade ties; publicly available information made no mention of the war or the Strait of Hormuz. On the same day, Pezeshizian also met with the Emir of Qatar, Tamim; according to *Gulf News*, Iranian state media merely stated that the two sides discussed bilateral relations, whilst the Qatari side claimed that the talks covered the war, the Strait of Hormuz and energy supplies. In Tehran, Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei met with security official Rezaei, emphasising the need to end the state of “neither war nor peace”. Iranian official Bagheri Kani stated that the US must first meet seven conditions before Iran would consider negotiations, and warned that the US might be seeking to reach a short-term agreement before the 3 November mid-term elections, only to resume military pressure afterwards.

The conflict has shown no signs of abating. According to *Gulf News*, on 27 October, the Saudi-led coalition intercepted three ballistic missiles fired towards the eastern region and four drones fired towards the south, before subsequently intercepting two more ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh; The Houthi rebels claimed to have launched a ballistic missile attack on Saudi Arabia’s largest oil refinery in Ras Tanura, a claim that has not been confirmed by the Saudi authorities. The Egyptian parliament approved the provision of military support to Saudi Arabia, which Houthi officials described as tantamount to a declaration of war on Yemen. Both the United States and Australia have advised their citizens against travelling to Saudi Arabia. Regarding the Strait of Hormuz, the Iranian military stated that an oil tanker struck a mine and caught fire outside the Iranian-authorised shipping lanes, adding that the strait was “completely closed” to vessels that did not comply with Iranian regulations.

Key points to watch: the specific details of Iran’s ‘seven conditions’ and the US response; whether Qatar’s mediation efforts will make headway; whether the Houthi attacks on Rastanura are true; and whether the fighting will further draw in countries such as Egypt.

Source:TASS、Gulf News、Reuters (reprinted by Euronext)

3. In September, profits at industrial enterprises above a specified scale rose by 14.4 per cent, an acceleration of approximately 10 percentage points compared with August.

The National Bureau of Statistics announced on 27 October that from January to September, the total profits of industrial enterprises above designated size nationwide amounted to 6,033.74 billion yuan, representing a year-on-year increase of 15.5 per cent; operating revenue stood at 106.21 trillion yuan, up 6.6 per cent. Profits for the month of September alone rose by 14.4 per cent year-on-year, according to Caixin, an acceleration of 10.2 percentage points compared with the 4.2 per cent growth recorded in August. By sector, profits in the mining industry rose by 38.2 per cent, whilst those in the manufacturing sector increased by 17.0 per cent; however, the electricity, heat, gas and water production and supply sector saw a decline of 14.3 per cent.

Growth came primarily from two sources. The first was the AI-related electronics sector: according to an interpretation by Yu Weining, Chief Statistician of the Industrial Department at the National Bureau of Statistics, profits in the manufacture of computers, communications and other electronic equipment increased by 1.0 times, driving the growth in total profits of industrial enterprises above designated size by 9.6 percentage points; profits in the high-tech manufacturing sector rose by 52.0 per cent, with their share of total profits rising to 25.5 per cent. The other driver was the raw materials sector, buoyed by oil and commodity prices: the non-ferrous metals sector grew by 79.7 per cent, the chemical industry by 49.2 per cent, coal mining by 57.0 per cent, whilst the petroleum processing sector returned to profitability. Downstream sectors came under significant pressure: profits in the automotive manufacturing sector fell by 22.8 per cent, ferrous metal smelting by 73.3 per cent, non-metallic mineral products by 46.9 per cent, and electricity and heat production and supply by 17.9 per cent, indicating that rising upstream costs are squeezing the mid- and downstream sectors. Profits for foreign-invested enterprises rose by just 0.5 per cent, whilst those for private enterprises increased by 8.7 per cent; both figures were below the overall average. The operating profit margin stood at 5.68 per cent, up 0.44 percentage points year-on-year; however, Caixin noted that the growth rate of accounts receivable reached a three-year high, indicating increasing pressure on enterprises to recover payments.

Key points to watch: the October PMI, the further squeeze on mid- and downstream profits from oil prices in the fourth quarter, and whether the strong growth in the AI-related electronics sector can be sustained.

Source:National Bureau of Statistics、Xinhua News Agency (Analysis by Yu Weining)、Caixin

4. OpenAI launches GPT-6.1 Terra: designed for computer operations, with prices halved

On 26 October, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, positioned as a fast, low-cost mid-range model primarily intended for computer operations, visual reasoning, programming and everyday office tasks, such as filling in forms, editing videos, interpreting technical drawings and automating repetitive desktop tasks. According to BleepingComputer, OpenAI states that it completes tasks more than three times faster than GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol; the API pricing is set at US$1 per million input tokens and US$5 per million output tokens, which is 50 per cent lower than that of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. In terms of test results, on the Agents’ Last Exam, Terra scored 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra at medium reasoning intensity, at approximately one-third of the latter’s cost; its score on Terminal-Bench 4.0 was 50.3 per cent, more than double that of the previous generation. OpenAI also notes that GPT-6.1 Sol or GPT-6 Astra are still recommended for the most complex programming tasks. The model is now available to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Education users, and has been launched on Codex and via the API.

The focus of this launch is on price and speed. Last week, Moon’s Dark Side launched the Kimi K3.1 Pro at approximately half the price of Claude Opus 5.5; now, OpenAI has slashed the price of its mid-range model by half, indicating that competition amongst AI agents is shifting from a focus on capability to a focus on cost per task. For Anthropic, which is preparing for its IPO, this will also dampen market expectations regarding its pricing power. Key points to watch going forward include: third-party evaluation results for Terra; whether Anthropic and Google will follow suit with price cuts; and whether more affordable computer-operated models can drive large-scale adoption in the enterprise sector.

Source:BleepingComputer、9to5Mac

5. Brazilian assets rebounded slightly following a sharp fall; the Finance Minister stated that “it is not a fundamental issue”

Following the sharp fall in Brazilian markets triggered by Lula’s re-election, Brazilian assets rebounded somewhat on 27 October. According to Reuters, the Ibovespa index rose by around 1 per cent, having fallen by 13.9 per cent the previous trading day; the real appreciated by nearly 2 per cent against the US dollar to around 5.31, whilst interest rate futures retreated. Finance Minister Durigan stated that the previous day’s sell-off reflected investors’ prior betting positions and “was not a reaction to fundamentals”, adding that the government would continue to uphold fiscal responsibility.

Market views remain widely divided. According to Reuters, JPMorgan believes the market has not yet formed a clear view of Lula’s new government and expects investors to reassess the situation in the coming weeks; investors at Armor Capital, however, view this sharp fall as a buying opportunity. The previous day, the Brazilian Ministry of Finance had cancelled the government bond auction scheduled for 27–29 October and repurchased a portion of its government bonds in an effort to stabilise the market.

Key points to watch: whether Lula’s team can swiftly announce a credible fiscal framework and the appointments to his economic team; the stance of the Central Bank of Brazil; and any legal challenges to the election result from Flávio Bolsonaro’s camp.

Source:Reuters (reprinted by Investing.com)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

On 27 October, US shares continued to rise. According to TheStreet, the S&P 500 rose by 0.60 per cent to 7,722.60 points, whilst the Nasdaq rose by 1.18 per cent to 26,477.81 points, whilst the Dow Jones Industrial Average remained largely unchanged, closing at 52,377.57 points; the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note stood at around 4.99 per cent. Tech stocks led the gains for the second consecutive day, with optimism stemming from Anthropic’s prospectus continuing to prevail; the prospectus also revealed that Anthropic expects to pay SpaceX up to US$120.5 billion for computing power by 2029, further illustrating the scale of expenditure on AI infrastructure. In terms of earnings reports, Boeing fell by around 10 per cent after reporting results that fell short of expectations and revising down its full-year delivery targets, whilst UPS reported better-than-expected results and raised its full-year guidance. Regarding oil prices, according to Reuters, diplomatic activity in Tehran and Moscow caused Brent crude to fall by approximately $2 during trading to around $113.7, whilst WTI fell below $100; however, attacks by Houthi militants on Saudi Arabia and a mine strike in the Strait of Hormuz limited the decline, with UBS raising its year-end forecast for Brent crude to around $110.

Looking at several news stories together: the deadlock over the formation of a government in Israel and Iran’s negotiating conditions have made the situation in the Middle East even harder to assess ahead of the US mid-term elections, with oil prices set to fluctuate between the prospects of ‘negotiation’ and ‘conflict’. High oil prices have already been reflected in China’s industrial profits, with profits surging in the upstream mining and raw materials sectors whilst declining in the downstream automotive, steel and power sectors. In the AI sector, falling model prices are occurring alongside rising expenditure on computing power; this week’s earnings reports from tech giants will reveal whether AI investment can be translated into revenue.

  • Capital expenditure and cloud business growth in the financial reports of Microsoft, Alphabet (28 October) and Amazon (29 October)
  • Final election results in Israel and the outlook for government formation
  • The details of Iran’s ‘seven conditions’ and the US response, as well as whether Brent crude can hold above $110
  • Third-party reviews of GPT-6.1 Terra and competitors’ pricing responses
  • China’s October PMI, Brazil’s fiscal framework and the trend in the real

Source:TheStreet、TipRanks、Reuters (reprinted by Euronext)
