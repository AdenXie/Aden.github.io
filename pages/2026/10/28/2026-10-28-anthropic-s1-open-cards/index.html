At the time, all speculation was based on unofficial sources—on 26 October 2026, the official announcement was made: Anthropic publicly filed its S-1 registration statement with the SEC, and the stock code was finalised ANT, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a consortium of around 20 investment banks; the roadshow is expected to begin in the week of 9 November. This regulatory filing, comprising 335 pages of main text and 65 pages of financial notes, has finally brought the ‘moment of truth’ into sharp focus—Nine sets of previous inferences: five were confirmed, two were clarified, and two were significantly revised; one of these directly overturned the conclusion reached in our previous article.

Introduction: From rumours to the official seal – the formal ‘money-checking moment’

For investors interested in the commercialisation of generative AI, 26 October 2026 is a date to note.

Following the formal filing of Anthropic, PBC’s (Registration No. 333-299633) S-1 form with the SEC’s EDGAR system, this mega-IPO—co-led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, and backed by around 20 investment banks including JPMorgan, Citigroup, Barclays and Wells Fargo—has, for the first time, allowed everyone to cross-check all the rumours circulating over the past two months, one by one.

On the eve of investment banks launching their full-scale US roadshow in November and making a final push to list on the Nasdaq before Thanksgiving, we have laid out the key conclusions drawn from our two previous in-depth analyses side by side with this formal document—which is strictly governed by law—and compared them point by point.

The results of the card draw were quite solid:“Loss of 80.7 billion primarily due to the revaluation of convertible bonds” “Revenue surges 14-fold” “Five-tier shareholding structure with super voting rights”All of this has been confirmed by the official financial reports; and, even more unexpectedly, two key figures that had previously caused widespread concern saw a dramatic turnaround following the official announcement, proving to be in far better shape than rumours had suggested.

II. Two Major Rebuttals: The actual operating loss was less than 700 million, and the concerns over customer concentration proved to be a false alarm

In this substantial S-1 prospectus, what deserves the closest scrutiny from all secondary market analysts and technology observers are two points that completely overturn the market’s previous negative narrative:Major amendments：

Clarification 1: The actual operating loss was only US$693 million, not the rumoured US$8.7 billion

During the period when the draft was leaked, the claim that ‘even after deducting the 72 billion fair value revaluation, Anthropic’s actual loss for the first half of the year still amounted to a staggering 8.7 billion’ was widely circulated—including in our own previous article.

A glance at the official S-1 income statement reveals the truth:

In the first half of 2026, Anthropic’s total operating revenue was US$16.286 billion, Total Operating Expenses—which cover computing power leasing, model R&D, staff remuneration and sales management—amount to US$16.979 billion。

Subtracting one figure from the other, the actual operating loss amounted to just US$693 million ($693M) – compared with an operating loss of US$2,135 million ($2,135M) in the same period of the previous year.

The prospectus states in black and white:

“Our operating loss was $693 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026.”(For the six months ended 30 June 2026, our operating loss was US$693 million.)

Figure 1Breakdown of Anthropic’s official profit and loss figures for the first half of 2026: revenue of US$16.286 billion, with an actual operating loss of just US$693 million; nearly 90 per cent of the massive reported loss (US$72.388 billion) was attributable to the revaluation of convertible bonds at fair value. Source: SEC EDGAR S-1 formal prospectus.

So where exactly did the previously circulating figure of ‘US$8.7 billion’ come from?

This is a classic example ofConfusion over terminology：

Total net loss $80,748M Less: revaluation of the fair value of convertible bonds $72,388M, the difference being approximately US$8.36 billion. This shortfall comprises three components: an actual operating loss of 693 million, net non-operating expenditure of approximately 7.65 billion (interest income of 1.11 billion, other non-operating net expenditure of −8.76 billion, the latter primarily comprising changes in the fair value of strategic investments and financial instruments), and income tax of approximately 20 million. To describe the entire difference resulting from ‘stripping the fair value revaluation from the net loss’ directly as an ‘operating loss’ is tantamount to cramming non-operating items into the operating line—this was precisely the terminology error committed by financial reports at the time (including when we ourselves paraphrased them); it was only with the formal filing of the S-1 that this line was truly drawn.

Given a total revenue of over US$16.2 billion, an operating loss of just under US$700 million means that:Anthropic’s operating loss margin for its core business has narrowed to approximately -4.2 per cent (compared with approximately -184 per cent in the same period last year), bringing it within touching distance of breaking even.

Case Study 2: Fears over customer concentration disproved by the facts

During the period when the draft was leaked, the market had expressed concerns that “the top two clients accounted for 20 per cent of revenue in the first half of the year”, whilst the decision by the AI programming tool Replit to switch its default model to OpenAI also caused a brief stir.

The official S-1 filing provides a compelling rebuttal:

In the first three quarters of 2026,No single customer accounts for 10 per cent or more of total revenue(In the first half of the year, Customer C alone accounted for 11 per cent), with the top five customers collectively contributing approximately 25 per cent; the S-1 filing also disclosed that in the third quarter of 2026, revenue from the ‘long tail’—i.e. customers other than the top five—accounted for approximately 79 per cent, a further increase from the approximately 64 per cent recorded in the first quarter.

In its place is a healthy, horizontal customer funnel:

  • Revenue over the past 12 months (TTM) exceeded US$100,000 The number of corporate clients has reached Over 11,900(Just over 950 a year ago, representing a growth of more than 12-fold);
  • TTM revenue exceeded US$1 million has more than 1,900；
  • Revenue in excess of US$10 million Super Lighthouse’s client base of approximately 250 companies；
  • More than half of the Fortune 500 companies have already adopted Claude’s enterprise services, with a penetration rate of over 30 per cent amongst the Forbes Global 2000.

The so-called structural weakness of ‘over-reliance on one or two major clients’ has been thoroughly debunked in the light of official data.

III. A stronger-than-expected financial foundation: a return to profitability and ample cash reserves

In addition to these two major reversals, a series of in-depth financial indicators disclosed for the first time in the formal prospectus demonstrate the financial resilience of a mature software giant:

  1. Adjusted operating profit has returned to positive territory：
    After excluding non-cash items such as share-based payment expenses ($2,185M) and liabilities relating to the charitable matching scheme ($126M), Anthropic’s adjusted operating profit for the first half of 2026 amounted to +US$1.618 billion, adjusted operating profit margin 10%(compared with −US$1.849 billion in the same period last year, marking a complete turnaround); adjusted net profit also recorded +US$2.671 billion。
  2. Operating cash flow (CFO) generates genuine self-sustaining revenue：
    In the first half of 2026, the company’s net cash flow from operating activities amounted to +US$3.93 billion (+$3,929M), compared with a net outflow of US$1.51 billion in the same period last year. This means that even without external equity financing, the company’s day-to-day operations alone are already generating a steady stream of real cash.
  3. A massive cash moat of US$131.65 billion：
    As at 30 June 2026, Anthropic’s total book value of cash, cash equivalents and highly liquid short-term investments amounted to US$131.65 billion ($131.65B)…exceeding the reserves of the vast majority of S&P 500 constituents, providing a substantial liquidity buffer for the future race for high-intensity computing power.
  4. Statutory definition of historical funding amounts：
    Section 15 of the prospectus (Item 15) disclosed for the first time the official figures for the two most recent rounds of substantial funding:Series G-1 preference shares raised US$23.5 billion, Series H-1 raised US$45.7 billion, and Series H-2 raised US$19.5 billion。
  5. An impressive Net Retention Rate (NRR)：
    NRR, the key metric used in the enterprise software sector to measure customer retention, recorded Approximately 900 per cent. The average net revenue contribution from our existing customer base is approximately nine times what it was a year ago—not due to churn, but rather to an almost explosive rate of organic growth.
  6. The ‘inflated revenue figures’ disclosed in all honesty：
    The prospectus makes the rare admission that in the first three quarters of 2026, there were approximately 5 per cent to 7 per cent of revenue comes from customers who breach our usage policy or commercial terms(The S-1 form does not specify the exact type; the risk section mentions ongoing external attempts to replicate, distil and extract the model). With the company set to comprehensively strengthen monitoring and enforcement between August and September 2026, this portion of revenue is expected to decline in subsequent quarters—the fact that the company has taken the initiative to proactively eliminate inflated figures in its prospectus demonstrates a strong commitment to compliance.

IV. A Detailed Breakdown of the 100-billion-yuan computing power chain: Media estimates vs the audit baseline

The “US$518 billion computing power procurement commitment”, which we discussed previously in our series of articles, has been subject to the most comprehensive statutory breakdown to date in the notes to the financial statements of the formal S-1 filing.

It should be clarified that,The main text of Form S-1 does not directly state a single figure of ‘US$518 billion’ in bold., This is a consolidated total compiled by financial media outlets of the maximum contract values for the major cloud service providers:

Source: SEC EDGAR S-1 formal prospectus.

Supplier contract-by-contract breakdown:

  • Broadcom: Five-year lease agreement for specialised hardware equipment, totalling approximately US$125.2 billion ($125.2B), interest-free and irrevocable; rent is payable quarterly, with a maximum of US$42 billionthe supply chain financing facility secured by the Infrastructure Partner 2026 NPA (asset-backed notes);
  • SpaceXAI (formerly X.AI, part of the Musk ecosystem): Up to US$120.5 billion (≤$120.5B) The cluster computing power procurement agreement (NVIDIA GPUs) entails monthly expenditure of approximately US$3.1 billion when operating at full capacity; the initial irrevocable term runs until 1 March 2027, after which either party may terminate the agreement by giving 90 days’ notice—an increase on the estimate given during the draft stage;
  • Google: Irrevocable TPU computing power guarantee agreement US$114.5 billion (≥$114.5B), covering the period from April 2026 to July 2033; any shortfall must be made up via a true-up; in addition, the forecast for 2028–2031 is US$125 billion to US$150 billion the procurement of additional computing power (not included in the fixed commitment table);
  • Amazon: The irrevocable minimum threshold for computing power procurement US$110.0 billion (≥$110.0B), with a term running from May 2026 to April 2036—a period of ten years—and also incorporating a true-up mechanism;
  • Microsoft: Irrevocable procurement threshold US$44.9 billion (≥$44.9B), valid from November 2026 to May 2033; payment is required regardless of actual usage—Claude is currently available via the three major cloud platforms: AWS, GCP and Azure;
  • AMD: No fixed amount is committed; if the entire initial batch of computing power is purchased, the expenditure will exceed US$20 billion (>$20B); in return, AMD has undertaken to purchase up to US$5 billion Class N non-voting shares (unlocked in tranches upon the achievement of GPU delivery milestones).

The absolute minimum requirements for statutory audits：

After excluding all optional cancellation clauses, flexible arrangements and potential options, the financial notes to the prospectus (F-59) provide the audited figures for firm contracts—The total value of fixed/guaranteed procurement commitments spanning 1 to 20 years amounts to US$265.6 billion ($265.6B)。

Whether it is the framework cap of 518 billion or the statutory obligation of 265.6 billion, Anthropic has, through these massive contracts spanning a decade, secured the core production capacity of global semiconductor and cloud computing giants for its own purposes.

V. Details of Top-Level Governance: Super Voting Rights, Independent Trust and the Conversion of the 5 per cent Public Welfare Fund into Equity Shares

In the area of governance and control—which attracts the most attention in the capital markets—the formal S-1 filing reveals the ingenious safeguards this not-for-profit organisation has put in place to strike a balance between commercial interests and ethical autonomy:

1. The design of a five-tier shareholding structure and absolute control

  • Class A Ordinary Shares: One voting right per share, for trading on the open secondary market;
  • Class N shares: The economic rights are the same as those of Class A shares, but they carry no voting rights; upon transfer to an unrelated third party, they are automatically converted into Class A shares. Amazon’s convertible bonds are subject to a 12 per cent conversion cap (any amount exceeding this is converted into rolling notes with no additional issuance rights), and external investors such as Google also hold primarily this type of share to prevent the cloud giant from holding an excessive stake;
  • Class E Employee Incentive Shares: Details revealed for the first time in the official version—employees are automatically allocated one Class E share for every six months they have worked at the company, with each share carrying one voting right;
  • Class T shares: Held exclusively by the Long-Term Benefits Trust (LTBT), which has no economic interest, and is responsible for appointing certain directors (Class T directors) and participating in specific governance arrangements;
  • Class F Founder’s Exclusive Shares: Through a dedicated Founder LLC Held separatelya stream. This group, which has no economic stake but holds sway over the entire company 50.1 per cent of the total voting rights—The seven co-founders (Dario, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah) thereby retained decisive control over the company’s strategic direction.

The individual economic stakes held by the seven founders were not disclosed as a single figure in the beneficial ownership table of the formal S-1 filing (external estimates during the draft stage put this at approximately 14 per cent) ; what is certain is the governance arrangement itself: the company is eligible for an exemption from certain governance requirements under the Nasdaq ‘Controlled Company’ rules, but the S-1 states that it intends to apply this exemption only to the Nomination Committee, with the majority of the Board of Directors still comprising independent directors. Another new detail is:The seven founders have each pledged to donate 80 per cent of their personal shareholdings in Anthropic to charitable causes.(A voluntary, revocable long-term commitment).

2. A genuine B Corp: a charitable trust receives a 5 per cent equity stake

Unlike many companies, which merely make vague charitable pledges in their prospectuses, Anthropic has gone ahead and completed a transfer of shares involving real money:

The company has provided the independent Anthropic Public Benefit Fund Injection 847 million shares (846,840,960 shares) of Class N shares, representing 5 per cent of the fully diluted economic interest(Based on the assumption that the convertible bonds are converted in full).

Furthermore, the Articles of Association stipulate that whenever the share price rises by 50 per cent compared with the previous price, the Board of Directors may, at its discretion, issue up to a further 2 per cent of shares to the fund to support long-term AI governance, alignment research and public interest initiatives.

3. A risk warning spanning approximately 94 pages and the challenges faced by the insurance industry in underwriting

Throughout the prospectus,The ‘Risk Factors’ section begins on page 26 and continues for approximately 94 pages (up to around page 119)—This is probably the most substantial prospectus of any AI company to date.

The document contained strong language rarely seen in the prospectuses of publicly listed companies:

“…sufficiently advanced AI systems could, if not developed, deployed, and operated with appropriate safeguards, oversight, and security measures, pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity…”
(AI systems that are sufficiently advanced may pose catastrophic or even existential risks to humanity if they lack appropriate safeguards, oversight and security measures during their development, deployment and operation.)

“With the scale of harm that could result from a catastrophic AI-related incident, no single insurer, and potentially the insurance industry in the aggregate, may have sufficient capacity or expertise to underwrite insurance commensurate with such exposure.”
(Given the scale of damage that a catastrophic AI incident could cause, no single insurer—or even the entire insurance industry taken as a whole—is likely to possess the underwriting capacity or specialist expertise required to deal with it.)

Including the statement that ‘commercial insurance may not cover AI disasters’ in formal securities disclosures not only reflects the founding team’s roots in Effective Altruism (EA), but also fulfils, from a legal perspective, the ultimate duty to inform the public of potential risks.

VII. Honest Explanation of Data Definitions (Key Factual Boundaries)

When reading this card-laying report, readers must maintain an objective and rigorous understanding of its scope:

  1. The price range and offering size were deliberately left blank in the initial S-1 filing: In accordance with SEC practice, the current filing constitutes a preliminary registration statement (Subject to Completion); the specific price range per share, the actual number of shares to be issued and the amount to be raised by existing shareholders will be formally finalised in a subsequent amendment to Form S-1/A; The valuation of between 1.5 trillion and 2 trillion US dollars currently being discussed in the media represents a non-binding indicative offer and has not been included in any formal documentation;
  2. This article does not cite any third-quarter figures whatsoever.: The statutory audited financial statements in Form S-1 are presented on a full-year and half-year basis; revenue for the third quarter alone does not appear separately in the statements (figures covering the nine-month period are only mentioned sporadically in discussions regarding customer concentration and the proportion of non-compliant revenue); the figure of $16.8 billion for a single quarter, which circulated during the draft stage, has not been verified by any official document;
  3. The roadshow schedule is taken from various financial news reports: The timeframe of launching the roadshow in the week of 9 November and listing before Thanksgiving (26 November) is the consensus timeline reported by media outlets such as Bloomberg and Reuters; the S-1 filing itself, in accordance with standard practice, merely states ‘as soon as practicable’;
  4. All financial figures in this article are taken from the audited S-1 financial statements and the original text., available at Original text from SEC EDGARCheck each item individually.

Conclusion: The True Test of the Commercialisation of Coming-of-Age Ceremonies

Having worked my way through this S-1 prospectus—comprising 335 pages of main text and financial notes up to F-65—what struck me most was the pace of evolution demonstrated by this company, founded by idealistic tech geeks.

Just over five years ago, when they left OpenAI to found Anthropic (in January 2021), there were those who questioned whether they were merely a group of armchair academics, sitting in an ivory tower and spouting empty rhetoric about ‘model alignment’ and ‘AI safety’, and who believed they stood no chance commercially against the ChatGPT empire backed by Microsoft.

Yet what is now before global investors is a textbook example of a financial statement:

Revenue surged 14-fold, surpassing the US$16 billion mark, whilst the actual operating loss narrowed to less than US$700 million, net operating cash flow stood at nearly US$4 billion, with over US$130 billion in cash reserves sitting on the balance sheet, whilst the company has also institutionalised the injection of 5 per cent of its actual shares into a charitable foundation.

When the ticker symbol ‘ANT’ lit up on the Nasdaq screen in November, it was not only a crowning moment for Dario Amodei and his team, but also a coming-of-age ceremony for the entire generative AI industry.

They laid their cards on the table for the world to see, using an official ledger:Large-scale models are not only capable of devouring capital, but have also, for the first time, demonstrated their ability to generate their own revenue under audit criteria—as for how high a price the market is willing to place on this ledger, the answer will soon be displayed on the Nasdaq screen.
