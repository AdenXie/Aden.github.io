Daily Briefing | 2026-10-16: High Interest Rates and War Reshape Growth Paths

AdenXie
  2026-10-16 09:00 2026-10-16 09:00 Created  
  • Daily Briefing 
 
  • News 
  • | Market Watch 
 2k Words  6 Mins  

Today's main theme

The common thread over the past 24 hours is that growth remains resilient, but the costs of supporting growth are rising significantly. Both strong U.S. producer inflation and retail data have caused speculation of interest rate cuts to recede. However, Federal Reserve officials have lowered the probability of a near-term rate hike, and the bond market has shifted to trading "high interest rates for longer." The huge debt demand for AI investment has also begun to spill over from technology valuation issues to credit and government bond pricing issues. At the same time, Google is expanding its AI licensing partnerships with news organizations, indicating that content copyright is shifting from litigation to paid revenue sharing. China, on the other hand, supported fourth-quarter growth with targeted interest rate cuts, mortgage subsidies, and local government bond quotas. The resumption of attacks on energy facilities between Russia and Ukraine, and the ongoing risks in the Red Sea and Hormuz, have further pushed energy security, shipping costs, and supply chain redundancy to the center of global policy and corporate capital allocation.

Five key news items

1. US inflation and consumption are relatively strong, but expectations for a Federal Reserve rate hike have cooled.

U.S. producer prices recorded their biggest monthly increase in about four and a half years in September, with upward pressure not only coming from energy but also spreading to goods and services. Retail sales were stronger than expected during the same period, and the number of first-time unemployment claims rose only slightly, indicating that demand and employment have not yet slowed down significantly. PressReuters reported on October 15Several Federal Reserve officials subsequently downplayed the necessity of an October rate hike, and interest rate market bets on holding rates steady at the October 27-28 meeting rose to 73%, up from 53% a month earlier. U.S. Treasury bonds instead received safe-haven buying, and the dollar index fell 0.45% from an 18-month high to 101.99. The key impact is that the market no longer simply trades "high inflation leads to interest rate hikes," but simultaneously takes into account the expansion of AI data center debt, tightening financial conditions, and tail-end risks to growth. Going forward, we should observe core inflation, wage growth, long-term yields, and the Federal Reserve's statements on asset valuations and credit expansion.

2. Google expands AI news licensing, with over 400 media outlets joining the partnership.

Google announced the expansion of its AI collaboration project for news publishers, with CNN, the Financial Times, Axios, and others joining, bringing the total number of participating publications to over 400. according toReuters reported on October 15The partners will provide Google with broader content usage rights and receive payment; the specific amount was not disclosed. Google promises that its AI products will display source attribution and provide direct links. This move comes at a time when publishers are worried that AI summaries will weaken search traffic while protecting copyright revenue through litigation and negotiation. It signifies a shift in the relationship between generative AI and the content industry from "crawling first, then controversy" to large-scale licensing. For the technology industry, the cost of high-quality real-time corpora will enter the long-term profit model of model and search products; For the media, it remains unknown whether the new licensing fees can cover traffic and subscription losses. Subsequent variables include contract revenue sharing, clickback quality, content withdrawal rights, and whether regulators consider the bargaining advantage of large platforms to be a competitive issue.

3. China's growth is expected to rebound slightly in the third quarter, with fiscal relay becoming a key focus.

A Reuters poll of 50 economists shows that China's GDP is expected to grow by 4.4% year-on-year in the third quarter, slightly higher than the 4.3% in the second quarter, but still below the official target range of about 4.5% to 5% for the whole year. Official data will be released on October 19. To stabilize the real estate market and domestic demand, the September policies included targeted interest rate cuts, mortgage subsidies, and the allocation of 550 billion yuan in unused debt quotas to support local government financing and infrastructure. The survey forecasts a median growth rate of 4.5% for the full year of 2026, 4.4% for 2027, and is expected to rebound to 4.5% in the fourth quarter.Reuters investigation on October 15It also shows that, due to inflationary constraints, most institutions expect the loan prime rate and the reserve requirement ratio to remain unchanged until at least the first quarter of 2027, with fiscal policy assuming greater responsibility for providing a safety net. The market will next need to verify not only GDP readings, but also real estate sales, private investment, the speed at which local government bond funds are disbursed, and whether household consumption can spontaneously recover.

4. Ukraine resumes strikes on Russian oil refineries; energy ceasefire rapidly collapses.

Ukraine attacked Rosneft's oil refinery in Ryazan, a facility that accounts for approximately 5% of Russia's refining capacity. Zelensky stated that as Russia continues to attack Ukraine's energy system, Kyiv is no longer bound by the ceasefire agreed upon with Trump regarding energy facilities. Ukraine had previously suspended strikes on oil refineries following a sudden announcement on Sunday, but the ceasefire broke down within days.Reuters reported on October 15Zelensky reportedly suggested that Ukraine might only halt such operations if the United States provided more Patriot air defense interceptor missiles. Differences between the two sides are also widening over air defense support, satellite communication restrictions, and missile early warning. Attacks on refining and chemical facilities will affect Russia's refined oil supply, export revenue, and maintenance costs, and will be passed on to the European market through diesel, shipping, and war risk premiums. The next steps depend on the scale of Russia's retaliation, the timing of the shutdown of the Ryazan plant, the US military aid negotiations, and the enforcement of energy sanctions.

5. WTO states that war permanently changes trade paths, and rising costs are difficult to reverse.

World Trade Organization Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala said that the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East are permanently changing the global trade landscape. Businesses are building alternative shipping routes, suppliers and inventories to avoid bottlenecks such as the Strait of Hormuz, and some high-cost arrangements will not be withdrawn even after the conflict ends. The WTO predicts that Middle East exports will decline by 17% and imports by 15% in 2026; However, supported by strong trade in AI-related products, the forecast for global merchandise trade growth was raised from 1.9% in March to 3.9%.Reuters reported on October 15The results show that this combination is not contradictory: the increase in total demand for technology products masks the sharp divergence in energy, shipping, and regional trade. For businesses, eliminating single points of failure in the supply chain means more capital occupation and lower turnover efficiency; For inflation, detours, insurance, and redundant inventory will form a structural floor price. Going forward, attention should be paid to ocean freight costs, war risks, the restoration of energy corridors, the export cycle of AI hardware, and trade financing conditions.

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Today's pricing theme is "not weak growth, but more expensive impact". US data lowers the probability of rapid easing, but the Federal Reserve remains reluctant to raise interest rates immediately, potentially leading to a continued high-level fluctuation in the yield curve: in the short term, it is anchored by policy interest rates, while in the long term, it simultaneously faces inflation, fiscal supply, and AI infrastructure financing needs. A decline in the US dollar from its highs does not equate to a trend reversal. If risk events boost safe-haven demand or US data continues to lead, non-US currencies will still be under pressure. In terms of risk appetite, AI licensing collaborations improve the legal predictability of platforms, but also confirm the increase in content costs; Coupled with the expansion of data center debt, technology stock valuations will rely more on real cash flow than simply on computing power narratives. Commodity markets need to distinguish between supply and demand: industrial products such as copper depend on the speed at which Chinese fiscal funds generate physical labor, while crude oil and refined oil products are driven by attacks on Russian and Ukrainian refineries, Middle East routes, and shipping insurance premiums. If China's policies rely more on fiscal policy rather than comprehensive reserve requirement ratio and interest rate cuts, the pressure on the RMB interest rate differential can be buffered to some extent, but real estate sales and residents' confidence will still determine the policy multiplier. Today, the focus will be on US long-term Treasury yields, whether the US dollar can withstand its decline, oil cracking spreads, shipping rates, and the revision of policy expectations ahead of China's October 19 data.
