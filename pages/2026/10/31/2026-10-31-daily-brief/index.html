Daily Briefing | 31 October 2026: Intel posts its biggest fall since 1974; the Nasdaq closes October with a sharp drop; the Bank of Japan says inflation has reached target; Binance suffers the largest-ever theft of US$6.3 billion

AdenXie
  2026-10-31 09:00 2026-10-31 09:00 Created  
  • Daily Briefing 
 
  • News 
  • | Market Watch

Today's main theme

This edition covers Friday 30 October, which was also the final trading day of October. The main theme of the day was ‘a concentrated unwinding of risk’. US tech stocks continued to be weighed down by cash flow issues related to AI investments. Intel closed down by approximately 29 per cent following weak fourth-quarter guidance, marking its largest single-day fall since 1974, whilst the Nasdaq fell by 2.84 per cent on the day, bringing its cumulative decline for October to over 5 per cent. The cryptocurrency market suffered another heavy blow, with Binance reporting that approximately US$6.3 billion in assets had been stolen – the largest cryptocurrency theft on record – which a blockchain analytics firm attributed to North Korean hackers. The Bank of Japan kept interest rates unchanged at 1.25 per cent, but for the first time assessed that core inflation had largely reached the 2 per cent target; one committee member advocated a rate rise, leading the market to anticipate the next rate hike in December. Another oil tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, whilst a Qatari mediator returned to Tehran with a revised proposal; Brent crude rose by approximately 2.5 per cent. Domestically, the central bank announced that, with effect from 2 November, overnight reverse repurchase agreements would serve as the primary open market operation tool; the ChiNext Index and the STAR Market Composite Index both fell by more than 4 per cent on the day.

Key News

1. Intel closed down by around 29 per cent; the Nasdaq fell by 2.84 per cent in a single day, and has fallen by more than 5 per cent in October

On 30 October, US shares fell across the board. According to TheStreet, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by 1.06 per cent to 51,785.82 points, the S&P 500 fell 1.83 per cent to 7,578.97 points, the Nasdaq fell 2.84 per cent to 25,442.31 points, and the Russell 2000 fell 2.03 per cent; the S&P 500 technology sector fell by nearly 5 per cent, with nearly 70 per cent of stocks declining, whilst the VIX volatility index rose by approximately 32 per cent at one point.

Intel led the decline. The company had issued fourth-quarter revenue guidance of US$15.0 billion to US$16.0 billion after the market closed the previous day, down from US$16.5 billion in the third quarter; According to StockTitan, the guidance for adjusted gross margin was 38.0 per cent, down from 44.1 per cent in the third quarter, whilst the guidance for adjusted earnings per share was US$0.25, below analysts’ expectations of US$0.43. The CFO stated that the slowdown in the PC market would continue at least until the first half of next year. According to TheStreet, Intel closed down by approximately 29 per cent that day, marking its worst single-day performance since 1974. The social media platform Reddit also plummeted by around 29 per cent; According to reports compiled by The Neuron, Reddit’s traffic from Google searches fell by an average of more than one million visits per week in October. CEO Huffman attributed this to Google’s AI model and plans to shift the site towards a login-based model to reduce data scraping. Amazon fell slightly by around 2 per cent following its earnings report.

On a monthly basis, according to Yahoo Finance, the Nasdaq fell by approximately 5.2 per cent in October, marking its worst month since March 2025; the S&P 500 fell by around 1.2 per cent, whilst the Dow Jones Industrial Average actually rose by approximately 1.5 per cent. This week, Alphabet and Amazon reported negative free cash flow, Intel issued weak guidance, and Reddit’s traffic was hit by the impact of AI-powered search, with market concerns regarding AI expanding from ‘over-investment’ to ‘who will be replaced by AI’. Key events to watch: Apple’s earnings report on 2 November, the October non-farm payrolls figures due in early November, and whether major tech firms will cut their capital expenditure forecasts for 2027.

Source:TheStreet、StockTitan、Yahoo Finance、The Neuron

2. The Bank of Japan has kept interest rates at 1.25 per cent and, for the first time, assessed that underlying inflation has broadly reached the 2 per cent target.

The Bank of Japan concluded its monetary policy meeting on 30 October, keeping the policy rate at 1.25 per cent. This was the first meeting since the rate was raised from 1.00 per cent in September, and the rate remains at its highest level since 1995. According to the *Seoul Economic Daily*, the vote was 8 to 1, with committee member Hajime Takada advocating a rate hike to 1.5 per cent, a proposal rejected by the majority. In its outlook report, the Bank of Japan assessed for the first time that core inflation was ‘broadly in line’ with the 2 per cent target; this is the first time this has been stated since the 2 per cent target was introduced in 2013; At the same time, it raised its inflation forecast for the next financial year from 2.4 per cent to 2.6 per cent, whilst the core inflation forecast—excluding fresh food and energy—was revised upwards from 2.6 per cent to 2.7 per cent. High oil prices and the depreciation of the yen have been the main drivers of inflation this year.

The central bank has also decided to accelerate the sale of its ETF holdings, selling approximately 860 billion yen annually at book value. According to Reuters’ calculations, at this rate it would take around 43 years to liquidate the entire portfolio, compared with 112 years under the previous plan.

At a press conference, Governor Kazuo Ueda stated that core inflation had reached a level consistent with the target, but that vigilance was required regarding the risk of inflation exceeding the target; going forward, the focus would be on monitoring whether medium- to long-term inflation expectations stabilised around 2 per cent. According to Reuters’ analysis, the next interest rate hike could come as early as December. The market has widely characterised this decision as a ‘hawkish hold’. According to the Seoul Economic Daily, the US dollar fell against the yen from the previous day’s level of 157.79 to around 157.12. Key points to watch: whether interest rates will be raised to 1.5 per cent at the December meeting, whether the yen will continue to strengthen, and the reaction of Japanese government bond yields.

Source:Seoul Economic Daily、Reuters (reprinted by Investing.com)、Bank of Japan Outlook Report

3. Binance says approximately US$6.3 billion has been stolen, making it the largest cryptocurrency theft in history

Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, confirmed on 30 October that an attack on Thursday evening had resulted in the theft of approximately US$6.3 billion in assets. According to Al Jazeera, the attack was carried out in around 20 minutes, with the attackers instructing Binance’s wallets to transfer assets to addresses under their control – a method similar to the February 2025 incident in which Bybit lost US$1.5 billion; Blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs stated that approximately US$6.36 billion was transferred out of Binance’s wallets across 10 blockchains. Binance said the figure “may still rise”.

Both TRM Labs and Chainalysis believe that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group linked to the Lazarus Group; Binance co-CEO Teng Bin stated that the investigation has not yet identified the party responsible. Binance stated that it has frozen approximately US$3 billion, of which over US$2.3 billion has been frozen by the two stablecoin issuers, Tether and Circle; the amount stolen is more than six times the size of its US$1 billion User Security Asset Fund (SAFU), but Binance has pledged to cover the full loss and expects to resume withdrawals within approximately 45 hours.

In the markets, according to Al Jazeera, Bitcoin fell as low as US$76,368—a six-week low—whilst Ethereum dropped by more than 3 per cent to around US$2,178; Coinbase stated it had not been affected, but its share price fell by 4.8 per cent. The crypto market had previously been under pressure from rising US interest rates and weakening demand for ETFs. Key points to watch: whether Binance will be able to resume withdrawals on schedule, the fate of the remaining approximately US$3.3 billion, and the regulators’ response to custody security at major exchanges.

Source:Al Jazeera、TheStreet

4. Another oil tanker attacked in the Strait of Hormuz; Qatar returns to Tehran with a revised proposal

According to Euronews, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that on the evening of 29 October, a small boat placed two explosive devices above the waterline of an oil tanker; the explosions caused a fire in the engine room, and the vessel temporarily lost power; The incident took place approximately 25 nautical miles north-east of Hasab, Oman. The fire has been extinguished, the crew are safe, and the vessel has resumed its voyage. The Financial Times reported that the tanker was named Louise 1, and this may be the first reported successful ‘sticky mine’ attack in the Strait since the outbreak of hostilities in February. On 30 October, the Iranian Revolutionary Guard claimed that two oil tankers had encountered incidents whilst on “unauthorised” routes; Oman stated that its navy had evacuated 25 crew members from a burning Panamanian-flagged vessel. US Central Command stated that Iranian mines had been cleared from the main shipping lanes.

On the diplomatic front, the Qatari mediator returned to Tehran on 30 October with a revised proposal. According to Euronews, citing CBS, there have been some signs of progress in the negotiations, and Iran is expected to respond by the end of this week; Axios reported that the mediators are seeking common ground on the inspection of nuclear facilities and the lifting of sanctions, but differences remain regarding the conditions for formally commencing negotiations. Axios and the Associated Press simultaneously reported that the US and Israel are considering launching a large-scale strike after the mid-term elections on 3 November, though Trump has not yet made a final decision. Speaking in a radio interview that day, Trump said a war with Iran would end “quickly”, but did not specify whether this would be through a deal or military action.

According to TheStreet, following reports of attacks on oil tankers, Brent crude rose by around 2.5 per cent to around $117.8, whilst WTI rose by around 2.2 per cent to around $104.2. Key points to watch: Iran’s response to the revised proposal, US military decisions following the mid-term elections, and the reaction of the shipping and insurance markets to this new form of attack.

Source:Europe News Channel、TheStreet

5. From 2 November, the central bank switched to using overnight reverse repurchase agreements as its primary operational tool, whilst the A-share market closed October with a sharp fall

The People’s Bank of China issued an announcement on 30 October stating that, with effect from 2 November, it will conduct overnight reverse repo operations on every working day, using a fixed-rate, quantity-based tender; the current operation rate is 1.25 per cent; The 7-day reverse repo will be converted to a fixed-volume, interest-rate tender with multiple winning prices, and will primarily be conducted during tax periods and at month-end. Tools such as ad hoc reverse and reverse repurchase agreements, and repurchase agreements with overseas central bank-type institutions, which were previously benchmarked against the 7-day reverse repurchase rate with basis points added or subtracted, will henceforth be benchmarked against the overnight reverse repurchase rate, with the range of basis points remaining unchanged.

On 19 October, Governor Pan Gongsheng stated that the interbank overnight repurchase rate (DR001) had effectively become the central bank’s target rate for monetary policy, but that the maturity of the main open market instruments and policy rates remained at seven days, creating a discrepancy between the two. He therefore indicated that consideration should be given to switching the maturity of the main operations to overnight. According to Sina Finance, overnight transactions now account for more than 90 per cent of the average daily trading volume in the money market. Since October, the DR001 has generally fluctuated between 1.25 per cent and 1.30 per cent, with the 1.25 per cent rate broadly aligning with the central tendency of market interest rates. Several experts have emphasised that this constitutes a reform of the interest rate control framework; it is not an interest rate cut, nor does it signify a change in the stance of monetary policy. It will be worth monitoring whether the Loan Prime Rate (LPR) and bond pricing will gradually shift to be anchored by the overnight rate.

In the markets, according to the 21st Century Business Herald, on 30 October, the Shanghai Composite Index fell by 1.44 per cent to 3,599.02 points, the Shenzhen Component Index fell by 2.73 per cent, the ChiNext Index fell by 4.13 per cent, and the STAR Market Composite Index fell by 4.66 per cent; Trading volume across both markets totalled 1.67 trillion yuan, an increase of 117.9 billion yuan compared with the previous day. The baijiu and banking sectors bucked the trend to rise, whilst the film and television, gaming and semiconductor sectors led the decline. According to data compiled by Sina Finance, the Shanghai Composite Index fell by 6.33 per cent in October, whilst the ChiNext Index dropped by approximately 23 per cent and the STAR 50 Index by approximately 24 per cent; the SSE 50 Index, however, fell by only 2.88 per cent, indicating a clear shift in capital from high-valuation technology stocks towards large-cap blue-chips. Key points to watch: the October PMI figures to be released on 31 October; the trend in overnight interest rates during the first week under the new framework; and whether technology stocks can stabilise amidst the overseas AI valuation correction.

Source:The Paper、Sina Finance、21st Century Business Herald、Sina Finance (Hot Topics Hourly Report)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

The sell-off on 30 October was seen simultaneously in the US stock market, the A-share market and the cryptocurrency market. According to TheStreet, the yield on 10-year US Treasury bonds fell by approximately 7 basis points to 4.917 per cent, as safe-haven capital flowed into the bond market; Bitcoin fell by around 3 per cent to around US$77,000. In Europe, according to Eunews citing preliminary figures from Eurostat, eurozone GDP grew by 0.4 per cent quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter, outperforming expectations, though this was primarily driven by Ireland’s 5.5 per cent growth; excluding Ireland, growth stood at only around 0.2 per cent; France contracted by 0.1 per cent quarter-on-quarter, whilst Germany grew by 0.2 per cent and Italy remained flat. The US Bureau of Labour Statistics reported that the Employment Cost Index rose by 0.8 per cent quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter.

Looking at several news items together: this week, the books on AI investment have been scrutinised repeatedly; the sell-off on the last day of October saw both the Nasdaq and Chinese tech stocks post significant monthly declines, whilst energy stocks and defensive sectors proved relatively resilient. High oil prices continue to drive up inflation; the Bank of Japan has deemed its inflation target to have been met and is prepared to continue raising interest rates, whilst expectations are also high for a Federal Reserve rate hike in December. The global interest rate environment remains unfavourable for highly valued assets. The People’s Bank of China has adjusted its operational framework rather than interest rate levels on this occasion; whilst the impact on liquidity will be limited in the short term, the more significant aim is to align policy rates with market rates.

  • Iran’s response to Qatar’s revised proposal, and the US’s decision regarding Iran following the mid-term elections on 3 November
  • US October non-farm payrolls and expectations for a Fed rate rise in December
  • Whether the Bank of Japan will raise interest rates in December, and whether the US dollar will fall below 155 against the yen
