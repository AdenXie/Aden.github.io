Daily Briefing | 2026-09-23: AI Regulation Intensifies, Sino-European Auto Investment and Hormuz Risks Go Hand in Hand

AdenXie
  2026-09-23 08:57 2026-09-23 08:57 Created

Today's main theme

The common theme over the past 24 hours has been that global growth and technology investment are still moving forward, but policies are beginning to price risks more clearly. On the one hand, AI is shifting from a "model competition" to payment, copyright, and financial data security: European and American tech companies want Australia to relax restrictions on training data, while global banks are demanding clearer responsibilities and consumer protections for proxy shopping. China's foreign economic policy has further directed competition in the automotive industry towards local European investment in order to cope with tariffs and upcoming increased localization requirements. Geopolitically, Iran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, while the United States has maintained high pressure and confirmed that communication is continuing. For the market, interest rates, energy logistics, AI capital expenditures, and trade rules are simultaneously determining the risk premium.

Five key news items

1. Global banks warn AI shopping agents are amplifying fraud and data risks, and payment rules are facing rewriting.

On September 22, several global banks issued warnings about the rapidly expanding "proxy business". NatWest, Bank of America, ING, Capital One, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, and ASB New Zealand, among others, point out that existing regulatory, anti-fraud, and liability mechanisms may not keep up with the pace of technology when AI agents search, select, and even complete payments for consumers. Risks include not only model recommendation bias, but also mishandling of sensitive financial data, agents choosing less secure payment methods, and fraudsters using automated processes to amplify fraud. There has been a significant change on the demand side: John Lewis' search traffic from AI tools in the UK has increased from about 0.3% to 2.5% within a year. Banks advocate for at least establishing clear disclosure, data protection, explainable decision-making, and consumer recourse mechanisms for AI participation in transactions. Its importance lies in the fact that AI is moving from "answering questions" to actually executing fund instructions, and the targets of financial regulation are expanding from people and institutions to software agents. It remains to be seen whether major payment networks, bank regulators, and technology platforms can form a unified framework for identity authentication, authorization, and compensation.Reuters

2. OpenAI and Anthropic are demanding that Australia relax AI training restrictions and link copyright to data center investment.

In submissions to the Australian Parliament's Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence, OpenAI and Anthropic urged the government to reconsider its position on prohibiting the unauthorized use of Australian copyrighted content to train AI models. Instead of demanding a blanket exemption, the two companies advocated for the establishment of limited, conditional training exceptions to strike a balance between creator rights and model development. Australia plans to push for more comprehensive AI regulation next year, but the government has previously ruled out changes to the copyright system and broad exemptions for training data. The debate is also linked to infrastructure investment: OpenAI is working with NextDC on a Sydney data center project, and Anthropic is also involved in related data center collaborations in Queensland. For Australia, this is no longer a simple copyright issue, but a policy trade-off between local content industry revenue, computing power investment, energy demand, and the competitiveness of the AI industry. The committee is expected to submit its report in November, and should focus on whether there are narrow exceptions to text and data mining, licensing payment mechanisms, and whether data center investment has been included in policy negotiations.Reuters

3. China supports automakers investing in Europe, with local production becoming a new path to addressing EU trade barriers.

Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao said on September 22 that China supports Chinese automakers' investment in Europe and hopes to resolve disputes in the automotive industry through the China-EU economic and trade consultation mechanism based on the principles of compliance, balance and non-discrimination. He previously met with Hildegard Mueller, President of the German Association of the Automotive Industry, and had a video conference with Ola Källenius, CEO of Mercedes-Benz and President of the European Automobile Manufacturers Association. The backdrop is that the EU has imposed additional tariffs of up to 35.3% on Chinese-made pure electric vehicles, while simultaneously developing stronger local production requirements; China's plug-in hybrid electric vehicles are currently not covered by the aforementioned additional tariffs on pure electric vehicles, making exports a new policy focus. Beijing's encouragement of local European investment signifies that industrial competition is shifting from simple exports to factories, supply chains, and R&D locations. For Europe, this could bring capital and jobs, but it could also intensify competition among local brands. The subsequent focus will be on the EU's local content rules, hybrid vehicle trade measures, and the speed of Chinese automakers' location selection and supply chain localization in Europe.Reuters

4. Volkswagen expects the Chinese passenger vehicle market to shrink by approximately 20% in 2026, with domestic demand and price competition intensifying.

Ralf Brandstaetter, head of Volkswagen China, said at an industry summit on September 22 that the size of the Chinese passenger vehicle market is expected to decline by about 20% in 2026, describing the current level of contraction as close to the impact of the pandemic. China's domestic auto sales had declined year-on-year for 11 consecutive months as of August, but auto exports remained strong during the same period, forming a clear divergence between "weak domestic demand and strong overseas expansion". For Volkswagen, China remains one of the most important markets. The simultaneous decline in sales and narrowing profit margins also reflects greater pressure on traditional joint venture brands in terms of new energy vehicle models, software capabilities, and price competition. At the macro level, automobiles are an important component of China's durable goods consumption, manufacturing investment, and local industrial chains. Continued contraction will increase the urgency of policies to promote consumption and industry consolidation. On the other hand, export growth will increase trade frictions between China and Europe. It remains to be seen whether consumption support policies such as trade-in programs will be strengthened, whether the price war among automakers will ease, and whether exports can continue to absorb excess domestic capacity.Reuters

5. Iran sets conditions for reopening Hormuz within seven days, simultaneously signaling high-pressure US-Iran negotiations.

On September 22, a senior Iranian official said that Iran could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the United States reduces military pressure and lifts the blockade of Iranian ports. Iran stated that a plan to end hostilities was submitted to the United States through intermediaries on September 16. On the same day, US President Trump publicly called on other countries to impose complete economic isolation on Iran at the UN General Assembly, but later stated that the US and Iran were still communicating that day and believed that an arrangement could eventually be reached. Before the war, the Strait carried about one-fifth of the world's oil and gas transportation, so any verifiable reopening path would directly affect crude oil, shipping, and inflation expectations. France has also begun pushing the UN Security Council to discuss an international mission to resume navigation through the Strait, indicating that energy security is returning to the multilateral diplomatic agenda. The current signals are still contradictory and cannot be considered as an imminent ceasefire; The key questions going forward are whether the United States responds to Iran's conditions, whether the port blockade is adjusted, whether the actual shipping flow in the strait increases, and whether the negotiations can shift from indirect contact to an enforceable agreement.Reuters

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Today's asset pricing can be observed along five lines: interest rates, energy, risk appetite, technology valuations, and China policies. In terms of interest rates, energy supply remains a significant source of tail risk to global inflation: if there is a verifiable resumption of navigation in Hormuz, oil prices and shipping insurance premiums are expected to decline, thereby alleviating the reflationary pressures faced by major central banks. If negotiations break down and energy prices rise again, it will squeeze the room for interest rate cuts and push up long-term yields. In terms of exchange rates, the US dollar will still be driven by both US interest rate expectations and safe-haven demand, while the currencies of energy-importing countries are more sensitive to changes in oil prices.

The contradiction between risk appetite and technology valuation is more structured. AI investment continues to drive demand for data centers, chips, and cloud computing, but bank warnings about the risks of AI agent payments and copyright disputes in Australia indicate that regulatory costs will rise along with revenue opportunities once commercialization enters real transactions. In China, weak domestic demand for automobiles and companies accelerating their investment in Europe are two sides of the same coin: domestic price competition and insufficient demand have prompted automakers to seek growth overseas, while European tariffs and localization rules have forced the export model to shift to capital and production capacity going global. Today, we should focus on observing the actual shipping flow and oil prices in Hormuz, long-term interest rates between China, the US, and Europe, the trend of the US dollar, and changes in European rules for investing in Chinese automobiles. If energy risks ease, the market may re-increase its valuation tolerance for growth assets; If oil prices and interest rates rise simultaneously, overvalued technology and highly leveraged industries will be the first to come under pressure.
