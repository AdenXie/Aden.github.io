Daily Briefing | 2026-09-20: Saudi Arabia Attack and US-China Talks Affect Energy and Technology Expectations

AdenXie
  2026-09-20 09:00 2026-09-20 09:00 Created  
  • Daily Briefing 
 
  • News 
  • | Market Watch 
 2.4k Words  8 Mins  

Today's main theme

New developments on September 19 brought two types of risks to the market simultaneously: on the one hand, the Houthi rebels in Yemen claimed to have attacked the Saudi capital, plumes of smoke appeared near Riyadh airport, and the vulnerability of energy transportation in the Red Sea and the Gulf was once again tested; On the other hand, Chinese and American finance officials have finalized a meeting in New York, with artificial intelligence security and trade issues on the agenda ahead of next week's high-level diplomacy. In China, the investigation into unfair competition in the platform economy has expanded, and members of the People's Bank of China's Monetary Policy Committee have warned that artificial intelligence may further amplify "strong supply and weak demand". Overseas AI companies are weighing new models, security commitments, and IPO expectations. The common theme is that supply capacity and security risks change rapidly, and whether demand, institutional arrangements, and policy coordination can keep up will determine how energy premiums, technology valuations, and China's domestic demand expectations are repriced. There was little trading over the weekend, and the full impact of the news on prices remains to be seen at the market opening in the new week. Previously, the market was already worried that rising energy prices and high financing costs would squeeze corporate profits. This new attack risk, regulatory actions, and diplomatic arrangements force investors to weigh immediate supply shocks against slower institutional changes. The title refers to expected changes, not price movements that have already occurred.

Five key news items

1. Houthi rebels claim attack on Riyadh, putting alternative energy transport routes under renewed pressure.

On September 19, the Houthi rebels in Yemen claimed to have attacked "sensitive locations" in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, with missiles and drones. Reuters reporters photographed black smoke and flames near the main airport. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted missiles heading toward Riyadh and thwarted attacks on several cities, including the Red Sea port of Yanbu. The extent of the damage to the airport facilities is still pending official verification. The Houthi rebels claimed this move was in response to an earlier attack on the Yemeni capital, Sana'a. The conflict stems from the fact that the Red Sea route has become an important alternative for Gulf energy exports when passage through the Strait of Hormuz is blocked, and the Houthi rebels have recently taken action against Saudi ships and energy facilities. If the Red Sea and onshore facilities face threats simultaneously, tanker insurance, freight rates, and crude oil risk premiums may rise, which could then be transmitted to fuel, inflation expectations, and the central bank's interest rate path. The focus going forward will be on actual facility damage, whether port shipments have been disrupted, the scale of shipping detours, and whether mediation can reduce the frequency of attacks. In particular, it is important to distinguish between attack claims, scene footage, and verified production losses: these three represent completely different supply impacts.Source: Reuters

2. Chinese and US finance officials plan to meet in New York; AI security is on the trade agenda.

According to Reuters, citing sources familiar with the matter, arrangements finalized on September 19 indicate that U.S. Treasury Secretary Besant and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng are scheduled to meet in New York at 10:30 a.m. Eastern Time on Sunday to discuss artificial intelligence security, trade, and other economic issues. The meeting was held at JPMorgan Chase headquarters, which only provided the venue and did not participate in the agenda. The talks are scheduled ahead of US President Trump's hosting of Chinese President*at the White House next week. The market should distinguish between "confirmed meeting" and "reaching an agreement": the report did not publish the negotiation text, nor did it promise to adjust tariffs or technical restrictions. A new highlight is the explicit inclusion of artificial intelligence security in the financial dialogue, which may link computing power, model usage rules, and terms of trade. For businesses, policy predictability affects cross-border orders, investment, and supply chain arrangements; For exchange rates and risk assets, whether the wording tends to soften is more important than the location of the meeting. Pay attention to the announcements from both sides after the meeting, the specific working mechanisms, and whether the summit between the heads of state next week will yield actionable results. Simply announcing continued communication may improve market sentiment in the short term, but companies still need to wait for clear rules before changing their investment plans.Source: Reuters

3. China investigates multiple online booking platforms, extending competition policies to hotels and travel.

Chinese state media reported on September 19 that Beijing's market supervision authorities have launched an investigation into Beijing Sankuai Information Technology Co., Ltd., a subsidiary of Meituan, for suspected violations of the Anti-Unfair Competition Law. Alibaba's subsidiaries Hangzhou Taomei Aviation Services, Tongcheng Network Technology, and Tujia-related companies are also included in the investigation. The relevant companies expressed their cooperation, and the investigation does not equate to a conclusion of illegality. Previously, regulators had fined Ctrip 5.2 billion yuan for monopolistic practices in online hotel bookings; This involvement of multiple platforms demonstrates that regulators are focusing on industry-specific issues such as pricing, platform rules, and the business environment for merchants. Hotel and travel consumption is highly correlated with platform traffic. If the transaction rules are adjusted, it may increase the platform's compliance costs and change commission and promotion revenue in the short term. In the long term, it may affect hotels' bargaining power, consumer choices and service prices. Investors need to wait for the investigation's determination and rectification details; the penalty standards from previous cases cannot be directly applied to the new investigation. We will subsequently observe platform disclosures, changes in merchant fees, and the specific definitions of regulatory restrictions on cross-platform operations. The decline in consumer prices is not an inevitable result, and increased competition may also prompt platforms to cut subsidies. The actual effect must be verified by transaction data.Source: Reuters

4. Central Bank policy advisors warn that AI may exacerbate China's "strong supply and weak demand" situation.

Huang Yiping, a member of the Monetary Policy Committee of the People's Bank of China, said at the Beijing Economic Forum on September 19 that the widespread application of artificial intelligence and the acceleration of innovation may further deepen and last longer the imbalance between strong supply and weak demand. He advocates increasing the proportion of residents' income in the economy through market-oriented reforms and considering increasing central government borrowing to help repair the balance sheets of local governments, financial institutions, and enterprises. This is a public recommendation from policy advisors and should not be considered as the central bank announcing new fiscal or monetary measures. The logic is that AI improves productivity and export supply, but it may not simultaneously increase residents' purchasing power; If domestic demand is insufficient, corporate profits, employment, prices, and debt repayment may all be under pressure. For the market, the focus is shifting from simple computing power investment to consumer revenue, local fiscal recovery, and corporate pricing power. Next, we need to look at official policy documents, residents' income and retail data, prices, and local government financing to determine whether demand-side support is keeping pace with supply expansion.Source: Reuters

5. Anthropic is considering the timing of its new model release, as AI competition and security commitments clash.

An exclusive Reuters report published on September 19, citing three sources familiar with the matter, reported that Anthropic is considering launching a new model to compete with OpenAI following its release of GPT-6 Astra this month. The company has not commented on the report, and the specific release date has not yet been determined. The report, citing a sample from enterprise spending platform Ramp, stated that Astra accounts for approximately 13% of its enterprise AI spending, while Anthropic's Claude Fable accounts for approximately 8%. This reflects the spending composition of a specific platform and is not equivalent to the market share of the entire enterprise. Anthropic’s CEO had previously called for a slowdown in the release of new capabilities to address security concerns, while the company also had to weigh its IPO expectations, model evaluation, and revenue growth. High interest rates and competition with open-source models have increased investors' demands for cash flow, and the iteration speed of leading models will also affect customer procurement, computing power spending, and security costs. The next step will be to determine whether the model is officially released, independent performance and security evaluations, corporate contract renewals, and revenue quality, rather than judging the outcome solely based on release rumors. If the security review is delayed, customers may temporarily switch to competing products; If a hasty launch encounters major problems, subsequent compliance costs will also backfire on growth.Source: Reuters

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

The first thing to test when the market opens next week is whether the Saudi attack has caused actual supply or shipping disruptions: if there are only risk warnings, the oil price premium may fall. If delays or other key milestones are damaged, rising crude oil and freight rates will push up inflation expectations again and limit the room for major central banks to ease interest rates. Changes in interest rate expectations will affect the US dollar, Japanese yen, and emerging market currencies, while suppressing the valuation of long-term growth assets through discount rates. The US-China talks may change risk appetite for the RMB, Asian export chains, and semiconductor-related assets, but policy changes should be judged based on official announcements rather than anonymous information. The China platform survey, along with Huang Yiping's view, points to domestic demand: if competition rules reduce business costs, there will be a lack of residents' income and local fiscal recovery, and consumption improvement may still be limited. In terms of technology, the AI model competition not only drives demand for data centers and chips, but also increases security assessments and capital expenditures; Valuations need to be tested by real paid growth and profit margins. Observe oil and gas spot and shipping prices, US Treasury yields and US dollar trends, RMB expectations, platform company announcements, and verifiable operating indicators of AI companies to avoid confusing risks at different time scales. Weekend news tends to amplify expectations when liquidity is low, and prices, trading volume, and policy statements after the official opening are more likely to test whether the market has truly changed its judgment.
