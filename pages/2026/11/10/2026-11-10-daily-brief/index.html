Daily Briefing | 10 November 2026: US shares staged a strong rebound, with the Nasdaq rising 2.7 per cent; Nvidia bucked the trend, falling 4 per cent amid the ‘spy scandal’ involving Jensen Huang; Trump said it was ‘highly unlikely’ that the US would strike Iran again; oil prices fell by around 5 per cent; China’s October PPI rose to 5.7 per cent, hitting a more than four-year high, whilst CPI fell to 0.7 per cent

Today's main theme

This report covers the period from 8 November (Sunday) to 9 November (Monday). Last week’s AI sell-off saw a strong rebound on Monday: the Dow Jones rose by 1,104 points, the S&P 500 gained 2.35 per cent, the Nasdaq rose by 2.74 per cent, the VIX retreated to around 20, and chip stocks were broadly stronger. The only notable laggard was Nvidia: in a letter to staff on Sunday, Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman whom US intelligence officials believe to be linked to Chinese state security services. Nvidia’s board has launched an independent investigation, and the share price fell by 4.27 per cent that day. In the Middle East, Trump stated that as long as negotiations made progress, it would be “extremely unlikely” that he would order further strikes against Iran. The Prime Minister of Qatar travelled to Washington on Monday to mediate, and the markets bet that the Strait of Hormuz might reopen, causing oil prices to fall by a further 4 per cent to 5 per cent; However, Secretary of State Rubio described Iran’s demands as “outrageous”, suggesting a breakthrough was not imminent. Domestically, the National Bureau of Statistics reported that the Producer Price Index (PPI) rose by 5.7 per cent year-on-year in October, the highest level since July 2022, whilst the Consumer Price Index (CPI) fell to 0.7 per cent, widening the gap between upstream and downstream prices to 5 percentage points. A-shares traded in a narrow range on the day with reduced trading volume, with coal and gas sectors performing strongly whilst lithium battery and solar power sectors weakened.

Key News

1. US shares staged a strong rebound: the Dow rose by 1,104 points, the Nasdaq gained 2.74 per cent, and the VIX fell by more than 20 per cent

According to Yahoo Finance’s closing figures, on 9 November the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose by 1,104.02 points, or 2.15 per cent, to close at 52,389.75 points; the S&P 500 rose by 173.08 points, or 2.35 per cent, to close at 7,549.94; the Nasdaq rose by 673.71 points, or 2.74 per cent, to close at 25,253.14; and the Russell 2000 rose by 3.17 per cent. The VIX index, which measures market volatility, fell by 21.92 per cent to 20.02. According to Kiplinger, this rise in the Nasdaq has pulled it out of the technical correction zone it had only just entered last week.

The gains were concentrated in the sectors that had fallen the most last week. According to Kiplinger, AMD surged 13.3 per cent after Oppenheimer upgraded its rating, whilst Micron rose 6.3 per cent and SanDisk gained 6.2 per cent; Caterpillar rose 7.2 per cent, making it the best-performing component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average, having fallen by more than 10 per cent the previous week. The sharp fall in oil prices also eased inflationary pressures on the stock market; according to Yahoo Finance, the average price of regular petrol in the US on Monday was approximately $4.01 per gallon, down about 10 per cent from its late-September peak. Safe-haven assets did not retreat as a result: the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note remained largely unchanged at around 4.86 per cent, whilst gold rose by approximately 1.8 per cent to $4,149 per ounce.

The rebound does not mean that concerns over interest rate hikes have been allayed. According to Kiplinger, citing CME FedWatch, the market is pricing in an approximately 84 per cent probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the Fed’s next meeting; analyst BeiChen Lin said that even a minor surprise could ‘seal the deal’ for a rate hike in December. According to Yahoo Finance, the market expects the October CPI, due to be released on Tuesday, to rise by 0.3 per cent month-on-month, with core CPI rising by 0.2 per cent month-on-month. Key events to watch: Tuesday’s CPI data, as well as this week’s earnings reports from AI infrastructure companies such as CoreWeave.

Source:Yahoo Finance、Kiplinger

2. Huang Renxun admits to an extramarital affair; his partner is alleged to have links to Chinese intelligence agencies; the NVIDIA board launches an independent investigation

According to Al Jazeera and Yahoo Finance, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair in a letter to staff on Sunday 8 November, writing, “I have been unfaithful to my wife,” and apologising to his family. The other party involved in the affair is Alice Huang (Huang Le), a Chinese national who previously served as an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based AI computing firm. According to Al Jazeera, citing a late October investigation by *The New York Times*, US intelligence officials believe she was recruited by Beijing to gather information on Huang and may have attempted to influence Nvidia’s chip sales to China; Megaspeed is reportedly one of Nvidia’s largest customers, having purchased approximately US$4.8 billion worth of equipment; US authorities have been investigating the company since 2024 to determine whether it has helped China obtain Nvidia chips subject to export controls. It remains unclear what information she has obtained or how much Huang Renxun is aware of; neither has been charged, and the two are not related. In late October, Huang Renxun told Bloomberg that his dealings with her were related to his company’s business and were “entirely normal”.

NVIDIA’s board of directors has launched an independent investigation, led by a committee of directors and external lawyers, to determine whether Jensen Huang has been targeted by foreign intelligence agencies; the company stated that there is no reason to believe that national security or proprietary information has been compromised, and expressed confidence in Huang’s leadership. Political reactions have been polarised: according to The Next Web, Republican chairmen Mulnall, Master and Crawford of the House Select Committee on China, the Foreign Affairs Committee and the Intelligence Committee, respectively, have requested that Huang attend a closed-door briefing on 18 November and respond by 11 November; Senator Warren has demanded an explanation from NVIDIA and is pushing for legislation to prevent advanced technology from flowing to China. Trump, meanwhile, described Huang as “a decent person”. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun stated that the allegations of “Chinese spies” were groundless and amounted to a smear campaign against China.

The market’s reaction centred on Nvidia alone. According to Yahoo Finance, Nvidia closed down 4.27 per cent at US$178.67 on 9 November, having fallen by as much as 8 per cent during the session, clearly lagging behind the broader chip sector, which saw widespread gains that day; NVIDIA has a market capitalisation of approximately US$4.5 trillion, accounting for nearly 7 per cent of the S&P 500. Analysts are focusing on ‘key person risk’: Jensen Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and has yet to name a public successor, though Dan Ives of Wedbush expects him to remain in post for the foreseeable future. The implications of this matter may extend beyond corporate governance: it was only last December that Trump authorised Nvidia to sell its H200 chips to China, and the ‘spy scandal’ could prompt Washington to tighten scrutiny of Nvidia’s overseas clients and chip exports. Key points to watch: progress on the board’s investigation; whether Huang Renxun attends the briefing on 18 November; and Nvidia’s financial results in mid-to-late November.

Source:Al Jazeera、Yahoo Finance、The Next Web

3. Trump says it is “highly unlikely” that the US will attack Iran again; the Qatari Prime Minister travels to Washington to mediate; oil prices fall by a further 5 per cent

According to Reuters (as reported by the Hawaii Forum Herald), Trump told CNN in a telephone interview on 8 November that it was “highly unlikely” he would order new strikes against Iran as long as negotiations made progress, adding that whether an agreement could be reached “depends on whether they sign a very strong agreement”; However, he also stated that, should strikes resume, the US military had already identified 38 bridges that could be swiftly destroyed, and announced that Operation Iron Gate, targeting Iran’s land and Caspian Sea transport routes, was now fully in effect. Trump also acknowledged that the Iran conflict was one of the reasons for the Republicans’ defeat in the mid-term elections.

Mediation efforts gathered pace on Monday. According to *Daily Call*, citing Reuters, Qatar’s Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Mohammed met with Trump in Washington on 9 November and is expected to meet with Vice-President Vance. According to the Iranian proposal reported by Reuters, the US would end its blockade of Iranian ports, lift some sanctions and cease air strikes, whereupon Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, whilst the nuclear issue and remaining sanctions would be left for future negotiations. Israel’s *Israel Today* newspaper, citing diplomatic sources, reported that the proposal also involves unfreezing approximately US$8 billion in Iranian funds held in Qatar; this claim has not been officially confirmed. However, significant differences remain: Rubio said on Sunday that Iran’s demands were “outrageous, absurd and unrealistic”, and that the negotiations had “not been very productive”; US and Gulf officials also told Reuters that an agreement was not imminent. Maritime risks have not abated either: the Iranian Revolutionary Guard claimed that two more oil tankers had struck mines and caught fire in the Strait, whilst the US military stated that on Saturday it had disabled a merchant vessel attempting to circumvent the blockade.

The market initially priced in a moderation. According to Yahoo Finance, the WTI December contract closed down 4.14 per cent at US$87.07, whilst Brent traded around US$91 per barrel in the afternoon; according to economies.com, Brent had fallen by a cumulative 16 per cent last week, marking its largest weekly decline since the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic. JPMorgan estimates that global crude oil supply exceeded demand by approximately 1.4 million barrels per day in October, up from 1.1 million barrels per day in September. Key points to watch going forward include the official statement following the meeting with the Qatari Prime Minister, whether the nuclear issue will be included in the first round of negotiations, and whether Brent can hold above the $90 mark.

Source:Reuters (reprinted from the Hawaii Forum Herald)、Daily calls、Today’s Israel News、economies.com

4. China’s PPI rose by 5.7 per cent year-on-year in October, hitting a more than four-year high, whilst the CPI fell to 0.7 per cent

The National Bureau of Statistics announced on 9 November that the national CPI rose by 0.7 per cent year-on-year in October, down from 0.9 per cent in September and below market expectations of 0.8 per cent, whilst remaining unchanged month-on-month; the average for January to October was up 0.9 per cent compared with the same period last year. The Producer Price Index (PPI) rose by 5.7 per cent year-on-year, higher than the 5.0 per cent recorded in September and the market forecast of approximately 5.3 per cent, marking the highest level since July 2022; month-on-month, it rose by 0.7 per cent, with the rate of increase slowing by 0.5 percentage points compared with September; the Purchasing Prices of Industrial Producers rose by 7.9 per cent year-on-year. According to data compiled by BigGo Finance, the core CPI, excluding food and energy, rose by 0.8 per cent year-on-year, down 0.2 percentage points from September, whilst the gap between the PPI and CPI widened to 5 percentage points.

Structurally, the rise in upstream prices was primarily driven by energy. According to CGTN, citing Dong Lijuan, Chief Statistician of the Urban Statistics Department at the National Bureau of Statistics, rising international energy prices led to a month-on-month increase of 6 per cent in oil and gas extraction prices and a 2.7 per cent rise in coal mining and washing prices. whilst increased demand for coal and gas during the winter months also played a part. Six sectors—including petroleum processing, organic chemicals, coal processing and gas—collectively accounted for more than 80 per cent of the month-on-month rise in the Producer Price Index (PPI). On the consumer side, food prices acted as a drag: falling prices for eggs, fresh vegetables and fresh fruit collectively contributed to a month-on-month decline of approximately 0.13 percentage points in the Consumer Price Index (CPI); Domestic petrol prices rose by 4.2 per cent month-on-month, whilst increased travel during the National Day holiday drove service prices up by 0.1 per cent. According to BigGo Finance, prices for data storage devices, tablets, computers and mobile phones rose significantly year-on-year, reflecting price increases in electronic products driven by demand for AI.

This set of data indicates that the current rise in prices is primarily driven by external factors, whilst domestic demand remains relatively weak. Zhang Di of Galaxy Securities believes that the recovery in demand remains sluggish, and that the key question is whether domestic prices can hold steady following the fall in crude oil prices; Feng Lin of Orient Gold Credit forecasts that the year-on-year CPI for November may fall to around 0.6 per cent. Profits at mid- and downstream manufacturing enterprises—particularly in sectors with weak pricing power, such as the automotive and household appliance industries—will be squeezed by rising costs. With oil prices having fallen sharply following the mid-term elections, the upward trend in the November PPI is likely to slow. Key points to watch: the forthcoming October data on imports and exports, aggregate social financing and credit, as well as whether the central bank will maintain its moderately accommodative stance.

Source:CGTN、Xinhua News、BigGo Finance

5. A-shares traded in a narrow range on lower volume: the Shanghai Composite Index rose by 0.32 per cent, with coal and gas sectors performing strongly whilst lithium battery and solar power sectors weakened

According to Sina Finance, on 9 November, the Shanghai Composite Index rose by 0.32 per cent to close at 3,660.79 points; the Shenzhen Component Index fell by 0.27 per cent to close at 11,145.49 points; and the ChiNext Index fell by 0.27 per cent to close at 2,458.56 points; the STAR Market Composite Index rose by 0.44 per cent, whilst the Beijing Stock Exchange 50 Index rose by 0.82 per cent. Trading volume across the Shanghai and Shenzhen markets totalled approximately 1.48 trillion yuan, down by around 175.2 billion yuan compared with last Friday. According to Sohu Finance, more than 3,100 individual stocks across the market fell.

In terms of sectors, upstream energy stocks mirrored the PPI data. According to Sina Finance and Sohu Finance, the coal sector opened higher and continued to rise, with Zhengzhou Coal & Electricity and Yunmei Energy hitting the daily upper limit, whilst Yankuang Energy rose by over 4 per cent; the gas sector also strengthened, with Dongfang Huan Yu and Shengtong Energy hitting the daily upper limit. Humanoid robots, 6G, satellite navigation, copper-clad laminates and electronic components were also relatively active. On the downside were energy metals and photovoltaics: EVE Energy fell by 6.75 per cent, Tianqi Lithium fell by over 3 per cent, whilst Sungrow and LONGi Green Energy also declined; the photovoltaic sector, which had led gains last week, opened higher but closed lower.

When the A-share market closed on Monday, the US stock market had not yet opened. The combination of sharp gains in US chip stocks and a decline in Nvidia shares that evening is likely to influence the opening of the A-share market’s computing power and optical module sectors today. The decline in trading volume suggests that investors remain on the sidelines, with no significant influx of capital beyond that aimed at stabilising the market. Key points to watch: whether A-share technology stocks will follow the US market’s rebound today, whether trading volume will return to above 1.6 trillion yuan, and the sustainability of the coal sector’s performance following the fall in oil prices.

Source:Sina Finance、Sohu Finance

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Taking several news items together, the main theme in the market on Monday was that ‘risk appetite has returned, but two risks remain’. The first risk concerns interest rates: the rebound in US equities has been fuelled by oversold conditions and falling oil prices, whilst the market’s pricing in of a rate rise in December has actually risen to around 84 per cent; should Tuesday’s CPI figures come in higher than expected, highly valued technology stocks will come under pressure once again. The second risk lies with individual stocks and policy: Nvidia’s decline indicates that the market is beginning to price in the Huang Renxun incident, which affects not only corporate governance but may also lead to a further tightening of US scrutiny on chip exports. This would have implications for the chip supply chain reliant on overseas customers and for China’s procurement of computing power.

Oil prices have been the key focal point this week. Expectations of a de-escalation in US-Iran negotiations have sent Brent crude tumbling from its recent high of nearly US$118 to around US$91, which is set to ease inflationary pressures in the US and the UK, whilst also slowing the rise in China’s November PPI. China’s data, however, serves as a reminder that the current ‘resurgence in inflation’ stems primarily from energy and AI-related electronic products, whilst consumer spending remains subdued; profits among upstream energy firms and mid-to-downstream manufacturing enterprises are set to continue diverging.

Checklist:

  • Progress on the NVIDIA board of directors’ investigation; whether Jensen Huang will attend the closed-door congressional briefing on 18 November; and whether the US will tighten restrictions on chip exports to China
  • Statements from the US and Iran following the Qatari Prime Minister’s visit to the US: can Brent hold above $90?
