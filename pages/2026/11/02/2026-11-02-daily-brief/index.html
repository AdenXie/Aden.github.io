Daily Briefing | 2 November 2026: Three departments meet today to discuss market stabilisation measures; South Korea’s October exports surge by 93.7 per cent to a record high; Apple launches ‘Qianwen’ in China; OPEC+ maintains December output levels

Today's main theme

This edition covers news from the weekend of 31 October to 1 November. Following the sharp fall in A-shares in October, regulators sent signals over the weekend aimed at stabilising the market: according to reports from several media outlets, the People’s Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the China Securities Regulatory Commission will each convene meetings today (2 November) with securities firms, fund managers and major banks to discuss promoting the use of capital market support instruments and optimising re-lending schemes for share buybacks and share purchases. External data, meanwhile, indicates that demand for AI continues to rise: South Korea’s exports in October reached a record US$115.3 billion, up 93.7 per cent year-on-year, with chip exports surging by 259 per cent – a stark contrast to last week’s concerns on the US stock market that investment in AI was ‘burning through cash too quickly’. In the technology sector, Alibaba announced that its Qianwen large language model will be integrated into Apple’s smart devices in China; this marks the first time a leading global consumer electronics manufacturer has utilised a local large language model as a system-level AI foundation in China. On the energy front, the seven core OPEC+ member states have decided to maintain their December production targets unchanged, leaving the difficult decisions to the ministerial meeting on 29 November, which will determine the 2027 quotas. In international politics, the G20 Foreign Ministers’ Meeting failed to issue a joint statement, with the US noting that the limited ceasefire in the Black Sea had garnered greater attention; negotiations between Iran and the Qatari mediators entered their third day, with the US mid-term elections taking place tomorrow.

Key News

1. Three departments held a meeting on 2 November to discuss optimising the re-lending scheme for share buybacks and share purchases, and to promote the use of market stabilisation tools

According to reports on 1 November by The Paper, Sing Tao Global Network and several other media outlets, the People’s Bank of China and the China Securities Regulatory Commission are due to hold a meeting on the morning of 2 November to discuss measures to promote the use of capital market instruments; some securities firms and fund management companies have already been notified; On the same day, the People’s Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the China Securities Regulatory Commission will also convene a meeting with major banks at the central bank to discuss optimising the re-lending policy for share buybacks and share purchases. The specific details of the meeting have not yet been announced.

Both of these instruments were announced by Pan Gongsheng, Governor of the People’s Bank of China, on 24 September 2024: one is a swap facility for securities firms, fund management companies and insurance companies, and the other is a special re-lending facility for share buybacks and shareholdings. According to an analysis by Sing Tao Global Network, the initial quota for the re-lending facility was 300 billion yuan, with an annual interest rate of 1.75 per cent; subsequently, the loan term was extended to three years, the required proportion of own funds was reduced from 30 per cent to 10 per cent, and state-owned capital operation platforms were also included among the eligible entities; in May 2025, the quotas for the two instruments were merged, with the total amount increased to 800 billion yuan. According to Wind statistics, in the first ten months of this year, more than 300 A-share companies disclosed announcements relating to ‘share buyback and shareholding increase loans’, with a cumulative total of approximately 78.1 billion yuan based on the loan ceiling; of this, Haier Smart Home received a special loan of 5 billion yuan, whilst BOE A secured two loans totalling 5.9 billion yuan.

The timing of the meeting is worth noting. A-shares continued to fall throughout October, with the Shanghai Composite Index closing at 3,599.02 points on 30 October, falling below the 3,600-point mark; Over the month of October, both the STAR Market Composite Index and the ChiNext Index fell by more than 20 per cent, whilst the Shenzhen Composite Index fell by 15.94 per cent and the Shanghai Composite Index by 6.33 per cent. Coupled with the official manufacturing PMI falling back to 49.3 per cent in October, market expectations for policies to stabilise the market and sustain growth have clearly intensified. Tian Lihui, Director of the Institute of Financial Development at Nankai University, believes that such instruments can provide incremental liquidity and reduce the cost of capital for listed companies and major shareholders, whilst also signalling the regulators’ commitment to stabilising the capital markets.

Key points to watch: whether specific measures—such as an increase in the quota, a cut in interest rates or a relaxation of eligibility criteria—will be announced following the meeting; whether the scale of operations under the swap facility will be expanded; and whether the technology sector of the A-share market will halt its decline this week. The central bank has, with effect from the same day, designated overnight reverse repurchase agreements as the primary open market operation tool; the money market rates in the first week under this new framework are also worth monitoring.

Source:The Paper、Sing Tao Global Network、Dazhong Net

2. South Korea’s exports in October reached a record US$115.3 billion, up 93.7 per cent year-on-year, with chip exports rising by 259 per cent

On 1 November, South Korea’s Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy announced that exports in October totalled US$115.3 billion, setting a new monthly record and representing a year-on-year increase of 93.7 per cent; imports stood at US$72.78 billion, up 36 per cent; and the trade surplus was US$42.5 billion. According to *The Korea Times*, cumulative exports from January to October exceeded US$900 billion for the first time, and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy expects full-year exports to surpass US$1 trillion as early as November.

The growth was primarily driven by the semiconductor sector. Chip exports in October totalled US$56.4 billion, a year-on-year increase of 259 per cent; the Ministry of Industry attributed this to strong global demand for chips and continued investment by technology companies in AI infrastructure. Other categories also saw growth: exports of petroleum products reached US$6.92 billion, up 80 per cent, driven by high oil prices; exports of motor vehicles totalled US$6.55 billion, up 18.1 per cent; and exports of cosmetics reached US$1.44 billion, up 57.5 per cent. By market, exports to China amounted to US$26.98 billion, up 133.6 per cent, having exceeded US$20 billion for five consecutive months; exports to the US totalled US$24.9 billion, an increase of 185.8 per cent, driven mainly by semiconductors, computers and electrical equipment for AI infrastructure; exports to South-East Asia totalled US$21.33 billion, an increase of 128 per cent; and exports to the EU totalled US$8.1 billion, an increase of 56.4 per cent.

South Korean exports are often regarded as a ‘barometer’ of global demand. These figures indicate that, despite concerns in the US stock market regarding the cash flows of major technology firms, actual procurement of AI hardware has not yet slowed, and memory chip manufacturers continue to benefit. Meanwhile, Industry Minister Kim Jeong-gwan cautioned that protectionism, supply chain adjustments and the situation in the Middle East mean the trade environment remains uncertain. Key points to watch: the share price performance of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix; China’s October import and export figures, due to be released in early November; and whether major tech firms will cut their capital expenditure for 2027.

Source:The Korea Times

3. AliQianwen is set to be integrated into Apple’s smart services in China, launching in December alongside iOS 27.3

On 31 October, Alibaba announced a partnership with Apple, under which the Qianwen large language model will be integrated into Apple Intelligence in China. According to Xinhua News Agency (as reported by CGTN), Qianwen will support features such as Siri, writing tools and visual intelligence, providing Chinese users with text and image understanding as well as content generation; these features will be available for trial in the latest iOS developer beta from 4 November, and will be rolled out to Chinese users in December with the official release of iOS 27.3. This service is available to users who have purchased an iPhone supporting Apple Intelligence in China, or whose device location and Apple account region are both set to China; users can choose whether to enable the service and which information to share, and Alibaba has pledged not to use users’ requests or generated content to train the model.

In terms of regulation, it has been reported that the Cyberspace Administration of China completed the filing process for seven mobile generative AI services, including Apple’s, in July this year. Wang Xiaojie, an artificial intelligence expert at Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, believes this marks the first time that a leading global consumer hardware manufacturer has utilised a Chinese large language model as the primary system-level AI foundation in China. In an interview with TechCrunch on the same day, Apple CEO John Ternus stated that Apple would not introduce AI for the sake of AI alone; successful AI integration should be such that users are largely unaware of its presence.

For Apple, the Apple Smart service has not been officially launched in China since its release in 2024; this partnership fills a gap in its offering. For Alibaba, Qianwen has gained a system-level entry point to iPhone users in China, meaning that competition amongst domestic large language models will extend from model capabilities to end-user access points. Key points to watch: the actual user experience of the beta version on 4 November; whether Apple’s sales in China will rebound as a result; and how other mobile phone manufacturers and large language model companies adjust their partnerships.

Source:CGTN (Xinhua News Agency)、TechCrunch

4. OPEC+ has kept its December production targets unchanged; the 2027 quotas will be decided on 29 November

According to Reuters (as reported by GV Wire), seven core OPEC+ member states—Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman—held an online meeting on 1 November, at which they decided to maintain their December production targets unchanged and signalled that there would be no further policy adjustments this year. These seven countries had already completed the phased exit from the 1.65 million barrels per day production cut for 2023 in September. OPEC+ has a further production cut of approximately 2 million barrels per day covering most of its members, which will continue until the end of 2026. The United Arab Emirates withdrew from OPEC in May and is no longer involved in this decision.

The backdrop is the impact of the war in Iran on Gulf exports. Gulf member states are striving to restore exports that have been disrupted for several months, but actual production in most countries remains well below their quotas. Before setting the 2027 benchmark production level, OPEC+ needs to complete a production capacity assessment in the fourth quarter; as the war has disrupted expansion projects, the assessment is not expected to be finalised until mid-November. A plenary ministerial meeting of the 21 member states will be held on 29 November, at which the 2027 production framework and quotas are expected to be decided.

Oil prices remain high. According to Reuters, Brent crude rose by more than 2 per cent on 30 October, closing at nearly US$118—its highest level since April—as traders braced for a possible escalation of hostilities with Iran following the US mid-term elections. Meanwhile, organisations such as the International Energy Agency have warned that a supply surplus could emerge in 2027 if supplies from the Gulf return to normal. Jorge León, an analyst at Rystad, said that this decision was “the easy one” and that OPEC+ faces an “extremely difficult decision” in 2027. Key points to watch: the reaction of oil prices at the opening on Monday, the results of the production assessment in mid-November, and whether shipping through the Strait of Hormuz can resume.

Source:Reuters (reprinted from GV Wire)

5. No joint statement issued at the G20 Foreign Ministers’ Meeting; Rubio says limited ceasefire in the Black Sea is attracting more attention

The G20 Foreign Ministers’ Meeting concluded on 31 October in Atlanta, USA; the two-day meeting did not issue a joint statement. According to Reuters (as reported by GV Wire), US Secretary of State Rubio stated after the meeting that there is growing international interest in a limited ceasefire in the Black Sea, an arrangement that would allow Russia and Ukraine to transport energy and food; the US is examining the proposal put forward by Turkey and India to resume grain shipments, but continues to prioritise a comprehensive end to the war through negotiations. He said this “would not end the war, but at least it would resolve one specific aspect of it”, and warned that the war threatens food and energy security and could also spill over.

Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov attended the meeting, to which Europe and Canada objected; the Ukrainian Foreign Minister was not invited. Canadian Foreign Minister Anand said she had discussed a limited ceasefire in the Black Sea and a suspension of attacks on Ukrainian power infrastructure with her Ukrainian and Indian counterparts; vessels and crews from India and Egypt have been affected and are seeking safe passage. Anand said Ukraine was willing to participate, but the Russian side had shown no willingness to move forward; Lavrov, meanwhile, stated that Moscow’s position remained unchanged. Rubio also briefly discussed with Lavrov the possibility of a meeting between Trump and Putin at next month’s APEC summit and the G20 summit in Miami in December, though he noted that Putin’s attendance at the summit had not yet been confirmed.

In the Middle East, according to Reuters, Iranian negotiators held their third day of talks with Qatari mediators in Doha on 1 November; no results have been announced as yet. On 31 October, Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei called on the military to respond to any attack with “no geographical restrictions”; Rezaei, head of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, stated that should hostilities resume, Tehran is discussing whether to lift its long-standing ban on the development of nuclear weapons, though no decision has yet been taken. Trump had previously indicated that he would not launch new strikes before the mid-term elections, but did not rule out taking action after the polls. Key points to watch: whether substantial progress can be made on the Black Sea ceasefire proposal; the US’s decision regarding Iran following the mid-term elections on 3 November; and the outcome of the Doha negotiations.

Source:Reuters (reprinted from GV Wire)、Arab News (Reuters)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

There was a development in the crypto market over the weekend: according to CoinDesk, Binance began a phased resumption of withdrawals on 1 November, with the first phase covering Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, USDC, BNB and XRP, as well as withdrawals on 15 other blockchains; Binance stated that it had used company funds to cover approximately 95 per cent of the affected assets, up from 90 per cent the previous day; the remaining 5 per cent consists of tokens outside the top ten with lower liquidity. Approximately US$3 billion remains frozen, primarily in USDT and USDC. According to CoinDesk’s live market data, Bitcoin is trading at around US$77,900 and Ethereum at around US$2,260, with no new panic selling observed in the market.

Looking at several news items together: the direction of the key variables at the start of this week is not consistent. On the domestic front, the meeting of the three government departments is the most direct factor affecting the A-share market. Should the meeting result in measures to expand market capacity or reduce costs, technology and growth stocks—which were heavily sold off in October—may lead the rebound; however, with the PMI indicating weak demand, whether policy measures can bring about a sustained inflow of capital will depend on the specific details of the regulations. Overseas, South Korean export figures indicate that demand for AI hardware remains strong. This provides support for the memory chip sector and the A-share computing power industry chain, whilst also shifting the focus of discussions on the ‘AI bubble’ towards the returns of downstream companies rather than demand itself. Apple’s integration of Qianwen is positive news for Alibaba and the domestic AI application ecosystem. As for oil prices, with OPEC+ holding off on production increases and the risk of military action against Iran following the election still present, Brent crude is likely to remain at elevated levels, further fuelling expectations of interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve and other central banks. Tomorrow (3 November) sees both the US mid-term elections and the RBA’s monetary policy meeting on the same day; the market widely expects the RBA to raise interest rates by 25 basis points to 4.85 per cent.

Checklist:

  • Outcome of the three-department meeting on 2 November, and whether the A-share technology sector can halt its decline
  • China’s October RatingDog Manufacturing PMI (expected to be released early this week) and the overnight reverse repo rate under the new framework
  • The RBA’s interest rate decision on 3 November, and the Australian dollar’s reaction against the US dollar and the renminbi
  • The results of the US mid-term elections on 3 November, and the post-election military decisions regarding Iran
  • Progress in the Doha talks: will Brent crude break through the $120 mark?
  • Share prices of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, and the status of Apple’s AI feature testing in China

Source:CoinDesk
