Daily Briefing | 18 November 2026: NVIDIA’s first ‘post-Jensen Huang’ financial report shows revenue of US$115.8 billion, exceeding expectations, with Q4 guidance of US$130 billion; Jensen Huang states he is willing to return to his post if the board approves, US software shares plummet ahead of Anthropic’s IPO pricing; US October retail sales rise by 0.2 per cent, import prices exceed expectations; A-share chip and AI sectors show divergent trends; Trump arrives in Manila to attend the Quad summit and sign a trade declaration with Thailand

Today's main theme

This edition covers the period from the Asian trading session on Tuesday, 17 November, through to the US after-hours market. The main focus of the day was Nvidia’s earnings report. The first earnings report following Jensen Huang’s resignation was released after the US market close: third-quarter revenue stood at US$115.8 billion, approximately US$7 billion higher than market expectations, with fourth-quarter revenue guidance set at US$130 billion (excluding revenue from data centre computing power in China); the share price rose by approximately 2 per cent to 3 per cent in after-hours trading. Prior to the earnings release, Huang Renxun made his first public statement, saying that he would be willing to return as CEO if the board believed his return would be in the best interests of shareholders, employees, the company and the United States; however, CBS, citing sources familiar with the matter, reported that the board was opposed to this. During the day, US equities continued to fall ahead of the earnings release and Anthropic’s IPO pricing, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping by approximately 955 points, led by enterprise software stocks such as Salesforce. US retail sales rose by 0.2 per cent in October, whilst core retail and import prices both exceeded expectations, leading the market to continue betting on interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve in December and January next year. In the A-share market, semiconductor equipment stocks bucked the trend to rise, whilst AI applications and robotics stocks weakened, with the ChiNext Index falling by 1.2 per cent. On the international political front, Trump arrived in Manila to attend his first Quad summit of his second term and signed a joint trade statement with Thailand; he will subsequently travel to Shenzhen to attend the APEC meeting.

Key News

1. NVIDIA reported third-quarter revenue of US$115.8 billion and provided fourth-quarter guidance of US$130 billion; Jensen Huang stated he was “willing to return to the role”, but the board reportedly opposed the move

NVIDIA announced its results for the third quarter of the 2027 financial year (ending 25 October) after the close of US markets on 17 November. According to the company’s financial report, quarterly revenue stood at US$115.8 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 103 per cent and a quarter-on-quarter rise of 20 per cent; Data centre revenue stood at US$107.6 billion, up 110 per cent year-on-year; GAAP gross margin was 74.8 per cent, whilst non-GAAP gross margin was 74.9 per cent; non-GAAP earnings per share were US$2.68. According to Investing.com, market expectations stood at approximately US$108.85 billion in revenue and US$2.47 in earnings per share, both of which were significantly exceeded. The company’s revenue guidance for the fourth quarter is US$130 billion (plus or minus 3 per cent), excluding revenue from data centre computing power in China, which is higher than market expectations of approximately US$124.9 billion; Gross margin guidance is 71 per cent to 72 per cent, in line with previous guidance, primarily due to rising memory costs. In the third quarter, the company returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through share buybacks and dividends.

This was the first earnings call chaired by interim CEO and CFO Kress. During the call, she stated that “our momentum is real”, that the Vera Rubin platform had “got off to a strong start”, and that the product roadmap was progressing as planned. According to Investing.com, the board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee; Jensen Huang and General Counsel Tete have agreed to appear before the House Committees on Foreign Affairs, the Select Committee on China, and the Intelligence Committee. The report also cited *The Wall Street Journal* as stating that the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has launched an investigation into Nvidia, focusing on whether the company’s disclosures misled investors.

Jensen Huang himself responded publicly for the first time to rumours of his return ahead of the earnings report. According to CoinDesk, citing The Wall Street Journal, he said that since his resignation he had received a flood of requests from employees, investors, partners and customers asking him to return. “If the board believes my return is in the best interests of shareholders, employees, the company and the United States, I would be honoured to respond to these calls and resume my role as CEO,” he said. However, according to CBS News, a source familiar with the board’s thinking stated that the board is opposed to his reinstatement. NVIDIA closed at US$155.32 during regular trading hours and rose by approximately 2 per cent to 3 per cent in after-hours trading; CBS reported that the share price briefly reached US$159.60. NVIDIA’s share price fell by 10.4 per cent in October and has dropped a further 24.8 per cent since the start of November. Key points to watch: whether the board formally responds to Huang Renxun’s remarks, the progress of investigations by the SEC and Congress, and whether strong guidance can help stabilise AI hardware stocks.

Source:NVIDIA Financial Results (SEC Form 8-K)、Investing.com、CBS News、CoinDesk

2. US shares continued to fall ahead of the earnings season, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping by around 955 points; Salesforce fell by more than 9 per cent, whilst enterprise software stocks led the decline ahead of Anthropic’s pricing announcement

According to 24/7 Wall St’s closing market update, on 17 November the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by approximately 955 points, a decline of around 1.85 per cent; the S&P 500 fell by approximately 1.05 per cent; and the Nasdaq fell by approximately 0.91 per cent; Closing figures from different sources vary slightly; the final data from the exchanges shall prevail. According to Yahoo Finance Live, the VIX rose to around 26 during the session, whilst the 10-year US Treasury yield stood at approximately 5.09 per cent, having fallen slightly. European equities also fell, with the FTSE 100 down 1.35 per cent and the German DAX down 1.78 per cent.

Enterprise software suffered the heaviest losses that day. According to 24/7 Wall St, Salesforce closed down 9.6 per cent, Intuit fell 7.1 per cent and ServiceNow dropped 6.8 per cent. According to TipRanks, there were no company-specific announcements from Salesforce that day; the market’s reaction was driven primarily by two factors: firstly, Anthropic set its share price on Wednesday and listed on the Nasdaq under the ticker ‘ANT’ on Thursday, leading investors to worry that AI agents such as Claude might replace some of the functions of enterprise software; secondly, Salesforce was implicated in a data breach linked to malicious code on the website optimisation platform Optimizely. Salesforce had already fallen by approximately 18.9 per cent year-to-date. Furthermore, according to Jiemian News (reprinted by Sina), research firm SemiAnalysis lowered its forecast for AI computing power capital expenditure in 2028 by nearly 30 per cent ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report, heightening caution amongst technology stocks.

Bitcoin also followed suit and weakened. According to CoinDesk, Bitcoin traded between $63,600 and $64,500 that day, whilst Nvidia rebounded slightly following a post-market rise. Key points to watch: Anthropic’s final offering price on Wednesday, its performance on its first day of trading on Thursday, and whether the sell-off in software stocks will spread to more SaaS companies.

Source:24/7 Wall St、Yahoo Finance (Live)、TipRanks、CoinDesk

3. US retail sales rose by 0.2 per cent in October, whilst core retail and import prices were higher than expected, fuelling further expectations of an interest rate rise

According to Reuters (as reported by KFGO), US retail sales rose by 0.2 per cent month-on-month in October, in line with expectations, and increased by 6.8 per cent year-on-year. Sales at car dealerships fell by 1.6 per cent, whilst furniture shops saw a decline of 2.3 per cent; clothing and sporting goods also recorded declines. Mail-order and online retail rose by 1.2 per cent, building materials by 0.5 per cent, and food services by 0.2 per cent. The closely watched core retail sales figure (excluding motor vehicles, petrol, building materials and food services) rose by 0.6 per cent, exceeding the forecast of 0.4 per cent, whilst September’s figure was revised upwards to 0.5 per cent. Core retail sales are more closely linked to consumer spending in GDP, indicating that consumers are still spending despite high prices.

Inflation figures are on the high side. Import prices rose by 1.1 per cent month-on-month in October, exceeding the expected 0.7 per cent and matching the September increase; fuel prices rose by 3.6 per cent, whilst prices excluding fuel and food rose by 0.9 per cent, and capital goods rose by 1.5 per cent. Reuters reported that this was linked to imports of non-electrical machinery driven by AI infrastructure development. Last month, the Federal Reserve kept its policy rate unchanged at 3.75 per cent to 4.00 per cent. According to CME FedWatch, the market expects a 25-basis-point rate hike in December, followed by another in January. Anderson, Chief US Economist at BMO, said that whilst consumers are under pressure, they are still spending, and actual expenditure is sufficient for the Federal Reserve to continue focusing on inflation. According to Yahoo Finance Live, another Federal Reserve official called for earlier rate rises on the same day, whilst the National Association of Home Builders’ confidence index fell to a 14-year low.

From the market’s perspective, robust consumer spending and high import prices mean the Federal Reserve has no reason to slow the pace of interest rate rises; this is also the backdrop against which the 10-year US Treasury yield has remained above 5 per cent and growth stock valuations have come under sustained pressure. Key developments to watch: speeches by Federal Reserve officials this week, as well as the November non-farm payrolls and CPI figures due to be released ahead of the December monetary policy meeting.

Source:KFGO (Reuters)、Yahoo Finance (Live)

4. A-shares fell on lower trading volumes, with chip and AI sectors showing mixed performance: semiconductor equipment stocks bucked the trend to rise, whilst the ChiNext Index fell by 1.2 per cent and the Hang Seng Index fell by 1 per cent

According to Jiemian News (as reported by Sina), on 17 November, the Shanghai Composite Index fell by 0.50 per cent to close at 3,584.58 points; the Shenzhen Component Index fell by 0.49 per cent to close at 10,638.52 points; the ChiNext Index fell by 1.20 per cent to close at 2,292.20 points, continuing its downward trend after hitting a new low for the year the previous day; the STAR 50 Index bucked the trend, rising by 0.62 per cent to close at 1,221.19 points. Trading volume on the Shanghai and Shenzhen markets totalled 1.46 trillion yuan, down by 26.8 billion yuan from the previous trading day; more than 4,100 individual stocks fell, with 67 hitting their daily lower limit.

There was a marked divergence across sectors. Semiconductor equipment and materials led the gains, with the CSI Semiconductor-related ETF rising by 3.18 per cent, whilst sectors such as lithography, target materials and photoresists performed strongly; the ChiNext Artificial Intelligence Index fell by 3.18 per cent, the STAR Market chip design sector dropped by 2.80 per cent, the robotics sector fell by 2.16 per cent, and the satellite industry declined by 1.91 per cent, whilst the film and television, physical AI, robotics and commercial space sectors saw a wave of stocks hitting their daily lower limits. Reports suggest that support for chip equipment stems from SMIC’s third-quarter gross profit margin exceeding the upper limit of its guidance, a capacity utilisation rate of 97.3 per cent, delivery lead times extending to 16–24 months, and a domestic production rate of approximately 35 per cent; On the AI applications front, the sector was weighed down by SemiAnalysis’s downward revision of AI capital expenditure forecasts and poor earnings from platform companies such as Baidu.

The Hong Kong market also saw a decline. The Hang Seng Index fell by 1.03 per cent to close at 23,508.17 points; the Hang Seng Tech Index dropped by 1.14 per cent, with internet platform shares falling whilst chip stocks rose. The trend over the past two days indicates that, against a backdrop of the US tightening export controls on AI chips and weaker domestic demand data, investors are more inclined to bet on the equipment and materials sectors—where domestic substitution is more certain—rather than the application sector, whose valuations rely heavily on the AI narrative. Key developments to watch: the reaction of the computing power and optical module sectors on the A-share market following Nvidia’s earnings report; the LPR quote on 20 November; and whether there will be any new developments regarding China-US economic and trade relations during the APEC summit.

Source:Jiemian News (reprinted from Sina Finance)

5. Trump arrived in Manila to attend the Quad summit, announced joint maritime patrol aircraft exercises, and signed a joint trade statement with Thailand

According to ABC News, Trump arrived in Manila at 17:33 local time on 17 November to hold a Quad Security Dialogue meeting with Australian Prime Minister Albanese, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Indian Prime Minister Modi; this marked his first Quad meeting since returning to the White House. Trump stated that the Quad would advance cooperation in the field of artificial intelligence and announced that the four nations would conduct a joint maritime patrol aircraft exercise. At the time of writing, there had been no reports of a joint statement being issued following the meeting. According to Gulf News, the Quad’s agenda also included maritime security, critical minerals and semiconductor supply chains.

On the sidelines of the ASEAN Summit, Mr Trump and Thai Prime Minister Anutin signed a joint trade statement. According to ABC News, Trump stated that Thailand would remove all tariffs on US exports, eliminate non-tariff barriers on products such as cars, medicines and poultry, and purchase US agricultural products worth US$2.6 billion; a formal agreement is to be finalised in the “coming weeks”, whilst Anutin also noted that further work remains to be done. According to Gulf News, Trump is expected to hold talks with Philippine President Marcos Jr. on 18 November, with the US-Philippines Mutual Defence Treaty and the situation in the South China Sea being key topics; on the same day, student groups in Manila staged a protest, holding placards reading ‘Trump, get out’.

Following his visit to Manila, Mr Trump will travel to Shenzhen to attend the APEC Informal Leaders’ Meeting. According to Hong Kong 01, the APEC CEO Summit will be held in Qianhai, Shenzhen, from 17 to 18 November. Xi Jinping will attend the opening ceremony and deliver a keynote speech. The summit is expected to be attended by more than 2,000 figures from the political and business spheres, with US companies constituting the largest foreign contingent, accounting for approximately 20 per cent; For the first time, the summit will issue an outcome document, the ‘Shenzhen Initiative’, in the form of an initiative. The Informal Leaders’ Meeting will take place on 18–19 November; the Kremlin had previously stated that a trilateral meeting between China, the US and Russia was scheduled for 19 November. Key points to watch: the outcome of the talks between Trump and Marcos Jr.; the specific arrangements regarding semiconductors and critical minerals at the four-party meeting; and Xi Jinping’s speech at APEC and the meeting between the Chinese and US leaders.

Source:ABC News (Live)、Gulf News、Hong Kong 01

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

NVIDIA’s financial results have largely allayed immediate concerns that ‘demand has peaked’: revenue exceeded expectations by approximately US$7 billion, and fourth-quarter guidance—excluding revenue from China—still surpassed market expectations by around US$5 billion, whilst data centre revenue doubled year-on-year. The relatively modest after-hours rise of around 2 per cent to 3 per cent suggests that market concerns have shifted from earnings to governance and policy: whilst Jensen Huang is willing to return, the board is reportedly opposed; investigations by the SEC and Congress are ongoing; and new export regulations will also affect business operations in Southeast Asia and China. The decline in gross margin from approximately 75 per cent to between 71 and 72 per cent also indicates that rising costs, such as those for memory, are squeezing profit margins.

The market movements during the day reflected another layer of concern. The sharp fall in software shares indicates that Anthropic’s IPO is not merely a fundraising event; the market is beginning to reprice the potential for AI agents to replace traditional enterprise software. Coupled with overheated core retail and import prices, and a 10-year US Treasury yield above 5 per cent, growth stocks are facing pressure on both earnings and valuations. The divergence in the A-share and Hong Kong stock markets follows a similar logic: capital is shifting towards sectors with a high degree of certainty regarding domestic substitution, such as semiconductor equipment and materials, whilst shunning application-side sectors whose valuations rely heavily on the AI narrative. Whether today’s (18 November) post-market sentiment surrounding Nvidia carries over into the Asian markets will serve as the first indicator for assessing the AI industry chain.

Checklist:

  • Whether NVIDIA can hold onto its after-hours gains during regular trading hours on Wednesday, and the board’s official response to the comments made by Jensen Huang
  • Whether Anthropic’s final offering price on 18 November will fall within the $70–$78 range, and how the share price performs on its first day of trading on 19 November
  • Will the sell-off in enterprise software shares intensify, and will companies such as Salesforce and ServiceNow manage to stabilise?
  • Reactions in the A-share computing power and optical module sectors to Nvidia’s earnings report; LPR rate announced on 20 November
  • Xi Jinping’s keynote speech at the APEC CEO Summit, the contents of the ‘Shenzhen Initiative’, and whether the trilateral meeting between China, the US and Russia on the 19th will take place as scheduled
  • Remarks by Federal Reserve officials: have expectations of a rate rise in December been further reinforced?
