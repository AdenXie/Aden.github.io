Daily Briefing | 13 November 2026: Jensen Huang steps down as NVIDIA CEO; CFO Kress takes over on an interim basis; 10-year US Treasury yields rise above 5.1 per cent; US shares close slightly lower; Trump states “there is no need to reach an agreement with Iran”; the Ministry of Commerce states that China and the US are moving forward with the “30 billion for 30 billion” tariff reduction arrangement

Today's main theme

This edition covers the evening of 11 November (Wednesday) through to 12 November (Thursday). Within a week, Nvidia’s ‘spy scandal’ had evolved from a personal scandal into a management shake-up: on Thursday morning, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as President, CEO and director, with Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress taking over as interim CEO whilst the company began the formal search for a new CEO. NVIDIA’s share price fell by as much as 13 per cent during trading and closed down 4.34 per cent, with only a few days remaining until its earnings report on 17 November. On the same day, US Treasury yields continued to rise, with the 10-year yield closing at around 5.10 per cent and touching 5.12 per cent during trading; demand at the 30-year Treasury auction was relatively weak; the three major stock indices closed slightly lower, with small-cap stocks falling more sharply. In the Middle East, Mr Trump stated on social media that the US “does not need to reach an agreement with Iran”; Vice President Vance, who had been in charge of communications with Iran, was reassigned to handle domestic affairs; and following further explosions targeting oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, oil prices reversed their earlier decline and rose. Domestically, the Ministry of Commerce stated that China and the US are maintaining close communication regarding the implementation of the ‘30 billion to 30 billion’ reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, whilst expressing serious concern over the ban on 5G equipment from Chinese companies imposed by certain EU member states. A-shares rose before falling back on Thursday, with the ChiNext Index dropping by 2.44 per cent and more than 4,300 stocks across the market recording losses.

Key News

1. Huang Renxun has stepped down as CEO and from the board of directors at NVIDIA; CFO Kress has been appointed interim CEO

NVIDIA announced on Thursday 12 November that co-founder Jensen Huang had resigned from his positions as President, CEO and board member, with immediate effect, bringing his 33-year tenure to an end. According to TechCrunch, regulatory filings submitted by the company show that the board accepted his resignation on Wednesday 11 November; Colette Kress, Chief Financial Officer and Executive Vice President, will serve as interim CEO until a permanent successor is found, whilst continuing to serve as CFO; Harvey Jones, who has served on the board since 1993, has been appointed as NVIDIA’s first-ever Chairman of the Board. In a statement, the board described Huang Renxun’s contribution to NVIDIA as “profound” and thanked him for his “inestimable contributions” to the company and the industry. The company did not disclose the reasons for his resignation.

The resignation came just four days after Huang Renxun admitted to an extramarital affair on 8 November. According to Al Jazeera, one of the parties involved was Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive believed by US intelligence officials to have links to Chinese intelligence agencies; The New York Times subsequently reported that he had also been involved in a personal relationship with Kimberly Powell, vice-president of medical business, who reported directly to him, raising concerns about a conflict of interest; according to Yahoo Finance, Reuters reported on Thursday a third relationship involving another NVIDIA employee. These remain allegations. Reuters had reported on Wednesday that the board of directors had met to discuss whether to remove him from his post. The board had previously stated that there was no reason to believe that national security or corporate secrets had been compromised, nor was there currently any evidence to suggest that Huang Renxun was knowingly assisting Beijing. Kress has served as NVIDIA’s CFO since September 2013, having previously held finance roles at Texas Instruments, Microsoft and Cisco.

According to Yahoo Finance, Nvidia fell by around 13 per cent at one point during Thursday’s trading session, breaking below the $164 level—a threshold it had held twice over the past 14 months—before closing down 4.34 per cent at $166.19. Its market capitalisation fell below $4 trillion, with the share price having declined by approximately 30 per cent over the past month. Analysts’ reactions were markedly divided: Bernstein assigned a ‘market perform’ rating with a target price of $200, stating that “it is difficult to make a case for buying at this point”; Oppenheimer downgraded its rating and removed its price target; Dan Ives of Wedbush lowered his price target from $300 to $225, but considered the valuation “too cheap” based on a price-to-earnings ratio of around 10 times 2028 earnings. Trump suggested on social media that Tim Cook, Apple’s former CEO and current Executive Chairman, should take over, describing it as “a legend succeeding a legend”; however, there are currently no signs that Cook intends to take on the role. Democratic Senators Warren and Kunes have called on the government to “preserve all records”. This matter also involves Anthropic’s IPO: according to Yahoo Finance, Nvidia has committed to subscribing for $10 billion in Anthropic’s $26 billion private placement; Anthropic is expected to set its price on 18 November and begin trading on 19 November. Key points to watch: the earnings report and management conference call on 17 November; whether the closed-door congressional briefing on 18 November will proceed as scheduled; and the board’s choice of successor.

Source:TechCrunch、Al Jazeera、Yahoo Finance (NVIDIA Live Stream)、Yahoo Finance (Market Close)、ABC News

2. The yield on 10-year US Treasuries rose above 5.1 per cent; demand at the 30-year auction was subdued; US shares closed slightly lower

Thursday marked the first day of trading in the bond market following Veterans Day, with yields continuing their upward trend following the release of the CPI figures. According to Yahoo Finance, the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note rose by approximately 10 basis points to 5.10 per cent, having reached an intraday high of 5.12 per cent; the yield on the 30-year note rose to 5.44 per cent during the session. According to TokenPost, the bid-to-cover ratio for that day’s $25 billion auction of 30-year Treasury bonds was 2.401, lower than the previous auction’s 2.542 and the average of 2.437 over the last six auctions; during the auction, the 10-year yield rose above 5.10 per cent. Goolsbee, President of the Chicago Federal Reserve, stated that inflation was at a “volatile moment”, prompting traders to bet further on a Fed rate rise in December.

The labour market has shown no signs of cooling. According to Yahoo Finance, in the week ending 7 November, the number of initial claims for unemployment benefits rose by 9,000 to 200,000, slightly higher than the expected 195,000, but still at a low level; the number of continuing claims fell by around 20,000 to approximately 1.7 million.

US shares closed slightly lower: According to Yahoo Finance, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by 189.40 points, or 0.36 per cent, to close at 52,297.09; the S&P 500 fell by 26.98 points, or 0.35 per cent, to close at 7,592.40; the Nasdaq fell by 100.10 points, or 0.39 per cent, to close at 25,366.09; the Russell 2000, which is more sensitive to interest rates, fell by 1.66 per cent; the VIX rose by 6.98 per cent to 18.71. AI cloud companies, which had led the rebound the previous day, weakened, with Nebius falling alongside other AI stocks in pre-market trading. According to documents filed by Nebius with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the company’s third-quarter revenue stood at US$980.8 million, a year-on-year increase of 571 per cent, with adjusted EBITDA of US$465.8 million and a net loss from continuing operations of US$37.5 million; capital expenditure for the quarter amounted to approximately US$7.3 billion, whilst the company raised US$5.75 billion through the issuance of convertible bonds during the same period. In other asset markets, gold fell by 3.07 per cent to $3,877.80 per ounce, whilst Bitcoin dropped by 3.71 per cent to approximately $71,500; Strategy fell by as much as 6 per cent following MSCI’s decision to exclude it from two US indices. According to Bloomberg (as reported by Yahoo Finance), merger talks between SpaceX and Tesla are making progress, with an announcement possible as early as December. Key points to watch: forthcoming inflation data, comments from Federal Reserve officials on the scale of the December rate rise, and whether the 10-year yield can stabilise above 5 per cent.

Source:Yahoo Finance (Market Close)、TokenPost、Nebius (US SEC Form 6-K)

3. Trump stated that “there is no need to reach an agreement with Iran”; another oil tanker was bombed in the Strait of Hormuz, causing oil prices to rebound from their earlier decline

According to the UAE’s *The National*, Trump wrote on Truth Social on 12 November: “We do not need a deal with Iran; those days are long gone.” He said the US had seized “hundreds of billions of dollars” of Iranian funds and “also controls their straits”. He also stated that Vice-President Vance, who had previously been involved in communications with Iran, would now turn his “full attention” to so-called issues of corruption and crime in Democratic-governed states such as Minnesota, California and Illinois. This stands in stark contrast to his remarks from a few weeks earlier, when he had suggested that “the war would end after the mid-term elections” and that “a deal was still possible”; On Tuesday, he also stated that further military action in the near future was “unlikely”, but that the US remained “on high alert”.

The maritime conflict continues to escalate. According to Reuters (as reported by Yahoo News), the Iranian military stated that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz: The Revolutionary Guard Navy said that two supertankers lost power on Wednesday after ignoring warnings and sailing on “unauthorised routes”, whilst the Iranian Army stated that two others struck a mine and caught fire on Thursday afternoon in the eastern part of the Strait. The UK Maritime Trade Operations Office, however, reported that on Wednesday a vessel was struck by an unidentified object approximately 14 nautical miles north-west of Ras Al Khaimah in the United Arab Emirates, causing a fire in the engine room; all crew members had been evacuated. The agency has repeatedly stated that the vessel was struck by an unidentified object rather than a mine. Reuters, citing data from the Washington-based Institute for Near East Policy, reported that at least 10 vessels were attacked in the first 10 days of November, compared with 37 incidents in October – the highest monthly total since hostilities began in late February. According to Breitbart News, citing Iran’s Fars News Agency, the Revolutionary Guards also claimed to have struck navigation facilities on Oman’s Dhidamal Island in the Strait; however, a senior Omani official told Maritime Executive that the island had not sustained any damage.

Oil prices reversed course to rise on the day. According to *El Nacional*, prices had previously fallen after Saudi Arabia increased production to near pre-war levels, but the losses were wiped out following Trump’s tweet. According to aNews, a subsidiary of Turkey’s Anadolu Agency, at 16:45 GMT, Brent crude rose by 1.1 per cent to $93.80 per barrel, though it was still down 4.9 per cent for the week; European natural gas prices rose by 6.7 per cent to €92.5 per megawatt hour. According to Yahoo Finance quotes, the WTI December contract closed at $88.90 per barrel, up 2.74 per cent. Key points to watch going forward include whether the White House will formally abandon the negotiation route, whether Qatar’s mediation efforts will be suspended, as well as developments regarding the attacks in the Strait and changes in oil tanker insurance premiums.

Source:*The National*、Reuters (reprinted by Yahoo News)、Breitbart News、aNews、Yahoo Finance

4. Ministry of Commerce: China and the US are in close communication regarding the ‘30 billion for 30 billion’ tariff reduction arrangement; China has expressed serious concern over the ban on Chinese 5G equipment in certain EU countries

He Yadong, spokesperson for the Ministry of Commerce, stated at the regular press conference on 12 November that China and the US are maintaining close communication on issues of mutual concern, such as trade and investment, and that any progress will be announced in due course. According to China News Service, he said that the ‘30 billion for 30 billion’ reciprocal tariff reduction framework would help stabilise and expand bilateral trade between China and the US, and that the business communities of both countries had high expectations for this. China hopes that the US will move in the same direction to ensure the arrangements are implemented as soon as possible, thereby creating more favourable conditions for agricultural trade between the two countries. He also mentioned that since May 2025, the two sides have held eight rounds of economic and trade consultations, and that China has fully implemented the consensus reached in previous consultations, adding that “cooperation is the only correct path”. The press conference did not disclose the specific list of goods covered by this arrangement or its effective date, nor did the report specify the currency in which the amounts were denominated.

At the same press conference, He Yadong responded to the move by some EU countries to ban equipment from Chinese companies. According to Xinhua News Agency, he said that the Ministry of Commerce had expressed serious concerns to the governments of the countries concerned, 5G is a key piece of infrastructure for the development of the global digital economy, and its construction and operation should respect market principles, technological neutrality and international rules. China firmly opposes the politicisation of technical issues and the over-emphasis on security concerns, and urges the countries concerned to rectify their erroneous practices and provide Chinese enterprises with a fair, impartial and non-discriminatory business environment. Xinhua did not name any specific countries. In recent years, the European Union has been discussing legislation requiring member states to phase out Huawei and ZTE equipment.

There were also two policy announcements in China on the same day. According to East Money, citing a notice from the National Development and Reform Commission, domestic petrol and diesel prices will be reduced by 645 yuan and 625 yuan per metric tonne respectively from 24:00 on 12 November, with the average price of 92-octane petrol falling by 0.51 yuan per litre. This reflects the impact of the sharp decline in international oil prices over the past two weeks. The State Council held its 22nd thematic study session that day, during which Premier Li Qiang emphasised the need to enhance the long-term sustainability of the social security fund, implement nationwide coordination of the basic old-age insurance scheme for enterprise employees, and advance provincial-level coordination of basic medical insurance. Key points to watch going forward include: the timing and scope of goods covered by the US-China tariff reduction arrangements; the schedule for the next round of economic and trade consultations; and progress on the EU’s legislation regarding telecommunications equipment from China.

Source:China News Network、China.com、Xinhua News、Sohu (reprinted from Xinhua News Agency)、East Money

5. A-shares rose before falling back: the ChiNext Index fell by 2.44 per cent, with turnover across both markets totalling 1.93 trillion yuan, and over 4,300 individual stocks declining

According to the 21st Century Business Herald, on 12 November, the three major A-share indices rose sharply in early trading before plummeting in the afternoon: the Shanghai Composite Index fell by 0.67 per cent to close at 3,655.83 points; the Shenzhen Component Index fell by 1.68 per cent to close at 11,197.98 points; the ChiNext Index fell by 2.44 per cent to close at 2,509.43 points; whilst the STAR 50 Index bucked the trend, rising by 0.73 per cent to close at 1,309.84 points. Trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.93 trillion yuan, a significant increase from the previous day’s figure of approximately 1.68 trillion yuan; 838 stocks rose whilst 4,320 fell, with 37 hitting the daily upper limit and six hitting the daily lower limit.

There was significant divergence across sectors. Defence-related sectors such as ground military equipment and naval equipment, as well as fisheries, forestry and semiconductors, led the gains; whilst precious metals, digital media, film and cinema chains, and electric motors led the losses. The top five stocks by turnover were, in order: Zhongji Xuchuang (33.62 billion yuan), New Ease (25.67 billion yuan), Cambricon (20.17 billion yuan), Dongshan Precision (18.26 billion yuan) and Changxin Technology (17.79 billion yuan), with capital continuing to concentrate on optical modules and domestically produced computing power. The weakness in precious metals was consistent with the day’s fall in gold prices, whilst the strength in the defence sector may be linked to renewed tensions in the Middle East.

At the close of trading on the A-share market, Nvidia had not yet announced Jensen Huang’s resignation, nor had US Treasury yields risen above 5.1 per cent. The sharp decline on heavy trading volume suggests that, following the previous day’s rebound, some investors chose to take profits, whilst the counter-trend gains in the STAR 50 and semiconductor sectors reflect the market’s preference for the ‘domestic substitution’ narrative. Key points to watch: How the A-share market will react today to the management changes at NVIDIA and the rise in US Treasury yields; the volatility of stocks in the NVIDIA supply chain, such as optical module manufacturers; and whether trading turnover can remain above 1.8 trillion yuan.

Source:21st Century Business Herald (Market Close)、21st Century Business Herald

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

On Thursday, the market faced two distinct risks. The first was interest rates: the yield on 10-year US Treasuries rose above 5.1 per cent, demand at the 30-year auction was weak, and initial jobless claims remained around 200,000, all of which suggest that the case for a Fed rate rise in December is strengthening; interest-rate-sensitive small-cap stocks and high-valuation growth stocks are under the greatest pressure. The second is corporate governance risk relating to a single company: Nvidia is one of the most heavily weighted companies in the S&P 500 and a key supplier in the AI capital expenditure chain. The sudden departure of its CEO could affect the market’s valuation of the company, as well as its strategic investments in firms such as Anthropic and the scrutiny in Washington regarding its exports to China. However, the index fell only slightly on the day, suggesting that the market is currently viewing this as an issue specific to Nvidia itself, rather than a problem with AI demand; the true test will come with the earnings report and guidance on 17 November.

Oil prices have once again become a key variable. Trump’s abandonment of the ‘agreement’ narrative, coupled with an increase in attacks in the Strait, may see a partial recovery of the energy risk premium that fell this week; this would push up inflation expectations, compounding the rise in US Treasury yields. On the domestic front, the recent adjustment to refined oil product prices reflects the previous decline in oil prices; should international oil prices rise again, the direction of the next price adjustment may shift. Should the US-China tariff reduction arrangements be implemented, this would be positive for agricultural trade and related sectors, though the timetable remains unclear at present.
