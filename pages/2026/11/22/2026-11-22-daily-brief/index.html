Daily Briefing | 22 November 2026: Nvidia’s accounting issues become the focus of an SEC investigation; shares fall 23 per cent in a single week—the worst performance since 2008; the Nasdaq drops by around 10 per cent over the week; Trump says he will impose ‘huge taxes’ on companies building data centres overseas; Stamp duty on securities transactions rose by 54.1 per cent from January to October, whilst land transfer revenue fell by 25.8 per cent; China and Singapore launch negotiations on Version 4.0 of their Free Trade Agreement

Today's main theme

This edition covers the period from the close of US markets on Friday 20 November to Saturday 21 November; some content was published on 20 November, Beijing time. Although there was no trading over the weekend, the week’s results have been tallied: the Nasdaq fell by approximately 10 per cent over the week, whilst the S&P 500 dropped by 8.6 per cent – its steepest weekly decline since April 2025. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average have now entered a technical correction phase. NVIDIA fell by 23 per cent over the week, its worst week since 2008; Reuters further reported that the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is focusing its investigation on NVIDIA’s accounting practices. At a White House press conference on Friday, Trump said he would impose “huge taxes” on US companies building data centres overseas, adding that he hoped Jensen Huang would “apologise and go home”. In China, the Ministry of Finance released fiscal data for January to October: stamp duty on securities transactions rose by 54.1 per cent year-on-year, whilst revenue from the transfer of state-owned land use rights fell by 25.8 per cent – two figures that paint contrasting pictures. On the front of opening up to the outside world, the Ministry of Commerce signed a memorandum of understanding with Singapore to launch negotiations on upgrading the China–Singapore Free Trade Agreement to Version 4.0.

Key News

1. Reuters: Nvidia’s accounting practices are the focus of an SEC investigation; its share price fell by 23 per cent in a single week, marking its worst performance since 2008

According to Reuters (as reported by the *Daily Star* on 21 November), two sources familiar with the matter said that SEC enforcement officials are examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia. The Wall Street Journal had already reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating Nvidia, though the focus of the investigation was unclear at the time; Reuters has not confirmed when the investigation began or exactly what matters it concerns. The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond. The investigation itself does not imply that the regulator has determined any violations have occurred.

There are numerous clues to the background of this story. According to Reuters, US authorities have been investigating since 2024 whether the Singapore-based company Megaspeed—once a major client of Nvidia—has been shipping Nvidia’s AI chips to China; the US Department of Justice is also scrutinising Nvidia’s US$12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face. Last year, Nvidia changed the basis for calculating regional revenue from billing address to the location of the customer’s headquarters; as a result, Singapore disappeared from the revenue breakdown, although the Financial Times had previously reported that sales in Singapore accounted for nearly half of its global revenue. According to Business Today, it is currently unclear whether this change in reporting methodology falls within the scope of the SEC’s review; three US House of Representatives committees have scheduled closed-door hearings with Jensen Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter for early December, whilst NVIDIA’s 10-Q quarterly report has been postponed to “the coming weeks” pending review by an independent board committee. Interim CEO and former CFO Corette Kress said during Tuesday’s earnings call that the company would be more selective regarding AI-related financing transactions.

As for the share price, according to Investing.com, Nvidia closed at US$125.53 on Friday, down US$20.45 or 14 per cent; it had already fallen by around 7 per cent before the Reuters report was published; It fell by 23 per cent over the week, marking its worst weekly performance since 2008, with the share price now approximately 48 per cent below its record high of 6 October. All this came on the heels of a very strong set of financial results: third-quarter revenue of US$115.8 billion, double the figure from the previous year; data centre revenue grew by 110 per cent to US$107.6 billion; and fourth-quarter revenue guidance of approximately US$130 billion. Key points to watch: whether Nvidia will issue a statement regarding the investigation, when the 10-Q will be filed, and the arrangements for the congressional hearing in early December.

Source:The Daily Star (Reuters)、Investing.com、Business Today

2. Weekly US stock market review: The Nasdaq fell by around 10% over the week, whilst the S&P 500 recorded its seventh consecutive weekly decline; next week will see the release of PCE inflation data, and the market will be closed for Thanksgiving.

According to Coinpaper, citing data from the Associated Press, in the week ending 20 November, the Nasdaq fell by 2,518.54 points, a decline of approximately 10 per cent, marking its worst week since March 2020; the S&P 500 fell by 8.6 per cent, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by 8 per cent – both representing their largest weekly declines since April 2025; the Russell 2000 fell by 7.5 per cent. The S&P 500 has now fallen for seven consecutive trading days, marking its longest losing streak since February 2020; it is approximately 11.6 per cent below its record closing high on 6 October, whilst the Dow Jones Industrial Average is also more than 10 per cent below its previous high; both indices have entered a technical correction phase. According to Journal Arta, the S&P 500 has retreated by 11.9 per cent, although figures vary slightly depending on the source.

Next week is Thanksgiving week; US stock markets will be closed on Thursday 26 November and will close early on Friday, but there is no shortage of economic data. According to the Journal Arta, the market will see the release of the PCE price index—which the Federal Reserve regards as its most important indicator (Wall Street expects core PCE to rise by 0.29 per cent month-on-month) , October’s personal income and expenditure figures, durable goods orders, the revised third-quarter GDP figure (the preliminary reading was 3.4 per cent growth), the Federal Reserve’s Beige Book, S&P Global’s preliminary November PMI and the Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index. On the earnings front, reports are due from Dell (expected Q3 adjusted earnings per share of $6.62 and revenue of $49.32 billion), HP Enterprise, HP, Analog Devices, Semtech, as well as retailers such as Best Buy, Dollar Tree, Kohl’s and Deere.

Regarding Anthropic, according to FourWeekMBA, the issuer has stated that four ETFs—two 2x leveraged and two 2x inverse—tracking Anthropic are expected to begin trading on Monday 23 November, although this date is still subject to change; during the first two trading days following the listing, ANT saw a cumulative trading volume of approximately 1.26 billion shares, exceeding the number of shares offered in the IPO. The timetable for the lifting of restrictions on up to 303 million restricted shares on 15 December remains unchanged. Key points to watch going forward: whether the PCE data will alter market expectations regarding a rate rise in December, and whether AI server orders from Dell and HP Enterprise can stabilise sentiment within the hardware supply chain.

Source:Coinpaper、Journal Arta、FourWeekMBA

3. Trump: Will impose ‘huge taxes’ on US companies that build data centres overseas; hopes Jensen Huang will ‘apologise and go home’

According to the transcript of the White House press conference on 20 November (compiled by SingjuPost), Trump said: “We will not allow companies to build data centres in other countries.” He subsequently added that companies could do so, “but we will impose huge taxes on them”, and “we want them to build them here”. According to Tom’s Hardware, citing Axios, Trump also said that “China will take them all away”, and that he had not yet discussed this proposal with his cabinet or advisers. It should be noted that, under the US Constitution, the power to levy taxes rests with Congress; this statement is currently merely a verbal remark and does not constitute a concrete proposal.

At the same press conference, according to the transcript and a summary by Tonghuashun Financial Breakfast, Trump said he hoped Jensen Huang would “apologise properly and then get back to work”. He also stated that the US stock market had lost 4 trillion US dollars since the election because “the world is unhappy with those who won the election”. He described APEC as a “highly successful economic summit”, stating that China, the US and Russia had formally agreed that “a nuclear conflict must never be allowed to occur”, that China and the US had agreed to establish certain “joint safeguards”, and that they would jointly increase the production of crude oil, petrol and diesel.

On 21 November, according to CBS News (Associated Press), Trump stated on a Fox News programme that he was willing to work with the incoming Democratic Congress to set a cap on credit card interest rates, saying, “I would do that”. A common proposal is a 10 per cent cap, whilst a survey by the New York Federal Reserve in August showed that the median credit card interest rate was approximately 22.2 per cent. Democratic Senator Warren, who is expected to become chair of the Senate Banking Committee, has expressed her support. Points to watch: whether the ‘hefty tax’ on overseas data centres will become a formal proposal, and the impact of a cap on credit card interest rates on banks and card issuers.

Source:SingjuPost (Transcript of the press conference)、Tom’s Hardware、Tonghuashun Finance、CBS News

4. Ministry of Finance: From January to October, stamp duty on securities transactions rose by 54.1 per cent, personal income tax rose by 22.9 per cent, and revenue from land sales fell by 25.8 per cent

According to data released by the Ministry of Finance on 20 November and republished by the 21st Century Business Herald, national general public budget revenue for January to October stood at 19,567.1 billion yuan, representing a year-on-year increase of 4.9 per cent; of this, tax revenue amounted to 16,262.2 billion yuan, up 6 per cent, whilst non-tax revenue totalled 3,304.9 billion yuan, down 0.2 per cent. Expenditure under the general public budget totalled 22,804.2 billion yuan, up by 1 per cent.

There are significant differences between tax categories. Stamp duty on securities transactions totalled 251.1 billion yuan, up 54.1 per cent year-on-year, reflecting brisk trading in A-shares during the first few quarters of this year; personal income tax amounted to 1,642.8 billion yuan, an increase of 22.9 per cent, with The Paper reporting an 89.6 per cent rise in October alone. Value-added tax and consumption tax on imported goods rose by 13.2 per cent; CCTV.com cited experts as attributing this to robust foreign trade and imports related to artificial intelligence. Meanwhile, property-related taxes continued to decline: deed tax fell by 14.9 per cent, land appreciation tax by 16.6 per cent, and domestic consumption tax by 3.3 per cent.

The pressure on government fund revenue is even more pronounced. From January to October, national government fund budget revenue totalled 2,912.3 billion yuan, down 15.5 per cent; of this, local-level revenue fell by 20.4 per cent, whilst revenue from the transfer of state-owned land use rights stood at 1,852.6 billion yuan, down 25.8 per cent. On the expenditure side, spending on social security and employment rose by 4.8 per cent, whilst expenditure on health and healthcare increased by 7.8 per cent (including childcare subsidies); however, expenditure on energy conservation and environmental protection, transport, and agriculture, forestry and water resources all fell year-on-year. With land-related fiscal revenue contracting and spending prioritising people’s livelihoods, coupled with the recent decline in trading volumes on the A-share market, the strong growth in stamp duty is likely to slow towards the end of the year. Key points to watch: whether additional fiscal instruments will be introduced before the end of the year, and the arrangements for next year’s deficit and special-purpose bonds to be announced at the Central Economic Work Conference, which is typically held in December.

Source:21st Century Business Herald、The Paper、Guangming Online (CCTV.com)

5. China and Singapore have signed a memorandum of understanding to launch negotiations on upgrading the Free Trade Agreement to Version 4.0, with a focus on rules governing government procurement and digital trade

According to the Ministry of Commerce website (as reported by The Paper), on 20 November, Minister of Commerce Wang Wentao and Singapore’s Deputy Prime Minister and Minister for Trade and Industry Gan Kim Yong signed the ‘Memorandum of Understanding on the Launch of Negotiations to Upgrade the China–Singapore Free Trade Agreement to Version 4.0’. The two sides agreed to conduct negotiations on areas of mutual interest, such as government procurement and digital-related trade and economic rules, building upon the China–ASEAN Free Trade Area, the RCEP, and the existing China–Singapore Free Trade Agreement and its Upgrade Protocol. The China–Singapore FTA was signed in October 2008 and has been upgraded twice, in November 2018 and December 2023.

This memorandum was signed the day after the conclusion of the APEC Shenzhen meeting. Whilst the APEC ‘Shenzhen Outlook’ made no mention of the WTO, the Chinese side repeatedly emphasised the multilateral trading system and the Asia-Pacific Free Trade Area during the meeting; against a backdrop of limited progress at the multilateral level, China continues to advance openness through bilateral and regional agreements. Government procurement and digital trade rules are both core chapters of high-standard agreements (such as the CPTPP and DEPA); the fact that China and New Zealand are negotiating these two areas first can also be seen as a pilot project for China to align with high-standard rules.

Meanwhile, the US took a largely positive view of APEC. According to the transcript of the White House press conference, Mr Trump described APEC as “very successful” and said that Mr Putin had “made it clear that he wishes to end the Russia-Ukraine war through diplomatic means”. However, the trilateral declaration made no mention of semiconductors or tariffs, and the US-China tariff truce is still set to expire on 10 January next year. Key points to watch going forward include the schedule for the China-US negotiations, as well as the progress of talks between the two countries before the tariff truce expires.

Source:The Paper (Ministry of Commerce)、FX168、SingjuPost (Transcript of the press conference)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Most of the weekend’s news served to provide further context for last week’s sharp fall. A Reuters report stating that the SEC was scrutinising Nvidia’s accounting practices shifted market concerns from ‘whether AI demand can be sustained’ to ‘whether the revenue figures are credible’—an issue that is far more difficult to resolve with a single set of strong financial results; NVIDIA’s 23 per cent drop over the week and the Nasdaq’s fall of around 10 per cent indicate that the market is reassessing the risk premium across the entire AI sector. Should Trump’s proposal to impose taxes on overseas data centres materialise, it would directly impact the overseas expansion plans of cloud providers such as Microsoft, Google and Amazon, whilst also introducing uncertainty for data centre projects in the Middle East and South-East Asia; however, as there is as yet no concrete plan for this idea, the market is more likely to treat it as merely additional policy noise.

The key focus next week will be on interest rates. If the PCE data comes in on the high side, expectations of a Fed rate rise in December will solidify, which would be detrimental to technology stocks that are already under pressure; if the data comes in on the low side, coupled with a sharp fall in the stock market, market bets on a pause in rate rises will continue to gain momentum. In China, fiscal data for January–October shows that revenue from land sales continues to decline, with the fiscal budget relying more heavily on tax revenue and adjustments to social welfare expenditure; the strong growth in stamp duty stems from brisk trading in previous quarters, whilst the recent decline in trading volume on the A-share market and Friday’s sharp fall on heavy volume are likely to cause this revenue stream to slow towards the end of the year. The negotiations to upgrade the China-Singapore Free Trade Agreement have limited direct impact on the market, but they demonstrate that China is continuing to advance regional opening-up beyond the context of Sino-US relations.

Checklist:

  • NVIDIA’s response to the SEC investigation, the filing date for the 10-Q, and congressional hearings in early December
  • Next week: PCE, revised GDP figures, durable goods orders and the Beige Book; the market is pricing in the Monetary Policy Committee meeting on 9 December
  • AI server orders and guidance in Dell and HPE’s financial reports
  • Trading in the Anthropic leveraged ETF following its listing on Monday: will ANT hold its $83 issue price?
  • Have there been any concrete legislative moves regarding the ‘hefty tax’ on overseas data centres and the cap on credit card interest rates?
  • Will the A-share market hold the 3,500-point mark on Monday, and will there be any additional fiscal measures or reserve requirement ratio cuts before the end of the year?
  • Arrangements for the first round of negotiations on the China–US Free Trade Agreement 4.0, and developments regarding the extension of the US–China tariff truce
