Daily Briefing | 3 November 2026: Repurchase and share buyback programmes converted to long-term arrangements; interest rates cut to 1 per cent; A-shares regain the 3,600-point mark; oil prices fall by over 7 per cent in a single day; Apple posts record revenue; Marvell to acquire Lumentum for US$115 billion

Today's main theme

This issue covers Monday, 2 November. The three-department meeting previewed at the weekend has yielded results: share buybacks and additional lending have been transformed from a temporary measure into a long-term institutional arrangement, with the interest rate reduced from 1.25 per cent to 1 per cent; swap facilities resumed operations on 3 November, with 31 securities firms and fund management companies having already applied for over 100 billion yuan. A-shares rose across the board that day, with the Shanghai Composite Index regaining the 3,600-point mark; trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.8 trillion yuan, with securities firms leading the gains. Overseas, Iranian negotiators left Doha with a draft agreement, which the market interpreted as a sign of détente; crude oil prices fell by more than 7 per cent in a single day, with Brent briefly dipping below US$105. Tech stocks, however, remained under pressure: Oracle launched a US$10 billion share issue and a US$10 billion bond issue on the same day to finance AI data centres; Marvell announced an all-share acquisition of optical communications firm Lumentum for a total transaction value of approximately US$115 billion; the Nasdaq fell by 1.07 per cent, whilst the Dow Jones Industrial Average actually rose, buoyed by the fall in oil prices. Apple released its first earnings report since CEO Tenus took office after the market closed, with revenue of US$114.5 billion setting a record for the period, though revenue from Greater China fell short of expectations. Today marks the US mid-term elections, and the Reserve Bank of Australia is also set to announce its interest rate decision this afternoon Sydney time.

Key News

1. The repurchase and additional lending facilities have been converted into a long-term institutional arrangement, with the interest rate reduced to 1 per cent; the swap facility will resume on 3 November.

According to the 21st Century Business Herald, the People’s Bank of China, the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the China Securities Regulatory Commission convened a symposium on 2 November regarding swap facilities for securities, fund and insurance companies, as well as a symposium on re-lending for share buybacks and share purchases. Zou Lan, Deputy Governor of the People’s Bank of China; Cong Lin, Deputy Director-General of the National Financial Regulatory Administration; and Liu Haoling, Vice-Chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission, attended the event, with 23 securities and fund companies and 26 banks taking part, alongside heads of relevant departments from the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council.

The meeting outlined two specific adjustments. Firstly, the re-lending facility for share buybacks and share purchases has been transformed from a temporary measure into a long-term institutional arrangement; the re-lending rate has been reduced from 1.25 per cent to 1 per cent, with financial institutions required to apply market-based mark-ups on top of this rate and increase lending. To date, the total value of agreements signed stands at approximately 440 billion yuan, with cumulative disbursements exceeding 280 billion yuan. When this instrument was introduced in 2024, the interest rate stood at 1.75 per cent; it has since been cut twice, and this marks the third rate cut. Secondly, the swap facility: with a current outstanding balance of 105 billion yuan, the central bank will expand the range of participating institutions, appropriately reduce swap rates, and optimise the collateral and operational models. According to the 21st Century Business Herald, the central bank will conduct a one-year, renewable swap facility operation on 3 November. Thirty-one securities and fund companies have already submitted applications, with the total amount requested exceeding 100 billion yuan, significantly higher than the 50 billion yuan in October 2024 and the 55 billion yuan in January 2025.

This comes against the backdrop of a sharp correction in the A-share market in October, with both the ChiNext Index and the STAR Market Composite Index falling by more than 20 per cent over the month, whilst the October Manufacturing PMI also fell back to 49.3 per cent. Experts quoted by Sina Finance believe that long-term arrangements can reduce the risk premium arising from policy uncertainty; others have cautioned, however, that the final cost of loans following an interest rate cut will still depend on the mark-ups applied by banks, and that policy can only provide a safety net—it cannot replace improvements in profitability and corporate governance.

The market reaction was immediate. According to the 21st Century Business Herald, on 2 November, the Shanghai Composite Index rose by 1.08 per cent to 3,638.03 points, regaining the 3,600-point mark; the Shenzhen Component Index rose by 1.63 per cent to 11,010.68 points; the ChiNext Index rose by 1.87 per cent to 2,458.7 points; trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.8 trillion yuan, an increase of more than 100 billion yuan compared with the previous trading day, with over 4,600 individual stocks rising. The securities sector led gains throughout the day, with East Money rising by more than 6 per cent. Key points to watch: the actual scale of the swap facility on 3 November, the spread applied to bank re-lending, and whether the inflow of new capital will enable technology and growth stocks to sustain their rebound.

Source:21st Century Business Herald (Conference)、21st Century Business Herald (Market)、21st Century Business Herald (Re-lending)、Sina Finance

2. Iranian negotiators left Doha with a draft agreement; crude oil prices fell by more than 7 per cent in a single day

According to Reuters (as reported by the Cyprus Mail), an official familiar with the negotiations said on 2 November that Iranian negotiators had left Doha on 1 November; their departure was not due to a deadlock in the talks, a draft agreement is currently being circulated, and the two sides may meet again. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Baghai stated that the negotiations were based on the seven-step plan drawn up by Iran in September, with the aim of reopening the Strait of Hormuz and ending the US naval blockade of Iran; Tehran proposed that implementation could be accelerated within the framework of the agreement; However, he emphasised that the final agreement could not be implemented, nor would the Strait be reopened, until the US lifted the blockade and Iran’s demands were met. He added that Iran did not believe the mid-term elections would ‘give rise to a new America’.

The US stance remains two-pronged. Trump had previously stated that he would not launch any new strikes before the election, and claimed that negotiations with Tehran were proving fruitful; according to Fox News, he said at a rally in Iowa on the evening of 1 November that “one day we’ll have to give them a proper whack”, adding that the war would end “very, very quickly”. Citing Axios, Fox reported that Trump told Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu he was inclined to allow the negotiations to continue for the time being; Netanyahu, however, suggested resuming strikes immediately after the election.

Oil prices reacted sharply. According to Marinelink, around midday Eastern Time, Brent crude fell by 7.47 per cent to $104.96, whilst WTI dropped by 7.2 per cent to $96.62; Yahoo Finance data showed the WTI December contract closing at $96.08, down 7.72 per cent. Last Friday, Brent was still trading near a six-month high of $118. Iranian President Pezeshchiyan stated that the Supreme Leader had granted the government “certain authority” to negotiate. Analysts remain cautious: Pavel Morchanov of Raymond James cautioned that there have been several “false dawns” in recent months; Goldman Sachs, meanwhile, stated that if Gulf output remains 3.5 million barrels per day below the 2025 average, Brent could rise to $140 in 2027. Key points to watch going forward include US decisions following the mid-term elections, whether Doha will arrange the next round of talks, and whether shipping through the Strait of Hormuz will truly resume.

Source:Reuters (reprinted by the Cyprus Mail)、Fox News、Marinelink、Yahoo Finance

3. Apple’s fourth-quarter revenue of US$114.5 billion set a record for the period, whilst revenue from Greater China fell short of expectations

Apple announced its results for the fourth quarter of the 2026 financial year (ending in September) after the close of US markets on 2 November; this was the first set of financial results since John Tenus took over as CEO on 1 September. According to iPhone in Canada and investingLive, revenue stood at approximately US$114.5 billion, representing year-on-year growth of around 12 per cent. This exceeded market expectations of approximately US$113.5 billion and also surpassed the company’s previous growth guidance of 9 per cent to 11 per cent; earnings per share were US$2.10, up 14 per cent year-on-year, compared with expectations of approximately US$2.00. iPhone revenue totalled US$57.6 billion, up 17 per cent; Mac revenue was US$10.8 billion, up 24 per cent; services revenue reached US$31.6 billion, up 10 per cent and a new all-time high; whilst iPad and wearables revenue fell by 10 per cent and 8 per cent respectively, both below expectations. The gross margin stood at 47.9 per cent, with approximately one percentage point attributable to tariff refunds. Full-year revenue for the 2026 financial year totalled approximately US$479 billion, a record high.

Revenue in Greater China stood at approximately US$17.5 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 21 per cent; however, according to investingLive, this fell short of market expectations of around US$18.8 billion, making it the most notable shortfall this quarter. Apple announced just last week that Alibaba’s Qianwen would be integrated into the Apple Store in China, launching in December alongside iOS 27.3; whether this will help boost sales in the Chinese market remains to be seen until the next quarter. Another variable is costs: according to iPhone in Canada, end-of-quarter inventory rose to US$14.5 billion from US$5.7 billion a year ago, whilst R&D expenditure increased by 32 per cent; the market had previously been widely concerned about the pressure on hardware gross margins caused by rising memory chip prices.

Apple’s share price fell by approximately 2.2 per cent on the day ahead of its earnings report. At the time of writing, there have been no reliable reports regarding the company’s guidance for the December quarter or the market’s final reaction to the share price in after-hours trading. Key points to watch: guidance on holiday season revenue and gross margin during the earnings call; management’s comments on memory costs and iPhone pricing; and whether Greater China will return to expected levels in the December quarter.

Source:iPhone in Canada、investingLive、MarketAlleys

4. Mawell acquires Lumentum in an all-share deal worth approximately US$115 billion; AI bottlenecks extend to optical interconnects

Marvell announced on 2 November that it would acquire the optical components company Lumentum in an all-share deal, with the total value of the transaction (including debt) amounting to approximately US$115 billion. According to The Motley Fool and BigGo Finance, each Lumentum share will be exchanged for 4.5 Marvell shares, equivalent to approximately US$1,132 per share, representing a premium of around 23.9 per cent over the closing price on 28 October (the last trading day before the rumours emerged); Upon completion of the transaction, Marvell shareholders will hold approximately 67 per cent of the combined company, whilst Lumentum shareholders will hold approximately 33 per cent. The boards of both companies have unanimously approved the deal, which is subject to shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to be completed in the first half of 2028. Mawell’s CEO, Matt Murphy, will continue to serve as Chairman and CEO, whilst three representatives from Lumentum, including CEO Heston, will join the board.

Lumentum manufactures lasers and optical components that connect AI chips in data centres; its revenue for the most recent quarter stood at US$1.26 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 136.5 per cent. Mawell states that the combined companies’ addressable market is as high as US$550 billion, with annual cost savings expected to exceed US$1 billion and adjusted earnings per share set to increase in the first year following completion of the transaction. This deal allows Marvell to expand from custom AI chips into optical communications, placing it in more direct competition with Broadcom, and also illustrates that bottlenecks in AI data centres are shifting from computing chips to data transmission.

Investors are adopting a cautious stance. According to BigGo Finance, Mawell’s share price has fallen by approximately 22 per cent since news of the negotiations emerged in late October; Bernstein considers the direction to be reasonable but the synergies unclear, whilst Morningstar is concerned that Mawell’s bid may be too high following Lumentum’s sharp rise. According to TheStreet, during trading on 2 November, Mawell fell by approximately 5.3 per cent, whilst Lumentum fell by approximately 10.3 per cent; MarketScreener data shows that Lumentum closed at US$684.40, down 18.59 per cent. Key developments to watch: shareholder votes at both companies, antitrust reviews in various countries (including China’s market regulators), and a revaluation of the optical module supply chain.

Source:The Motley Fool、BigGo Finance、MarketScreener、TheStreet

5. Oracle issued US$10 billion in shares and US$10 billion in bonds on the same day; the Nasdaq fell by 1.07 per cent

According to CNBC and The Next Web, on 2 November, Oracle launched both a US$10 billion share issue and a US$10 billion investment-grade bond issue to finance the construction of AI data centres. The bond issue comprises six tranches, including a floating-rate bond maturing in 2029 and five fixed-rate bonds maturing in 2029, 2031, 2033, 2037 and 2046, led by Bank of America Securities; the two offerings are independent of one another. The proceeds are designated for general corporate purposes, which may include capital expenditure, debt repayment, acquisitions, dividends and share buybacks.

This issue marks the implementation of the plan announced at Oracle’s Investor Day on 28 October: the company stated at the time that it would raise up to US$20 billion in new capital market financing by the end of the 2030 financial year, split equally between equity and debt, with CFO Maxon describing this as “the final tranche required”. According to The Next Web, Oracle invested US$55.7 billion in data centres during the financial year ending May 2026, primarily to serve AI clients such as OpenAI; as at 31 August, its total liabilities stood at approximately US$236.1 billion, of which US$125.3 billion comprised senior unsecured debt; The company expects free cash flow to return to positive territory only in the second half of the 2028 financial year. The share price fell by approximately 5 per cent that day.

US equities showed a mixed performance overall. According to Yahoo Finance, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.41 per cent to 51,983.98 points, the S&P 500 fell 0.51 per cent to 7,541.40 points, and the Nasdaq fell 1.07 per cent to 25,164.32 points; the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note fell to around 4.89 per cent, whilst gold rose by 1.47 per cent to US$3,956.20 per ounce. Falling oil prices drove gains in airline and consumer stocks; according to TheStreet, United Airlines rose by more than 7 per cent during the session, whilst Amazon rebounded by around 4 per cent following a positive rating from Morgan Stanley; technology stocks continued to be weighed down by concerns over AI funding and valuations. Key points to watch: the discount applied to Oracle’s IPO pricing, the uptake of the bond offering, and whether other cloud providers will follow suit with equity financings.

Source:CNBC、The Next Web、Yahoo Finance、TheStreet

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Looking at several news items together, the A-share and US stock markets moved in opposite directions on the first day of November. A-shares were directly driven by policy: the extension of the re-lending programme, interest rate cuts, and the resumption and expansion of the swap facility are all providing the market with additional liquidity, with securities firms being the most direct beneficiaries. However, the main reasons for the October decline were the overseas AI valuation correction and weak domestic demand; following the policy support, the market will still need to be judged on its fundamentals. As for US equities, the sharp fall in oil prices has eased concerns over inflation and interest rate rises, benefiting airlines, consumer stocks and cyclical shares; however, Oracle’s simultaneous share and bond issuance, Marvell’s high-priced acquisition and Apple’s underperformance in Greater China have kept technology stocks under pressure, with the debate surrounding AI investment having shifted from ‘is there sufficient demand?’ to ‘where will the money come from and when will returns materialise?’. Mawell’s acquisition of Lumentum serves as a valuation benchmark for the optical module sector on the A-share market and may, in the short term, spark discussions regarding concentration among overseas clients.

Oil prices are the biggest variable this week. A single draft document caused crude oil prices to fall by more than 7 per cent in a single day, indicating that the market has already factored in a substantial war premium; should negotiations hit a snag, prices are likely to rebound swiftly. The outcome of today’s US mid-term elections will have a direct impact on post-election military decisions regarding Iran. The Reserve Bank of Australia is due to announce its interest rate decision today; the four major banks had previously unanimously forecast a 25-basis-point rate rise to 4.85 per cent. It will be worth watching whether the fall in oil prices prompts the RBA to adopt a more dovish tone in its statement.

Checklist:

  • The scale of swap facility operations on 3 November, and whether the rebound in A-shares will continue and whether trading volume can be maintained at around 1.8 trillion yuan
  • Apple’s December quarterly guidance from its earnings call and the after-hours share price reaction
  • The results of the US mid-term elections, as well as post-election military decisions regarding Iran and the next round of talks in Doha
  • Will Brent crude hold above $105?
  • RBA Interest Rate Decision and the Movement of the Australian Dollar
  • Oracle’s IPO pricing, and the performance of Mawell, Lumentum and the A-share optical module sector

Source:21st Century Business Herald、Yahoo Finance
