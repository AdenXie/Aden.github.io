The answer to the two test cases: demand is still there, but the bills have arrived too

Introduction: This is the sixth instalment in the AI Capital Expenditure series. The first instalment‘AI: The Mainstay of Capital Expenditure’A discussion on how computing infrastructure drives macroeconomic growth in the US; Part Two‘In the same week, macro and micro perspectives delivered conflicting verdicts on AI capital expenditure’The dichotomy between describing GDP as ‘very strong’ and financial results as ‘too expensive’; Part Three‘The Political Calculus of AI Capital Expenditure in the Wake of the Mid-Term Elections’Assessing the impact of political variables at state level; Part Four‘Pricing Three Times a Week’Treat the 11–17 financial results and the 11–18 briefing as ‘the first and second halves of the same event’; Part Five‘Jensen Huang Resigns’It is said that what NVIDIA has lost is its political patron.In that post, we set out two specific conditions for a pullback this week. They have now all been met.

0. This time, we set the exam papers ourselves.

At the end of Part V, we set out two clear test conditions:

  1. 17 November: NVIDIA Q3: Data centre results meet expectations and gross margin holds steady at 74% → ‘Sustained’ correction to 40 per cent
  2. 11–19 Anthropic IPO: If the share price falls below the issue price on the first day (dropping below $70) → ‘Breakdown’ followed by a further upward revision

When they were written, they were conditional sentences. Now they have become school reports.

This series has undergone numerous revisions.—S-1: the article that overturned its own valuation model; the one on the split verdict that publicly corrected the ‘AI accounts for half the growth’ claim in the load-bearing column; and the one by Huang Renxun in which he admitted to ‘predicting the turning point but not the scenario’.However, this is the first time that ‘specific thresholds have been set out in advance and assessed on a case-by-case basis upon expiry’. We set the thresholds ourselves, and we also determine the criteria, so there isn’t much room for manoeuvre in this article.

Results:I passed both questions. Neither of them felt like a comfortable pass.

Figure 1Decision cards for two test points. On the left is Anthropic (priced at $83, closed at $97.17 on the first day, did not breach $70; evidence of demand is confirmed), on the right is NVIDIA (data centre revenue of 107.6 billion, non-GAAP gross margin of 74.9 per cent; both criteria met, but Q4 gross margin guidance has been lowered to 71–72 per cent); at the bottom are the scenario iterations: 35/45/20 → 42/40/18.

I. Key Point 1: Anthropic has not traded below its IPO price, and has in fact risen amidst a falling market

Let’s look at the figures first

Ruling: There was no breach; the claim is substantiated. Our prediction of ‘a break above $70 → a sharp upward revision’ did not materialise.

However, if one were to write only this sentence, it would oversimplify the matter.

Three points that must be made clear

Anthropic currently has three ‘market capitalisations’ in existence, depending on the denominator:

Calibre Number of shares Priced at $83 Corresponding to a closing price of $97.17
A · Shares in circulation 13.087 billion shares(Class A 7.428 billion + N 5.659 billion) Approx. $1.09 trillion Approx. $1.27 trillion
B · Fully diluted Approx. 18 billion shares(including potential ordinary shares such as options and RSUs) Approx. $1.5 trillion Approx. $1.75–1.8 trillion
Source of the shortfall Approx. 4.9 billion shares — —

For the purposes of this article, the main figures are consistently expressed in terms of A (shares in free float). Whenever fully diluted figures are cited, the basis must always be stated in brackets. This is not a matter of nit-picking—there is a 65 per cent difference between 1.09 trillion and 1.8 trillion; if readers are unaware of the denominator, they may be led in two different directions by the same set of facts.

Just as an aside: of that shortfall of 4.9 billion shares, 3.86 billion were set aside for the 2021 share scheme alone.

Figure 2Anthropic’s three market capitalisation metrics. Above is the price trajectory on the first day ($83 → $111.50 → $115.10 → $97.17 → next day’s snapshot $87.65); below is a comparison of the three methodologies: 13.087 billion shares in circulation correspond to $1.09 trillion / $1.27 trillion; fully diluted at approximately 18 billion shares corresponds to $1.5 trillion / $1.75–1.8 trillion. The difference between the two metrics is approximately 4.9 billion shares, representing potential ordinary shares.

The downside: it rose during a bear market

19 November, the day of the listing,The Nasdaq fell by 2.3 per cent, whilst the Dow Jones fell by 1,101 points。

In other words,This share rose by 17 per cent against the trend in a falling market. There are two sides to this detail:

  • The positive side: The demand is genuine. Achieving more than six times oversubscription (according to Reuters, citing Bloomberg) at a time when overall risk appetite is contracting, and a 17 per cent gain on the first day – this wasn’t driven by the broader market.
  • The downside: The -9.8 per cent the following day illustrates how short-lived the buzz is.In a market with no inflow of new capital, the first-day gains of new listings are often the result of short-term reallocation of existing capital, rather than long-term investment by new entrants.

In our previous article, we used the phrase ‘offset price declines with volume’. This week’s developments require us to refine this statement: it is now ‘offset price declines with volume + increase volume’. Not only did it accept a valuation lower than the rumoured $2 trillion ($1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis), but it alsoSold 19 per cent more shares(825 million → 980 million shares).

This is a textbook example of strong demand coinciding with undervalued prices. The two are not mutually exclusive: buyers are willing to purchase, but will only buy more if a larger discount is offered.

The political manoeuvring began even before the listing

A point in time that is easily overlooked:Senator Warren wrote to Dario Amodei on 27 October… more than three weeks ahead of the IPO. The letter pointed out that the company itself had acknowledged that AI could pose ‘catastrophic or existential risks’, and warned that ‘entering the public market on an unprecedented scale could cause market-wide volatility on the very first day of trading’.

She also singled out one particular issue:Index providers have abolished the waiting period for new shares, enabling newly listed companies to be included in pension accounts more quickly.

This tip paid off on 19 November: Rapid inclusion in the Nasdaq 100 (assessed at the close of the seventh trading day; a company need only rank amongst the top 40 by market capitalisation to qualify, with inclusion taking place approximately 15 trading days later; SpaceX set a precedent in July this year). On the same day, four senators from both parties introduced an AI disclosure bill requiring listed companies to disclose more AI-related information.

Therefore, ‘regulatory risk’ does not begin only after a company goes public, but is already on the agenda before the pricing stage.

Conclusion: Both statements are true. Data centre revenue of $107.6 billion significantly exceeded the consensus estimate of $101.87 billion; non-GAAP gross margin of 74.9 per cent was above the 74 per cent forecast. The pullback conditions we had set have been triggered.

There is one correction to be made: A figure of ‘73.6 per cent gross profit margin’ was once cited within the team; this was a misquote. The two official figures are respectively GAAP 74.8 per cent, non-GAAP 74.9 per cent。

A cause for concern: Q4 gross profit margins will take a hit

74.9 per cent is this season’s result. 71–72 per cent is the forecast for next season.

Management described the 71–72 per cent figure as ‘the bottom’ and stated that it would rebound to 72–73 per cent in FY2028. In other words, this is not a permanent decline, but ratherVera Rubin: The Phased Costs Arising from Product Structure and Costs During the Ramp-up Phase。

But we must do the maths properly:Revenue guidance has jumped from $115.8 billion to $130.0 billion (+12 per cent), whilst the gross margin has fallen from 74.9 per cent to 71–72 per cent (approximately -3 percentage points). This is a classic case of ‘sacrificing profit margins for growth’.

Figure 3On the left is a comparison of NVIDIA’s Q3 actual results and Q4 guidance (revenue: $115.844 billion → guidance: $130 billion; non-GAAP gross margin: 74.9% → guidance: 71–72%); On the right is the breakdown of the approximately $522 billion in financial commitments (purchase commitments of $390 billion, data centre leasing and cloud service commitments of $100 billion, and a financial guarantee for Neocloud of $32 billion); At the bottom are key points from Kress’s conference call regarding the increase in GPU rental fees, along with a clarification that “$2.5 trillion represents the backlog in the cloud industry, not orders specific to NVIDIA itself”.

This means, ‘If we hold out this season, we’ll have to pay the toll next season.’

Summarise the nature of this financial report in one sentence:It demonstrated the demand, whilst foreshadowing the cost.

It is true that four figures exceeded expectations, and it is also true that the Q4 guidance was higher than the consensus. However, the downward revision to the gross margin guidance indicates that:This round of growth does not come without a cost; it requires sacrificing profit margins on the product mix. Management says this is the bottom and that things will pick up next year—This is a promise that needs to be verified; it is not a fact that has already occurred.

The strongest piece of evidence is not the target price, but the rent

During the earnings call, Kress made a statement that carried more weight than any analyst’s target price (translation):

“Under multi-year agreements, Hopper GPUs have been re-contracted at prices of up to twice their original cost; rates for the B300 have risen by more than 60 per cent since its launch.”

This is evidence of price, not evidence of expectations. This does not prove that ‘people still want to buy’, but rather that ‘existing contracts are not only being renewed, but are being renewed at higher prices’.

This also explains why the assertion that ‘demand is alive’ holds water:If demand is artificial, rents cannot possibly double.

One point that must be clarified

The conference call will cover $2.5 trillionThis figure.

This is the ‘cloud industry backlog’, which stood at $2 trillion three months ago. It is not Nvidia’s own order backlog. If this qualifier were omitted, the entire argument would become a colossal error—presenting the entire industry’s order book as a single company’s revenue guarantee.

Circular Financing: From Qualitative to Quantitative

During the conference call, Kress disclosed three measures of the company’s financial commitments:

Project Amount
Procurement Commitment $390 billion（$390B）
Data Centre Leasing + Cloud Services Commitment Over $100 billion（$100B）
neocloud Financial Guarantee $32 billion（$32B）
Total Approximately $522 billion（$522B）

The key lies in the footnote on the last line: that $32 billion is explicitly ‘contingent on these companies being able to raise funds themselves’.

This statement transforms the label ‘revolving finance’ from a qualitative description into a quantifiable risk exposure.It and CoreWeave’s CDS spread are two ends of the same chain—The quality of some of Nvidia’s trade receivables depends on its customers’ ability to secure financing. Kress also stated that the company is “taking a very close look” at which companies to finance.

The board has put its foot down

The exact words of Harvey Jones, Chairman of the Board, during the conference call:

“NVIDIA’s board of directors unanimously accepted his resignation and has no intention of reinstating him as CEO.”

This is an official statement, not media speculation.The statement in the title of Part Five—‘There is no substitute for a political guarantor’—has now been confirmed at board level.

At the same time, the company has established a new“National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee”(comprising independent directors, external advisers and legal advisers), the succession search has engaged Heidrick & Struggles. Both of these moves point to the same thing:NVIDIA recognises that good governance is more important than any one individual, but it also knows that this takes time.

The market did not respond favourably

On the day following the release of its financial results (18 November), Nvidia’s share price fell 2.89%, with a cumulative fall of over 13%。

What is even more noteworthy is the structure:AMD and Broadcom rise by more than 3 per cent. Extending the timeframe to the week in which Jensen Huang resigned,AMD and Broadcom both rose by more than 18 per cent, Micron +13 per cent, Qualcomm and Intel around +5 per cent.

“NVIDIA’s crisis is a boon for its competitors.” This assessment is now backed up by data. The market is not ‘bearish on AI’, but rather“From a single company to several companies”。

III. Considering the two criteria together

Checkpoints The conditions we have set In fact determine
Anthropic Broke through $70 on the first day → Breakout and upward revision $83 offer price, $97.17 closing price on the first day It has not fallen below the issue price; demand is confirmed.
NVIDIA Data centres meet targets + gross profit margin held steady at 74 per cent $107.6 billion (exceeding consensus), 74.9 per cent (exceeding guidance) Both conditions are met

I’ve passed both questions. But if you put the two ‘discomforts’ side by side, you’ll see the same thing:

  • What is unsettling about Anthropic isValuation: Demand is strong, but this comes at the cost of lower pricing and a larger print run.
  • NVIDIA’s unease stems fromGross profit: Growth is strong, but it comes at the cost of a 3-point drop in the gross profit margin next quarter.

In other words: the evidence on the demand side has grown stronger, and the bills on the cost side have started to arrive. This is not a contradiction; it is two sides of the same coin—AI capital expenditure is shifting from ‘expansion at any cost’ to ‘cost-conscious expansion’。

IV. Scenario Iteration: 35/45/20 → 42/40/18

Scenario Part One This article On the basis of
Continuation: CAPEX continues; valuation recovery 35% 42% NVIDIA beats expectations on four fronts; Q4 revenue guidance exceeds consensus; Anthropic raises its pricing, expands its offering and gains 17 per cent on its first day of trading; GPU rental renewals double
slow down: Growth has slowed but has not collapsed 45% 40% Q4 gross margin guidance down by 3 percentage points; Nvidia falls by over 13 per cent in a week; CEO vacancy remains unresolved; export controls shift from a policy issue to an enforcement issue
Rupture: a precipitous fall in capital expenditure 20% 18% Evidence on the demand side has strengthened, so the forecast has been revised downwards; however, three unresolved issues are preventing it from returning to 15 per cent.

Why has the default rate only fallen to 18 per cent, rather than returning to the earlier level of 15 per cent?

BecauseStrong demand does not necessarily mean low risk, there are three matters still pending:

  1. There is still no list of successors. The board says it is considering candidates from both inside and outside the organisation, but to date there have been no reliable reports naming any specific candidates. The longer the vacancy remains, the more pressing the question becomes: ‘Who is responsible for providing guidance?’
  2. The scope of the DOJ is unknown. The Ministry of Justice and the Ministry of Commerce confirmed this week that Megaspeed is under investigation on suspicion of smuggling chips into China.Criminal investigation. The investigation has only just begun, and it remains unclear which transactions are involved or whether it will lead to the discovery of internal NVIDIA documents.
  3. Q4: The decline in gross profit margin is structural. Management says this is the trough of Rubin’s ramp-up phase and that it will rebound in FY2028—this is a promise, not a fact. If the figure actually falls below 71 per cent, the situation will change.

Compared with 35/45/20, the net changes in this iteration are ‘continuation +7, slowdown -5, rupture -2’.The demand side has been given full marks, but the risk side has not been deducted a single point.

V. Background: The political track is advancing in parallel

This section is included merely as background, as it merits a separate article.

Items Status

Senate Banking Committee hearing Scott said that both Huang and Lutnick had agreed to attend, and hoped this would take place ‘before Christmas’ (originally scheduled for 3 December for Huang and 10 December for Lutnick).

Two investigative reports in the NYT 18 November: Chips flowing to Alibaba and the Chinese military are within easy reach; 19 November: Nvidia has been asked to sell chips to companies with links to the military

Significance: Huang Renxun has gone from being a ‘policy adviser’ to a ‘person under investigation’, whilst Nvidia’s business in China has gone fromBusiness issuesbecomeLaw enforcement issues. Should the DOJ’s criminal investigation focus on specific transactions, Nvidia must update its disclosure of compliance risks in its 10-Q —This is the tail risk not factored into the pricing of the 11–17 financial results.

The red line remains unchanged: The Ministry of Justice stated that Megaspeed is currently the subject of a criminal investigation… This does not amount to a conclusion that Alice Huang is a spy. The two matters must be treated separately.

Macroeconomic Context: 11–19 Trump, Putin and Xi held trilateral talks in Shenzhen and issued the ‘Declaration of Peace’, which stated that nuclear war ‘must not and will never be waged’, called for an increase in oil production and supply, advocated for UN reform, and stipulated that the ICC ‘must not abuse its jurisdiction’.We have found no official link between this declaration and the export controls on semiconductors., so it is used merely as background information, without drawing any causal inferences.

VI. Preview of the Next Part

The hearings on 1 December and 3 December, together with the Lutnick line, will form the main subject of the next article.

The evidence is now complete: the Megaspeed investigation and why it was terminated; the Teter-Kessler phone call (presenting both the allegations and the defence side of the story); reports that Lutnick linked the easing of chip export restrictions to China with the ‘China deal’; and the divisions within the administration.

The hearing provided fresh ammunition—for the first time, statements were made under oath. This is also the only thread in this series that links ‘policy’ and ‘law enforcement’.

VII. Explanation of Data Gaps and Definitions

  1. Anthropic’s final number of shares issued and amount raised: The total issue size was $81.3 billion (including existing shares), whilst the company’s net proceeds amounted to $79.4 billion.Over-allotment option: 147 million shares remain unexercised, though the final figure may change again.
  2. ANT: $87.65 (-9.8%) the following day – a snapshot from the market page, not confirmed at the close.
  3. NVIDIA: The intraday low on 18 November and trading volume did not align; Reports confirm a closing price of -2.89 per cent.
  4. AMD and Broadcom’s 11–18 per cent rise is calculated using the conservative criterion of ‘an increase of more than 3 per cent’, figures from snapshots taken at second-level intervals were not included (as the timestamps differed between pages).
  5. NVIDIA’s financial commitments of approximately $522 billion were disclosed verbally during Kress’s conference call, the company has not published a breakdown of the figures.
  6. $2.5 trillion represents the cloud industry backlog…not due to a backlog of orders from Nvidia itself—this statement has been verified against the original transcript of the conference call.
  7. The claim that the gross profit margin was 73.6 per cent was a misrepresentation within the team, with the official figures standing at 74.8 per cent under GAAP and 74.9 per cent on a non-GAAP basis.
  8. There is no official documentation to support a direct link between the trilateral summit and chip policy, this paper does not draw any causal inferences.
  9. ‘Over-subscription of more than six times on the first day of trading’ is as reported by the media(Reuters, citing Bloomberg); the original Bloomberg article was not read directly.
