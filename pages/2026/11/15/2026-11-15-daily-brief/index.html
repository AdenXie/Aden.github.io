Daily Briefing | 15 November 2026: The US FAA suspended all flights nationwide for over four hours; more than 14,000 flights were cancelled over two days; Anthropic’s IPO was oversubscribed by approximately six times, with pricing set for 18 November; two ministries issued the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’ implementation plan for new energy storage; Europe has formed a negotiation team for Ukraine; and another oil tanker struck a mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz.

Today's main theme

This edition covers the period from 13 November (Friday) to 14 November (Saturday), with some background information dating back to earlier in the week. Although there was no trading over the weekend, several major events for next week were set in motion during these two days. In the US, the FAA grounded all flights nationwide on Friday afternoon due to a communications network failure; the grounding lasted for more than four hours, making it the longest since 9/11, with a total of over 14,000 flights cancelled across Friday and Saturday. Transport Secretary Duffy bluntly described the US air traffic control system as “incredibly outdated”. On the AI front, according to Bloomberg, subscriptions for Anthropic’s IPO have reached approximately six times the offering size. The company plans to set its price on 18 November and list on the Nasdaq on 19 November, slotting in just after Nvidia’s earnings report on 17 November. Domestically, the National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration have issued the ‘15th Five-Year Plan Implementation Scheme for New Energy Storage’, proposing that installed capacity for new energy storage reach 300 million kilowatts by 2030, and establishing a capacity-based pricing mechanism for grid-side independent energy storage. On the international political front, EU leaders in Dublin agreed that the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, together with EU institutions, would represent Europe in peace talks on Ukraine, whilst the US stated it was facilitating the largest prisoner exchange since the war began. In the Middle East, another oil tanker struck a mine and caught fire in the eastern part of the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, as the situation at sea continues to deteriorate.

Key News

1. The FAA has suspended flights nationwide for more than four hours, with a total of over 14,000 flights cancelled on Friday and Saturday

According to CBS News, the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) grounded all flights nationwide from 15:36 Eastern Time on Friday 13 November; the suspension was lifted at approximately 20:20, lasting just over four hours—the longest nationwide grounding since the 11 September 2001 attacks. According to FlightAware data, a total of approximately 9,159 flights were cancelled and around 7,190 were delayed on Friday; According to a live tally by Yahoo News, as of 14:48 Eastern Time on Saturday, more than 5,000 US flights had been cancelled that day, exceeding FAA Administrator Bedford’s estimate of 4,000 to 5,000. Of these, American Airlines cancelled 892, Delta 734, United 733 and Southwest 540, whilst JetBlue cancelled approximately 43 per cent of its flights.

The fault lay with the communications network. According to Yahoo News, Transport Minister Duffy said that whilst a remote FAA site was upgrading from copper to fibre-optic cables, a router began ‘flooding’ other routers, resulting in thousands of connection requests every four to five seconds, which disrupted communications with aircraft and radar; The project was carried out by L3 Harris, the company responsible for operating the network. Duffy said an investigation was underway to determine whether the issue stemmed from an “operational error” by the contractor or a equipment fault, adding that “those responsible will be held to account”. According to CBS, government sources indicated that the problem was linked to Cisco routers manufactured between 2001 and 2006; Secretary of Homeland Security Mullin stated that no signs of a cyberattack had been detected. Bedford said there was “no evidence of malicious behaviour”, and the FAA has disabled the relevant functions on approximately 60 routers of the same model, though it also acknowledged that the network “will continue to experience faults until it is replaced”. Daniels, chairman of the air traffic controllers’ union, said that communications had been interrupted for some aircraft, and air traffic controllers had diverted flights to frequencies that were still operational.

The impact of this incident extends beyond weekend travel. It has once again brought to the fore the issues of an ageing and underfunded air traffic control system in the US, and will also incur costs for airlines in terms of rebooking, compensation and crew scheduling, which may weigh somewhat on fourth-quarter results. The FAA expects flight schedules to return to normal by the end of Sunday. Key points to watch: the reaction of airline shares on Monday, the findings of the investigations by the FAA and L3 Harris, and whether Congress will use this as an opportunity to expedite funding for the modernisation of the air traffic control system.

Source:CBS News、Yahoo News (Live)

2. Anthropic’s IPO has seen demand of approximately six times the offering size; pricing is set for 18 November and the share listing for 19 November; NVIDIA’s subscription commitment is not included in the order book.

According to a Bloomberg report on 12 November, subscriptions for Anthropic’s IPO have reached approximately six times the number of shares on offer. According to the company’s amended prospectus, Anthropic plans to issue 825 million Class A shares at a price of between US$70 and US$78 per share, comprising 801.4 million new shares and 23.6 million shares being sold by existing shareholders. under the ticker symbol ANT, to be listed on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley acting as lead underwriters. According to The Private Banker, the pricing date has been set for 18 November (Wednesday), with trading commencing on 19 November; Based on the price range, the total proceeds are estimated at approximately US$57.75 billion to US$64.35 billion; the fully diluted valuation at the upper end of the range is approximately US$1.4 trillion, which is lower than the nearly US$2 trillion previously rumoured in the market, but higher than the US$965 billion from the private placement round in May. Approximately 20 per cent of the shares are being offered to retail investors, which is significantly higher than the 5 to 10 per cent typically allocated in large IPOs.

In addition, seven companies—NVIDIA, Google, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and an affiliate of TSMC—have committed to subscribing to a total of US$26 billion worth of non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price. This portion does not form part of the public offering and is not included in the order book. Following Jensen Huang’s resignation, there was some market speculation as to whether NVIDIA’s subscription commitment might change; however, there have been no reports to date that the company has revised its commitment. According to Yahoo Finance, Anthropic’s revenue for the nine months to 30 September stood at US$33.2 billion, compared with approximately US$2.5 billion in the same period last year; however, according to The Private Banker, the amended prospectus reveals a net loss of US$104 billion for the nine-month period, primarily attributable to changes in the fair value of convertible bonds and derivative liabilities.

The fact that the offering was oversubscribed by six times demonstrates that, despite management turmoil at Nvidia and the 10-year US Treasury yield exceeding 5 per cent, market demand for leading AI model companies remains strong. However, the prospectus makes no commitment to price at the upper end of the range, and a significant portion of the subscription multiples consists of large ‘anchor’ bids from institutional investors; the final allocation is therefore likely to be substantially reduced. Key points to watch: whether NVIDIA’s earnings report on 17 November will affect pricing sentiment; whether the final issue price will exceed the upper end of the range; and whether the stock’s performance on its first trading day on 19 November will divert capital away from other AI stocks.

Source:Bloomberg、The Private Banker、Yahoo Finance

3. Two departments issue the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’ implementation plan for new energy storage: 300 million kilowatts of installed capacity by 2030, and the establishment of a capacity-based tariff on the grid side

According to Xinhua News Agency, on 13 November, the National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration issued the ‘Implementation Plan for New Energy Storage during the 15th Five-Year Plan’, which proposes that by 2030, the national installed capacity of new energy storage will reach 300 million kilowatts, with an average storage duration of approximately three hours, and that the industry will transition from a phase of large-scale development to a phase of market-driven development. During the 14th Five-Year Plan period, installed capacity for new energy storage increased from 3 million kilowatts at the end of the 13th Five-Year Plan to 136 million kilowatts, with grid-side installations accounting for over 50 per cent of the total. Based on these figures, the 15th Five-Year Plan will require an additional increase of approximately 164 million kilowatts, more than doubling the current capacity.

The proposal focuses on revenue mechanisms. Xinhua News Agency reported that the plan proposes establishing a capacity-based tariff mechanism for grid-side independent new energy storage under a list-based management system, refining the charging and discharging pricing mechanism, dynamically adjusting peak-off-peak time-of-use tariffs according to supply and demand, and encouraging industrial and commercial users as well as industrial parks to build energy storage facilities. Currently, the main sources of revenue for energy storage come from capacity revenues, electricity market revenues and frequency regulation ancillary service revenues. A spokesperson from the National Energy Administration stated that during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, the increase in new energy storage for on-site consumption within provinces will be primarily grid-side, focusing on ensuring electricity supply, integrating renewable energy and maintaining grid safety and stability. The target of 300 million kilowatts must be aligned with the market’s absorption capacity and the sustainability of the compensation mechanism.

New energy storage projects have long faced the problem of unstable revenue streams. The capacity tariff effectively provides a fixed income for grid-side energy storage, which helps to improve project returns; however, the list-based management system also means that not all projects will be eligible. For the industry chain, this proposal offers a relatively clear outlook on demand for the next five years. Key points to watch: the detailed rules and project lists for capacity-based tariffs in each province; the market reaction of energy storage-related sectors on Monday; and the October national economic data to be released by the State Council Information Office on 16 November.

Source:Xinhua News、21st Century Business Herald (Full text of the proposal)、21st Century Business Herald (Press Conference Preview)

4. The EU has confirmed that five countries and EU institutions will take part in the Ukraine peace talks, whilst the US says it is pushing for the largest prisoner exchange since the war began

According to Radio Television Éireann (RTÉ), EU leaders held an informal meeting in Dublin on 12–13 November, during which European Council President Costa announced that Europe would be represented by a team comprising the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, alongside EU institutions, at the next round of Ukraine peace talks, rather than appointing a single special envoy. President Zelenskyy and US Special Envoy Kushner stated that the European delegation would take part in the next round of talks in Abu Dhabi next week. von der Leyen said that the European Commission would shortly propose the 22nd round of sanctions against Russia and present a plan to bridge Ukraine’s 2027 funding shortfall before the next European Council meeting; According to RTÉ, the funding shortfall for 2027 is estimated at around 70 billion euros, with the Czech Republic, Hungary, Slovakia and Bulgaria refusing to contribute. German Chancellor Merz told the US: “Please do not spring any surprises.”

The US, Russia and Ukraine had previously resumed negotiations. According to the Public Broadcasting Service (PBS), delegations from the three sides held talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November—the first such meeting since the Geneva talks in February. Whilst no major breakthrough was achieved, they agreed in principle to a large-scale prisoner exchange. According to *The Moscow Times*, citing Reuters, Kushner said on 13 November that the US was pushing for “the largest prisoner exchange since the war began”, which could reunite thousands of people with their families; Special Envoy Witkov stated that Putin had agreed to allow Europeans to participate in the next round of talks. However, Kremlin spokesman Peskov stated that whether Europe would be included depended on whether it was “genuinely willing” to advance the peace process. The two sides remain deeply divided on territorial issues: Russia controls approximately one-fifth of Ukraine’s territory, whilst Kyiv refuses to cede the remaining parts of the Donbas region.

Europe’s entry into the negotiations signifies that the talks are shifting from a bilateral issue between the US and Russia to broader post-war European security arrangements, though this will also complicate the agenda. For the markets, progress in the Russia-Ukraine negotiations will primarily affect European gas prices, the euro and European defence shares. Key points to watch: the agenda for next week’s Abu Dhabi talks and the actual role of the European delegation; the specific numbers and timing of prisoner exchanges; and discussions on funding for Ukraine at the EU summit on 26–27 November.

Source:RTÉ、PBS、The Moscow Times (Reuters)

5. Another oil tanker has struck a mine and caught fire in the eastern part of the Strait of Hormuz; the Revolutionary Guards claim to have struck a US military radar installation in Bahrain

According to the Associated Press (as reported by Yahoo News), Iranian state media stated that on the morning of Saturday 14 November, an oil tanker struck a mine and caught fire in the eastern part of the Strait of Hormuz; the name of the vessel and details of any casualties have not been disclosed. According to a live broadcast by Al Jazeera, the Iranian Navy stated that this was the fourth such incident within 24 hours and accused the vessel of entering a no-sail zone; the United Nations called for an end to lethal attacks on ships. The Associated Press also reported that the Iranian Revolutionary Guard claimed to have destroyed a US early-warning radar system in Bahrain on Friday in retaliation for a US strike on a communications facility at the Iranian port of Sirik. These claims could not be independently verified, and neither US Central Command nor the Bahrain Ministry of Information responded to requests for comment.

On the diplomatic front, Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement in Istanbul on 13 November, pledging to adopt a unified stance should either party come under external armed attack. According to Al Jazeera, this agreement is seen as a deterrent. On the other hand, the International Energy Agency had stated just the day before that Gulf exports were recovering and that the oil market would see a surplus in the fourth quarter; yet attacks in the straits continue to rise, creating a stark contrast between the two developments.

Following the release of the IEA report on Friday, Brent crude was trading at around $95 per barrel, whilst WTI closed at around $89; the impact of weekend events will not be reflected in oil prices until the market opens on Monday. Should mine-striking incidents continue to occur frequently, tanker insurance premiums and freight rates may rise again, offsetting the downward pressure on oil prices caused by the IEA’s assessment of a supply surplus. Key points to watch: whether the US will respond to the incidents at Sirik Port and the radar incident; changes in the number of attacks in the Strait; and whether Brent can hold above $95 on Monday.

Source:Associated Press (reprinted by Yahoo News)、Al Jazeera (live)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

The weekend’s news has packed next week’s schedule: China’s October economic data on Monday, Nvidia’s earnings report on Tuesday, Anthropic’s pricing on Wednesday, and Anthropic’s IPO on Thursday. The AI sector is facing a set of conflicting signals. Anthropic’s oversubscription of approximately six times indicates that investors remain interested in leading model companies, but with its funding round amounting to tens of billions of dollars, the market is concerned that it may divert capital away from Nvidia, AMD and ‘new cloud’ companies; NVIDIA’s earnings report and guidance, released following management turmoil, will in turn influence sentiment regarding Anthropic’s pricing. With the two events occurring just one day apart, volatility is likely to intensify.

The direct impact of the FAA’s grounding order on the market is primarily felt in aviation shares and airlines’ fourth-quarter costs; however, what warrants greater attention is the issue of ageing infrastructure that it highlights, which may subsequently become a matter for congressional funding and relevant contractors. Domestic energy storage solutions represent a medium- to long-term positive for the new energy industry chain; the key lies in whether the detailed rules on capacity-based electricity tariffs in each province can be implemented. On the geopolitical front, the expansion of Russia-Ukraine negotiations to Europe is a sign of de-escalation, whilst the mine strike incident in the Strait of Hormuz signals rising tensions; this may lead to opposing pressures on European natural gas and oil prices.

Checklist:

  • On 16 November, the State Council Information Office released the national economic data for October: will industrial output, consumption and investment continue the trend seen in the first three quarters?
  • NVIDIA’s 17 November earnings report and interim CEO Kress’s first conference call
  • Will Anthropic’s final share price on 18 November be above the $70–$78 range, and how will the share perform on its first day of trading on 19 November?
  • Reactions in the aviation sector on Monday, and the findings of the investigations by the FAA and L3 Harris
  • The energy storage sector’s response to the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’ and the pace at which provincial capacity-based tariff guidelines are being introduced
  • The agenda for next week’s Russia–Ukraine peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the scale of the prisoner exchange, the attack in the Strait of Hormuz and the price of Brent crude
