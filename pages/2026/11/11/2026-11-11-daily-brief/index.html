Daily Briefing | 11 November 2026: US October CPI rises 0.4 per cent month-on-month, exceeding expectations; probability of a December rate hike rises to around 90 per cent, Anthropic sets IPO price range with a valuation of up to US$1.4 trillion; CoreWeave reports 168 per cent revenue growth in Q3 and raises guidance; China’s October exports rise by 35 per cent year-on-year, with the trade surplus for the year exceeding US$1 trillion; Trump postpones new strikes against Iran

Today's main theme

This edition covers Tuesday, 10 November. The previous day’s rebound in US equities failed to hold, driven by inflation data: the US CPI rose by 0.4 per cent month-on-month in October, whilst the core CPI rose by 0.3 per cent, both 0.1 percentage points higher than expected. This was the final CPI report ahead of the Federal Reserve’s meeting on 8–9 December. The yield on the 2-year US Treasury note rose by approximately 19 basis points in a single day, whilst the 10-year yield approached 5 per cent. The market’s pricing for a 25-basis-point rate rise in December rose to around 90 per cent, and Federal Reserve Governor Waller also indicated that a 50-basis-point rate hike could be considered. Technology stocks led the decline, with Nvidia falling a further 5.5 per cent. On the same day, Anthropic announced its IPO price range; at the upper end, it would raise approximately US$64.4 billion with a valuation of around US$1.4 trillion, lower than the US$2 trillion previously rumoured in the market. After the close, CoreWeave released its third-quarter results showing revenue growth of 168 per cent and an upward revision to its full-year guidance, though interest expenses also rose significantly. Domestically, customs data showed that exports in October rose by 35.2 per cent year-on-year, whilst the trade surplus for the first ten months of the year had already exceeded US$1 trillion. In the Middle East, Trump stated that, at the request of Gulf states, he would temporarily suspend new strikes against Iran, whilst maintaining the naval blockade; Qatar reported progress in negotiations, whilst Iran denied that talks were taking place between the two sides. A-shares rose slightly that day, with the ChiNext Index up 1.46 per cent, whilst the oil and gas and coal sectors weakened.

Key News

1. US CPI rose by 0.4 per cent month-on-month in October, whilst the core CPI rose by 0.3 per cent, both exceeding expectations; the probability of a rate rise in December has risen to around 90 per cent

The US Bureau of Labour Statistics announced on 10 November that the seasonally adjusted Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose by 0.4 per cent month-on-month in October, compared with 0.6 per cent in September; the core CPI, excluding food and energy, rose by 0.3 per cent month-on-month. According to Yahoo Finance, market expectations stood at 0.3 per cent and 0.2 per cent respectively. The report did not include year-on-year CPI figures: the Bureau of Labour Statistics explained that, as survey data was not collected during the government shutdown in October 2025, any index lacking data for October 2025 could not be used to calculate the 12-month change; only a few items, such as petrol, had year-on-year figures, with petrol rising by 37.4 per cent year-on-year (unadjusted for seasonal variations).

Looking at the breakdown, food prices rose by 0.5 per cent month-on-month, with prices for food consumed at home up by 0.4 per cent and those for eating out up by 0.5 per cent; energy prices rose by 0.7 per cent, a marked slowdown from the 5.2 per cent recorded in September, with electricity prices up by 0.9 per cent and petrol prices up by 1.0 per cent. Housing rose by 0.3 per cent, with owner-equivalent rent up 0.3 per cent and rent for primary residences up 0.2 per cent. Hospital services, recreation and motor insurance rose by 0.8 per cent, 0.8 per cent and 0.7 per cent respectively; second-hand cars fell by 1.2 per cent. According to Yahoo Finance, Kevin Gordon of Charles Schwab commented that “the October CPI was not great”, noting that the breadth of inflation across goods and services remains uneven.

The market’s reaction centred on interest rates. According to Kiplinger, the yield on the 2-year US Treasury note rose by 18.9 basis points to 4.736 per cent, the 10-year yield rose by 12.6 basis points to 4.988 per cent, and the 30-year yield rose to 5.349 per cent—all reaching their highest levels since late October; CME FedWatch indicates that the probability of a 25-basis-point rate rise in December stands at approximately 90 per cent, whilst the probability of a 50-basis-point rise is around 10 per cent. Federal Reserve Governor Waller stated that a 50-basis-point rate rise “should be considered”; According to TheStreet, he would support a rate rise of at least 25 basis points in December and would consider a larger increase if core inflation continues to rise. US equities closed lower across the board: according to Yahoo Finance, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 157.86 points, or 0.30 per cent, to close at 52,231.89; the S&P 500 fell 0.38 per cent to 7,520.93 points; and the Nasdaq fell 0.74 per cent to 25,065.88 points. NVIDIA closed down 5.47 per cent at US$168.89, marking the second consecutive day it was the worst-performing component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average; according to TheStreet, a shareholder lawsuit alleges that the company and Jensen Huang misled investors regarding personal matters. Gold fell by 1.87 per cent to $4,037.6 per ounce. Key developments to watch: the PCE price index due to be released at the end of the month, and comments from Federal Reserve officials on the scale of interest rate rises ahead of the December meeting.

Source:U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics、Yahoo Finance (CPI)、Yahoo Finance (Market Close)、Kiplinger、TheStreet

2. Anthropic announces IPO price range: US$70 to US$78 per share, with a valuation of up to approximately US$1.4 trillion

According to *Fortune* and Al Jazeera, Anthropic submitted an updated prospectus on 10 November, planning to issue 825 million shares on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ‘ANT’, with a price range of US$70 to US$78 per share. At the upper end of the range, the offering would raise approximately US$64.4 billion, resulting in a fully diluted valuation of around US$1.4 trillion. In addition, seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, have agreed to subscribe for $26 billion worth of non-voting shares at the IPO price. The roadshow began on Tuesday and, according to *Fortune*, trading could commence as early as next Wednesday or Thursday.

This valuation is lower than the US$2 trillion cited in some media reports a few weeks ago, and also lower than the US$1.5 trillion previously discussed by investors; it is directly linked to the fall in AI share prices since last week. By way of comparison, SpaceX sought a valuation of approximately US$1.75 trillion when it went public in June this year. Business figures remain strong: according to Al Jazeera, Anthropic’s third-quarter revenue stood at approximately US$17 billion, compared with US$1.4 billion a year earlier; according to *Fortune*, annualised revenue at the end of October was approximately US$81 billion, up from around US$71 billion in late September. The company’s computing power commitments from partners such as Amazon and Google total nearly $900 billion. *Fortune* also noted that the updated prospectus states the company’s internal estimates suggest it may be “on the verge” of reaching the threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy regarding a significant acceleration in AI progress, though no model has yet crossed that threshold.

According to Kiplinger, in addition to rising US Treasury yields, news of Anthropic’s IPO was also one of the reasons behind the pressure on tech shares on Tuesday. An IPO worth tens of billions of dollars could see some capital flowing out of existing AI stocks. Key points to watch going forward include the subscription multiple during the roadshow, where the final price will land within the range, and the stock’s performance on its first day of trading.

Source:*Fortune*、Al Jazeera、Kiplinger

3. CoreWeave’s third-quarter revenue rose by 168 per cent; the company has raised its full-year guidance, whilst interest expense rose to US$846 million

CoreWeave released its third-quarter results after the close of US trading on 10 November. According to financial data compiled by 24/7 Wall St. and Quantisnow, third-quarter revenue stood at US$3.661 billion, up 168 per cent year-on-year and exceeding market expectations of approximately US$3.54 billion; The loss per share of US$1.27 was better than expected, but the GAAP net loss widened to US$709 million from US$110 million a year earlier. As at 30 September, the revenue backlog stood at approximately US$132.5 billion. The company raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to US$13.0 billion to US$13.3 billion, with full-year capital expenditure guidance set at US$37.0 billion to US$39.0 billion; the lower end of this range was also raised, as infrastructure was being delivered to customers faster than planned. CEO Intratato described it as a “breakthrough quarter” and noted that the adjusted operating margin had reached double digits a quarter ahead of schedule.

Market concerns over debt are also reflected in the figures. According to Quantisnow, net interest expense in the third quarter stood at US$846 million, compared with US$310 million a year earlier, and has already exceeded the operating profit for the quarter; the adjusted operating margin was 10 per cent, down from 16 per cent a year earlier. During the quarter, the company issued approximately US$4.2 billion in convertible bonds and established a ‘market-price offering’ programme for up to 35 million Class A shares, which could dilute existing shareholders. According to 24/7 Wall St., Interato also mentioned that approximately US$9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits are expected to be received over the next three months, which would alleviate some of the financial pressure. Ahead of the earnings release, CoreWeave’s share price had already fallen by approximately 42 per cent from its peak.

This financial report addresses the market’s concerns following Microsoft’s decision last week to scale back its computing power lease with Oracle: demand has not weakened, and the order backlog continues to grow; however, the faster the company expands, the higher its interest and financing costs become. Key points to watch: CoreWeave’s share price reaction on Wednesday, management’s comments on renewal pricing and the utilisation rate of older-generation GPUs, and Nvidia’s financial results in late November.

Source:24/7 Wall St.、Quantisnow、investingLive

4. China’s exports in October rose by 35.2 per cent year-on-year, with the trade surplus for the first ten months exceeding US$1 trillion

According to the Associated Press, China’s customs authorities announced on 10 November that, in US dollar terms, exports in October rose by 35.2 per cent year-on-year, whilst imports increased by 41.6 per cent year-on-year; the trade surplus for the month stood at US$105.3 billion, down from US$133.5 billion in September. The trade surplus in goods for the period from January to October reached US$1.04 trillion, exceeding US$1 trillion for the second consecutive year, with two months still remaining until the end of the year.

Looking at individual markets, exports to the US in October rose by 32.9 per cent year-on-year; the Associated Press noted that this was based on a low base caused by last year’s tariff shock; the trade surplus with the US for the first ten months stood at approximately US$266.5 billion. Exports to the EU rose by 24.3 per cent in October and by 17.2 per cent over the first ten months of the year, with the trade surplus with the EU exceeding US$310 billion. Dave Chia of Moody’s Analytics expects exports to remain robust for the rest of the year, driven by electronics and automobiles; Alicia García-Herrero of Natixis, however, believes that overcapacity, excessive domestic savings and insufficient domestic demand are forcing China to continuously push goods overseas, a model she considers unsustainable.

It is worth noting that the growth rate of imports exceeded that of exports. Given that the year-on-year increase in the industrial producer purchase price for October, released the previous day, stood at 7.9 per cent, a significant portion of the sharp rise in import value may be attributable to higher energy and raw material prices, rather than solely to a recovery in domestic demand; this will require confirmation from the customs authority’s commodity-specific data. A trade surplus of this magnitude is also likely to increase pressure from trading partners, such as the European Union, for China to rebalance its economy. Key points to watch going forward include: customs data on import volumes by commodity; the forthcoming October figures on social financing, credit and economic activity; and developments regarding the EU’s trade measures against China.

Source:Associated Press (reprinted by WISH-TV)、Quartz

5. Trump said that, at the request of Gulf states, he would hold off on further strikes and maintain the blockade; Qatar said progress had been made in the negotiations, whilst Iran denied that negotiations were taking place

According to Euronews, Hashimi, a spokesperson for the Qatari Ministry of Foreign Affairs, stated on 10 November that the meeting between the Qatari Prime Minister and Mr Trump at the White House the previous day had been positive, and that ‘progress’ had been made in the US-Iran negotiations, but that ‘we cannot say how close or how far away an agreement is’. Trump said that Gulf states such as Qatar and Saudi Arabia had urged him not to launch new strikes, and that he would refrain from doing so for the time being, but that the US naval blockade of Iranian ports would remain in place. Iran’s proposal is as follows: if the US ceases its attacks, lifts the port blockade and grants exemptions from certain sanctions, the two sides could resume negotiations within two days at the earliest, with the nuclear issue to be addressed at a later date.

Iran’s statements, however, have been inconsistent. According to Reuters (as reported by GV Wire), Rezaei, an adviser to Iran’s Supreme Leader, said in an interview with Al-Mayadeen that there are currently no negotiations taking place between Tehran and Washington, and that Iran has demanded a ‘clear’ response from the US regarding its conditions, but has not yet received one; Iran insists that the Strait of Hormuz will only be reopened once the US has first met the seven conditions set out in the June interim agreement. According to Euronews, the Qatari Prime Minister also discussed the conflict with ministers from Saudi Arabia, France, the UK, Germany and Italy on Tuesday on the sidelines of the G7 summit in Paris.

Oil prices reacted more moderately to the suspension of sanctions than they had in previous days. According to Yahoo Finance, the WTI December contract closed down 0.44 per cent at US$86.47 per barrel; according to TheStreet, Brent crude was trading below US$91. The Strait remains closed and the blockade has not been lifted; the next move for oil prices will depend on whether the two sides can agree on the sequence of negotiations. Key points to watch going forward include whether the US will respond to Iran’s seven conditions, the time and venue for the first round of formal negotiations, and whether Brent will fall below $90.

Source:Europe News Channel、Reuters (reprinted from GV Wire)、Yahoo Finance

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Several news stories on Tuesday all pointed to the same thing: the cost of capital is rising, and AI assets need to be revalued. The higher-than-expected CPI figure has shifted the likelihood of a December interest rate hike from ‘highly probable’ to ‘nearly certain’, with the focus of discussion now shifting to whether the increase will be 25 or 50 basis points; The 10-year US Treasury yield is approaching 5 per cent, putting the greatest pressure on tech stocks with high valuations and delayed cash flows. Anthropic and CoreWeave illustrate this from two perspectives: Anthropic’s revenue is growing rapidly, but its valuation range is approximately 30 per cent lower than expected a few weeks ago; CoreWeave’s orders and revenue are both growing faster than expected, yet its interest expenses have already exceeded its operating profit. Demand is not the issue; the question is how much the market is willing to pay for this growth given a long-term interest rate of 5 per cent. Nvidia’s sharp decline over two consecutive days is attributable not only to the Huang Renxun incident itself but also to this broader valuation contraction.

A-shares did not follow the US market lower that day, as the US market closed after the A-share market, and the main market themes in China differ from those in the US. According to Sina Finance, on 10 November, the Shanghai Composite Index rose by 0.08 per cent to 3,663.72 points, whilst the Shenzhen Component Index rose by 0.86 per cent, whilst the ChiNext Index rose by 1.46 per cent. Trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.50 trillion yuan, a slight increase from the previous day, with over 4,200 individual stocks rising; cinema chains, gaming, consumer electronics and humanoid robots led the gains, whilst oil and gas extraction, coal and port and shipping sectors led the declines. The pullback in the oil and gas and coal sectors may be linked to falling oil prices and expectations surrounding US-Iran negotiations. Today, the A-share market must digest multiple pressures, including the decline in US technology stocks, rising US Treasury yields and a further drop in Nvidia’s share price. China’s export data indicates that external demand remains the main driver of growth, but the substantial trade surplus is likely to lead to further trade friction.

Checklist:

  • CoreWeave’s share price reaction to its third-quarter results on Wednesday, and whether interest expenses and financing plans have raised new concerns
  • Will the subscription figures and final pricing of Anthropic’s roadshow further divert funds away from AI shares?
  • Comments from Federal Reserve officials on the scale of the December interest rate rise, and whether the yield on 10-year US Treasuries will break through the 5 per cent mark
  • Updates on the NVIDIA shareholder lawsuit and the board of directors’ investigation; will Jensen Huang attend the closed-door congressional briefing on 18 November?
  • The US response to Iran’s seven conditions: will Brent fall below $90?
  • China’s October data on aggregate social financing, credit and economic activity: Can A-share technology stocks withstand the pressure from a correction in the US stock market?
