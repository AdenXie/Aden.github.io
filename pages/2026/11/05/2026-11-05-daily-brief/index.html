Daily Briefing | 5 November 2026: Democrats sweep the mid-term elections to secure control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives; the Dow Jones rises by over 3 per cent whilst Brent crude falls by nearly 10 per cent; the A-share STAR 50 index surges by 9.92 per cent; capital shifts from the semiconductor sector to cloud giants; the National Development and Reform Commission publishes the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’ infrastructure list

Today's main theme

This edition covers Wednesday, 4 November. There is only one main story: the results of the US mid-term elections were announced, with the Democrats securing control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Vice-President Vance stated bluntly that ‘the reason for losing the election was the war’, whilst Trump also acknowledged that oil prices were one of the main factors. The markets interpreted this outcome as signalling “a reduced likelihood of further escalation with Iran”; Brent crude fell by nearly 10 per cent in a single day, dropping below US$95 to its lowest level since early September, whilst US equities surged, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average rising by 1,569 points, or 3.03 per cent, to reclaim the 53,000-point mark. However, the rally across the US stock market was not universal: whilst cloud giants such as Amazon and Microsoft surged, shares in the semiconductor, data centre and energy sectors fell, as investors bet that a Democratic-controlled Congress would slow the expansion of AI data centres, thereby easing capital expenditure pressures on the tech giants. Following the previous day’s sharp decline, A-shares staged a strong rebound, with the STAR 50 Index rising by 9.92 per cent and the ChiNext Index by 6.40 per cent; trading volume across both markets returned to above 2 trillion yuan, led by gains in Changxin Technology, Zhongji Xuchuang and New Ease. On the domestic policy front, the National Development and Reform Commission outlined plans for transport and water network infrastructure during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, including the construction of approximately 15,000 kilometres of new railway lines, around 15 civil transport airports and more than 400 major water network projects.

Key News

1. The Democrats have won control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives; Vance said, “We lost the war.”

The US held its mid-term elections on 3 November, with the results largely clear by 4 November. According to NBC News, prior to the election, the Republicans held a majority in the Senate (53 to 47) and in the House of Representatives (219 to 214); this time, the Democrats have recaptured both chambers, and NBC predicts that the Democrats will begin the new Congress with the largest majority in the House of Representatives in nearly 20 years. According to CBS News, as of the morning of 4 November, the Democrats had secured at least 54 seats in the Senate, whilst their share of seats in the House of Representatives was projected to be between 251 and 268; the Senate race in Alaska was still being counted, whilst the margin in the Mississippi Senate race stood at only a few dozen votes.

The Democrats secured victories in several traditionally Republican states. According to CBS and NBC, the Democrats won Senate seats in Texas (Talarico defeated Paxton), Ohio (Brown), South Carolina (Andrews defeated Graham), North Carolina (Cooper), Iowa, Kansas and Maine, amongst others; in Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed was elected, becoming the first Muslim US Senator. In the gubernatorial elections, the Democrats secured victories in Georgia, Texas, Ohio and Iowa, whilst the Republican Byron Donatz won in Florida.

His post-election remarks are worth noting. According to CBS, Vance posted on X on the morning of 4 November, stating, “The reason we lost was the war,” and suggested that many voters who had supported Trump in 2024 had switched to the Democrats this time around, citing reasons including a desire to end the war with Iran, the cost of living, “America First” and US-Israel relations. Trump said that day that he was “somewhat surprised” but would accept the result, describing Vance’s account as “largely accurate” and attributing the loss to rising oil prices, whilst adding that “if I’d been on the ballot, we would have won”. He also denied that he was considering replacing Defence Secretary Hegessy.

In terms of the implications, once the new Congress begins its work in January, Trump’s legislative agenda, budget, funding and authorisation for war with Iran, as well as regulation of AI and data centres, will all face increased scrutiny through hearings and investigations by the Democrats. However, the President still retains considerable executive leeway on matters such as foreign policy and tariffs. Key developments to watch: the final results in Alaska and Mississippi; the appointment of party leadership; and Trump’s post-election decision regarding a war with Iran.

Source:CBS News、NBC Washington（NBC News）

2. Brent crude fell by nearly 10 per cent in a single day, dropping below US$95, whilst the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose by 1,569 points

The election results have directly altered the pricing dynamics of oil. According to Oilprice.com, around 1.00 pm Eastern Time on 4 November, Brent crude fell by US$8.94, or 8.6 per cent, to US$94.66—its lowest level since 4 September—having traded at close to US$118 just last Friday; According to FinanceFeeds, the January Brent contract fell to around $93.5 in late trading, a drop of nearly 10 per cent on the day, whilst WTI fell by approximately 8.9 per cent to around $87.6. According to the UAE’s *The National*, both benchmark oil prices have fallen by more than 15 per cent cumulatively this week.

In addition to the election, diplomatic signals have also contributed to the fall in oil prices: according to a report cited by Marinelink, Mr Trump told the *Financial Times* that the US had no immediate plans to strike Iran, whilst Qatar is also stepping up its mediation efforts. However, the risks have not disappeared. Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei stated that US hostility would not change regardless of which party controlled Congress; On the evening of 3 November, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed that four oil tankers had struck mines and caught fire south of the Strait of Hormuz, whilst two other vessels had been seized; however, according to InvestingLive, this claim has not been independently verified, nor have the names or flags of the vessels been disclosed. Data from the US Energy Information Administration showed that US crude oil stocks fell by 865,000 barrels in the week ending 30 October, contrary to market expectations of an increase. Karl Larry of Enverus cautioned that an escalation of the situation in Iran could occur more quickly than a resolution.

US shares surged. According to closing data from TheStreet, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose by 1,569.40 points, or 3.03 per cent, to close at 53,356.12; the S&P 500 gained 1.58 per cent to 7,688.62; the Nasdaq rose 1.44 per cent to 25,736.51 points; and the Russell 2000 rose 1.58 per cent. According to CNN, this marked the Dow’s best single-day performance following a mid-term election in 44 years. Analysts believe that a divided government reduces the risk of drastic policy changes; the fall in oil prices has also eased concerns over inflation and further interest rate rises. Gold rose by nearly 3 per cent at one point during early trading, breaking through the $4,000 per ounce mark. Key points to watch: whether Qatar’s mediation efforts will yield substantive progress, whether reports of an oil tanker striking a mine will be confirmed, and whether the fall in oil prices will alter the Federal Reserve’s assessment for December.

Source:Oilprice.com、FinanceFeeds、The National、Marinelink、investingLive、TheStreet

3. Capital is shifting from semiconductors and data centres to cloud giants, as the market bets that the Democrats will ‘put the brakes on the giants’

The most interesting post-election movement in US equities has taken place within the AI sector. According to TheStreet, at 2.50 pm Eastern Time, Amazon was up 7.22 per cent, Microsoft up 7.07 per cent, Apple up 6.27 per cent, and Alphabet, Google’s parent company, up 4.13 per cent. Meanwhile, according to *The Boston Globe*, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was down by around 4 per cent at midday, whilst oil and gas shares had fallen by approximately 5 per cent; according to Reuters (as reported by Yahoo Finance), data centre operators Core Scientific, Iren, CoreWeave and Applied Digital were broadly lower, with intraday losses of around 6 per cent; Solar stocks such as First Solar and Sunrun, meanwhile, were up by around 10 per cent.

The market logic is as follows: a Democratic-controlled Congress may slow down the expansion of AI data centres. According to Reuters, the Democratic-led Congress is expected to hold further hearings and investigations into AI infrastructure development; many newly elected Democrats campaigned against the construction of new data centres, with Texas being a case in point. For the cloud giants, the main reason their share prices have come under pressure in recent months is precisely that capital expenditure has been too high and free cash flow has been squeezed; if external constraints lead everyone to build a little less, existing data centres will actually become more valuable, and cash flow pressures will also ease. Mike Bell of RBC says that a “clear rotation from semiconductor and energy stocks towards hyperscale cloud providers” is taking place in the market. Some analysts also believe that it would be difficult for legislation to actually halt data centre construction; a more likely outcome is that the pace will slow.

This represents a new variable for the AI industry chain: whilst the market had previously been concerned that the tech giants were ‘burning through cash too quickly’, the focus has now shifted to whether policy measures might help reduce their expenditure – a development that directly impacts order forecasts for chip, optical module and power equipment manufacturers. Key points to watch: the new National People’s Congress’s initial moves regarding data centre and AI regulation; Nvidia’s financial results and guidance in mid-November; and whether various cloud service providers will adjust their capital expenditure plans for 2027.

Source:TheStreet、The Boston Globe、Reuters (reprinted by Yahoo Finance)

4. A-shares staged a strong rally in the afternoon: the STAR 50 index rose by 9.92 per cent, whilst trading volume across both markets returned to 2 trillion yuan

Following the sharp fall in optical module shares the previous day, A-shares traded within a narrow range on the morning of 4 November, before surging suddenly in the afternoon, with the main indices closing near their daily highs. According to Sina Finance and the 21st Century Business Herald, the Shanghai Composite Index rose 2.16 per cent to 3,686.17 points, whilst the Shenzhen Component Index rose 4.22 per cent to 11,335.69 points, the ChiNext Index rose 6.40 per cent to 2,544.73 points, the STAR 50 Index rose 9.92 per cent to 1,265.47 points, and the Beijing Stock Exchange 50 Index rose 4.56 per cent. Trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 2.05 trillion yuan, an increase of more than 330 billion yuan from the previous day, marking the first time since 22 September that it has returned above the 2 trillion yuan mark; across the market, approximately 4,667 stocks rose, 779 fell, and 123 hit the daily upper limit. According to Financial World, the STAR Market Composite Index rose by 9.90 per cent, marking its largest single-day gain since the index was launched, with more than 150 STAR Market stocks hitting the daily price limit or rising by over 10 per cent.

Leading the gains were technology shares, which had suffered the heaviest losses the previous day. Semiconductors, memory chips, advanced packaging and CPO saw sharp gains, with Changxin Technology rising by over 13 per cent and trading volume exceeding 20 billion yuan; Zhongji Xuchuang rose by over 11 per cent; New Ease rose by over 13 per cent; and Cambricon rose by over 10 per cent; whilst the baijiu, beverages and insurance sectors bucked the trend and fell.

There are three reasons for the rise. Firstly, policy: the reduction in the interest rate on the share buyback and share purchase re-lending facility to 1 per cent, effective from 4 November, and the implementation of the third tranche of 100 billion yuan in swap facilities, with several successful bidders among the securities firms indicating that the funds will be used to increase holdings of shares and ETFs. Secondly, liquidity: according to the 21st Century Business Herald, equity ETFs have seen net inflows for 22 consecutive trading days, totalling over 555.9 billion yuan. Thirdly, clarifications from companies: Zhongji Xuchuang responded to rumours of ‘order cancellations by overseas clients’, stating that the vast majority of orders remain unaffected; Guangxun Technology denied rumours of production halts due to component shortages; and Changxin Technology plans to raise the upper limit for its share buyback programme to 5 billion yuan. The outcome of the US mid-term elections has also removed a source of external uncertainty. Institutional views remain cautious: Everbright Securities believes the market may gradually stabilise, but a trend reversal remains to be seen; several analysts have cautioned that valuations and market congestion in the technology sector remain high, suggesting significant volatility. Key points to watch going forward: whether trading volume can be maintained at around 2 trillion yuan, and the impact of the weakness in US chip stocks on the A-share computing power sector.

Source:Sina Finance、21st Century Business Herald、The Financial Sector

5. The National Development and Reform Commission outlines infrastructure plans for the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’: the construction of approximately 15,000 kilometres of new railway lines, 15 new airports and more than 400 water network projects

On 4 November, the National Development and Reform Commission held the second press conference in its ‘Striving for the 15th Five-Year Plan’ series, with the theme of ‘Strengthening the Foundations for Development’. According to the 21st Century Business Herald, Xia Xin, Director-General of the Department of Infrastructure Development, stated that during the ‘15th Five-Year Plan’ period, the plan is to construct approximately 15,000 kilometres of new railway lines, of which about 10,000 kilometres will be high-speed rail, thereby essentially completing the ‘Eight Vertical and Eight Horizontal’ high-speed rail network; approximately 2,500 kilometres of new national high-grade waterways and more than 100 automated berths for vessels of 10,000 metric tonnes or more will be constructed, whilst around 15 new civil transport airports will be built. He stated that, given the ample pipeline of new projects in the transport sector and the significant demand for the upgrading and renovation of existing infrastructure, investment is expected to remain substantial; at the same time, priority will be given to projects of strong strategic significance and high returns, whilst reducing inefficient, idle and duplicative construction, and “strictly guarding against speculative hype and a rush to invest in projects in new fields and business models”.

With regard to the national water network, according to the 21st Century Business Herald, Guan Xifan, a first-level inspector at the Department of Rural Economy, stated that of the 40 key water diversion, transfer and drainage channels in the national water network, 35 have been completed or are under construction, whilst of the 588 key water regulation and storage nodes, 473 have been completed or are under construction, accounting for approximately 80 per cent; During the 15th Five-Year Plan period, more than 400 major water network projects—comprising three major categories and nine types—will be advanced in stages; central government investment will be maintained at a reasonable level, and the issuance of water conservancy infrastructure REITs will be expanded.

Against the backdrop of the October manufacturing PMI falling back to 49.3 per cent, with domestic demand remaining weak, infrastructure remains a key driver for stabilising growth. However, the press conference did not disclose the total investment figure, instead placing greater emphasis on project quality and preventing a rush to launch projects; this indicates that infrastructure investment during the 15th Five-Year Plan period will prioritise returns rather than merely pursuing scale. Key points to watch: the allocation of quotas for ultra-long-term special government bonds and special-purpose bonds next year, as well as the pace at which major projects are launched.

Source:21st Century Business Herald (Transport)、21st Century Business Herald (Water Network)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Looking at several news stories together, the impact of the mid-term elections is most evident in oil prices. The market believes that a Democratic-controlled Congress would limit the political scope for continuing the war; consequently, Brent crude fell from close to US$118 to below US$95 within a week, with the war premium being significantly reduced. However, Iran’s hardline stance and unconfirmed reports of oil tankers striking mines indicate that shipping through the Strait is still far from returning to normal, and oil price volatility is set to remain high. The fall in oil prices is good news for central banks worldwide, as it eases the pressure on the Federal Reserve and the Reserve Bank of Australia to raise interest rates further.

The second impact is within the AI industry chain. Whilst the US stock market reacted with ‘positive news for cloud giants and negative news for chips and data centres’, the A-share market saw sharp rises in chips and optical modules, representing divergent trends: The A-share market’s rally is largely driven by a rebound from oversold levels, fuelled by policy-driven capital, ETF inflows and corporate clarifications, whilst the rotation in the US market—should it persist—may affect sentiment in the A-share computing power sector today and in the days ahead. Regarding domestic infrastructure, the National Development and Reform Commission has emphasised the need to “strictly guard against speculative hype”, which serves as a signal to cool down related thematic stocks.

  • Today’s performance of the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, Nvidia and the A-share optical module and memory chip sectors

  • Will cloud giants such as Amazon and Microsoft be able to sustain their upward momentum, and will data centre shares remain under pressure?
  • Trends in 10-year US Treasury yields and the US dollar following the sharp fall in oil prices
