Daily Briefing | October 11, 2026: Employment Support and AI Capital Reassessment Go Hand in Hand

Today's main theme

The news of the past 24 hours collectively points to a resource reallocation driven by high costs and security risks. AI remains central to corporate competition and capital expenditure. Nvidia's plans to further invest in or acquire Reflection AI indicate that the chip giant is extending into the model and software layers. However, the sharp drop in AI-related debt issuance also indicates that investors are beginning to rigorously question cash flow and returns in a high-interest-rate environment. After achieving 87.7% of its annual urban employment target in the first three quarters, China is preparing to expand job supply through service sector policies, making stable growth more directly reflected in employment and residents' expectations. Meanwhile, Russian airstrikes caused new heavy civilian casualties, and Saudi Arabia continued to host an energy and investment conference despite the airport attack. Policy support, technological expansion, and geopolitical conflicts are vying for the same batch of capital. The market is no longer just looking at growth rate, but also at financing resilience, supply security, and policy credibility.

Five key news items

I. AI debt issuance has cooled significantly, and capital has begun to review the returns on infrastructure investment.

According to a Financial Times report on October 10, AI-related debt issuance fell to approximately $23 billion in September, less than half that of August and far below the peak of approximately $113 billion in June. There were no new AI bonds issued in the US investment-grade market that month. Since 2026, tech companies have still raised approximately $466 billion in AI-related debt, far exceeding the $101 billion in 2025. However, the rapid decline from its peak indicates that investors are beginning to worry about leverage, high interest rates, and the speed at which data center returns will be realized. Previously, from January to August, U.S. investment-grade issuers had completed approximately $306 billion in financing, while potential deals such as Broadcom's approximately $60 billion financing with Anthropic and SpaceX's approximately $40 billion chip financing are still awaiting market scrutiny. The change does not mean the end of the AI investment cycle, but rather that financing conditions have shifted from "scale first" to prioritizing customer contracts, power supply security, data center utilization rate, and free cash flow. Going forward, we should observe U.S. long-term bond yields, credit spreads, whether new projects are being scaled back, and whether large technology companies can cover capital costs with cloud revenue and model services.Source: Financial Times

II. China added 10.52 million urban jobs in the first three quarters, with plans to further expand employment through the service sector.

China's Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security announced on October 10 that 10.52 million new urban jobs were created nationwide from January to September, achieving 87.7% of the annual target. The urban surveyed unemployment rate averaged 5.2% from January to August, and employment remained generally stable. The next step in the policy will be to work with relevant departments to formulate documents to promote employment through the development of the service industry, strengthen the role of the service industry as the main channel for absorbing jobs, stabilize employment in labor-intensive industries, and explore new jobs in emerging and future industries. As of the end of September, the number of people enrolled in basic old-age, unemployment and work-related injury insurance reached 1.079 billion, 251 million and 327 million respectively, with a cumulative surplus of 11.2 trillion yuan in the three social insurance funds. Data shows that the overall target is progressing rapidly, but the special policies for the service industry also reflect that structural pressures still exist: there is a mismatch between college graduates, workers in traditional industries, and new technology positions. Whether policies can improve income expectations and consumption depends on the wages, stability, and social security coverage of newly created jobs, not just the quantity. Going forward, we will focus on fiscal and tax support in service sector documents, platform employment rights, vocational training, and youth unemployment indicators.Source: Human Resources and Social Security System Release｜Additional reporting: Reuters

III. Nvidia is in talks to acquire Reflection AI, extending competition from chips to open models.

Reuters, citing a Financial Times report on October 10, reported that Nvidia is discussing further investment in Reflection AI or a direct acquisition of the open-weight model startup. Negotiations are still in the early stages and could involve a full acquisition or a "talent acquisition" involving hiring a team and licensing the technology. The deal could be done within weeks, or it could fall through. Nvidia had previously invested $800 million in Reflection. Founded in 2024 by former DeepMind researchers, the company focuses on automation software development and this week launched its first open weighted model, Beam, in an attempt to compete with low-cost Chinese models such as DeepSeek and Kimi in programming and agent tasks. The company sought a pre-investment valuation of $25 billion when it raised funds this year. If the deal goes ahead, Nvidia will further expand from GPUs and networking equipment into models, development tools, and enterprise applications, while increasing customer stickiness to its hardware and software ecosystem. However, this would also amplify conflicts of interest and regulatory scrutiny: Nvidia both supplies the industry and may control important model assets. Subsequent variables include the transaction structure, the attitude of antitrust authorities, Beam's actual performance, and whether the open weight commitment can be maintained after the merger.Source: Reuters

IV. Russian airstrikes in Zaporizhzhia kill 20 people; easing sanctions triggers a stronger backlash.

On October 10, Russian forces launched an attack on the southern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia, killing 20 people and injuring 18, including three children. The local area is less than 30 kilometers from the front line. This is the third large-scale casualty attack in Ukraine in a week, following attacks in Kramatorsk and Priluki in the previous two days, which killed nearly 80 civilians. Kyiv was also hit by a new round of drone and missile attacks that day, causing power outages in some areas and the subway to be suspended again. The incident occurred a day after the United States announced a temporary easing of sanctions on Russian diesel fuel, which Zelensky said sent the wrong signal to Moscow that it could continue fighting. US, European, and Ukrainian officials had previously discussed a joint peace plan in Miami, but Putin said that given Ukraine's continued attacks on targets inside Russia, it was unlikely that negotiations would resume immediately. The combination of military escalation and easing sanctions could increase Europe's energy, security, and financial burdens, and make any ceasefire arrangement more difficult to build mutual trust. Further attention will be paid to whether the US special envoy will visit Russia, the non-aggression plan for energy facilities, European aid to Ukraine, and whether diesel trade exemptions will translate into new fiscal revenue for Russia.Source: Reuters

V. Following the attack on Riyadh Airport, Saudi Arabia insisted on holding an energy and investment conference.

Saudi Arabia confirmed on October 10 that the World Petroleum Congress and the Future Investment Initiative will still be held in Riyadh as planned. Previously, the Houthi rebels attacked King Khalid International Airport, killing four people, severely damaging a passenger plane, and causing widespread flight disruptions. Britain, France and Germany have warned their citizens to avoid travelling to Riyadh. The World Petroleum Congress is scheduled to be held from October 10 to 14, while the Future Investment Initiative is scheduled for October 26 to 29. Financial figures such as JPMorgan Chase CEO Dimon and Citigroup CEO Fraser were originally included in the list of attendees. The organizers stated that security will be strengthened at airports, venues, hotels, and transportation, and that delegates will be provided with alternative routes, such as entering Riyadh by land from Dammam. However, the Italian energy minister has switched to attending the meeting remotely, and Lufthansa, Etihad Airways, and Dubai Airlines have also suspended some flights. Saudi Arabia insists the conference is aimed at maintaining its credibility in energy diplomacy and attracting foreign investment, but the attacks directly pass on the costs of aviation, insurance, and personnel safety to business activities. Going forward, we will focus on actual attendance, flight resumption, the scope of the Houthi attack, and whether the risk premium will affect Saudi investment projects and oil market expectations.Source: Reuters

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Interest rates remain the core variable connecting today's news. Long-term yields in the United States are at multi-year highs, posing higher discount rates for projects with longer payback periods such as data centers, model training, and chip procurement. The cooling of AI debt issuance stands in stark contrast to Nvidia's acquisitions of software assets: capital will continue to flow into AI, but prefers leading companies with cash flow, platform control, and pricing power. In terms of exchange rates, high energy prices and safe-haven demand may still support the US dollar. Europe is also under pressure from the Russia-Ukraine war, fiscal spending, and energy imports, limiting the euro's rebound potential. On the commodity side, while the risks in Saudi airspace and Riyadh airport will not directly disrupt crude oil production, they will increase shipping, aviation, and insurance costs. If regional conflicts escalate, oil prices and inflation expectations could push up bond yields again. Risk appetite is therefore likely to remain divergent: leading technology companies with stable cash flow outperform highly leveraged AI projects, energy and defense assets will benefit relatively, while aviation, tourism, and overvalued growth stocks are more sensitive. If China's service sector employment policies can improve wages and job stability, it will boost consumption and RMB expectations. The real test is whether youth employment, residents' income, and service retail can improve simultaneously. Today, we will focus on AI credit spreads, energy prices, European risk assets, and specific tools for China's employment policies.
