Daily Briefing | 14 November 2026: NVIDIA’s board approaches Arm CEO Haas; Bessent claims the Department of Commerce is delaying the blacklisting of Megaspeed; US October PPI rises to 6.5 per cent year-on-year; AMD falls nearly 9 per cent, dragging down the Nasdaq; China’s renminbi-denominated loans fall by 100 billion yuan in October; Iran says LNG tanker struck a mine; IEA says oil market returns to surplus; ChiNext Index falls 3.32 per cent

Today's main theme

This edition covers the period from the evening of 12 November (Thursday) to 13 November (Friday). On the day following Jensen Huang’s resignation, the issues surrounding Nvidia expanded from ‘who will take over’ to ‘who in Washington is to blame’: According to the *Financial Times*, NVIDIA’s board has approached Arm CEO René Haas to discuss him taking over; Treasury Secretary Bessent has publicly stated that the Treasury and several other departments had advocated months ago for Megaspeed—linked to the ‘spying scandal’—to be added to the Entity List, but that the final decision rested with the Department of Commerce. AI hardware shares continued to fall on Friday, with AMD down nearly 9 per cent and the Nasdaq down 0.91 per cent; however, both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq still recorded weekly gains, ending a four-week losing streak. US PPI rose by 0.3 per cent month-on-month in October, slightly below expectations, but climbed to 6.5 per cent year-on-year – the highest level since November 2022. The market has already fully priced in a rate hike of at least 25 basis points by the Federal Reserve in December. Domestically, financial data for October released by the central bank showed that renminbi-denominated loans fell by approximately 100 billion yuan that month, with both household and corporate lending contracting, whilst M2 growth slowed to 7.1 per cent. In the Middle East, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard stated that an LNG tanker had struck a mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz, whilst the International Energy Agency reported that exports from the Gulf had resumed, with the oil market returning to a surplus for the first time since the outbreak of hostilities. A-shares fell across the board on Friday, with the ChiNext Index down 3.32 per cent; computing hardware led the decline, whilst banking and baijiu stocks bucked the trend to rise.

Key News

1. NVIDIA’s board of directors has approached Arm CEO Haas; Bessent stated that the Treasury had long advocated blacklisting Megaspeed

According to a 13 November report in the *Financial Times*, as relayed by Reuters, Nvidia’s board has approached Arm CEO René Haas to discuss him succeeding Jensen Huang. The report states that Haas is one of several candidates, and there is no guarantee that he will be offered or accept the position; Arm declined to comment, whilst Nvidia did not immediately respond, and Reuters was unable to independently verify the report. According to *Fortune*, Simmons, a partner at the executive search firm Crist Kolder, believes the board would favour someone with a technical background, and mentioned that Ian Buck, a vice-president who joined Nvidia in 2004, could be an internal candidate. Interim CEO Kress will continue to serve concurrently as CFO.

On the same day, the political dimensions of the incident were also escalating. According to *Fortune*, Treasury Secretary Bessent said in an interview with CNBC on 13 November that the Treasury Department, the Department of Defence, the Office of the United States Trade Representative and the State Department had all supported adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department’s Entity List “for several months”, but that “the pen is in the Commerce Department’s hand” and the final decision rests with the Commerce Department. The US Department of Justice is investigating whether Megaspeed and its Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatric, have violated chip export controls; according to *The Information*, the two companies have shipped at least US$7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China over the past two years, and Megaspeed is headed by Alice Huang, who is linked to Jensen Huang. *Fortune* also cited Axios and Reuters as reporting that Bessent had a face-to-face dispute with Commerce Secretary Lutnick following the midterm elections, and that senior White House officials are considering whether to replace Lutnick; Lutnick has agreed to appear alongside Jensen Huang at a Senate Banking Committee hearing on 10 December. Neither the Department of Commerce nor Nvidia responded to *Fortune*’s requests for comment.

In terms of market reaction, according to Reuters, Nvidia closed down 2.01 per cent at US$162.85 on Friday, marking its sixth consecutive week of losses—the longest streak of weekly declines since October 2012; Arm fell by more than 5 per cent during US trading hours, though the decline narrowed somewhat following the news report. Key points to watch: the earnings report and Kress’s first conference call on Tuesday 17 November, the board’s choice of successor, as well as Lutnick’s future and whether export control policies will be tightened as a result.

Source:RTÉ (Reuters)、*Fortune* (Besent)、*Fortune* (Kress)、MarketScreener (Reuters Closing)

2. US PPI rose by 0.3 per cent month-on-month in October and climbed to 6.5 per cent year-on-year; AMD fell by nearly 9 per cent, weighing on the Nasdaq

The US Bureau of Labour Statistics announced on 13 November that the seasonally adjusted month-on-month change in the Producer Price Index (PPI) for final demand rose by 0.3 per cent in October, below the 0.4 per cent forecast in a Dow Jones survey, whilst the September figure was revised upwards to 1.4 per cent. According to data compiled by StockTitan, the unadjusted year-on-year figure rose by 6.5 per cent, compared with an expected 6.6 per cent; the September figure was revised to 6.3 per cent, marking the largest 12-month increase since November 2022. The core PPI, excluding food and energy, rose by 0.3 per cent month-on-month, in line with expectations, whilst the year-on-year figure stood at 5.0 per cent; however, the index excluding food, energy and trade services rose by 0.5 per cent month-on-month, exceeding the forecast of 0.3 per cent. Commodity prices rose by 0.5 per cent, services by 0.2 per cent, whilst energy fell by 0.1 per cent, indicating that price pressures are spreading from energy to other commodities.

Fed officials continue to strike a hawkish tone. According to Reuters, San Francisco Fed President Daly said her “inclination” was that the Fed needed to raise interest rates, whilst Boston Fed President Collins said that tighter policy was on the horizon due to elevated inflation; CME FedWatch indicates that a rate hike of at least 25 basis points in December is now fully priced in, whilst the probability of a 50-basis-point hike stands at around 10 per cent. According to Yahoo Finance, the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note rose to an intraday high of 5.17 per cent, its highest level since October.

US shares closed with mixed results. According to preliminary data from Reuters, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 46.14 points, or 0.09 per cent, to close at 52,343.23; the S&P 500 fell 31.53 points, or 0.42 per cent, to close at 7,560.87; the Nasdaq fell 230.13 points, or 0.91 per cent, to close at 25,135.96 points, with the technology sector being the worst performer among the 11 sectors. According to Investing.com, AMD fell 8.75 per cent to US$534.06, Dell fell 7.18 per cent and Cisco fell 6.24 per cent, whilst AI hardware and ‘new cloud’ companies such as Broadcom, CoreWeave and Nebius also weakened; The VIX rose by 3.10 per cent to 19.28. Despite Friday’s decline, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq still posted cumulative gains for the week, ending a four-week losing streak. Reuters also noted that Anthropic is expected to go public next week, which could divert funds away from existing AI stocks. Key points to watch: the PCE data due at the end of the month, comments from Federal Reserve officials ahead of the monetary policy meeting on 8–9 December, and whether the 10-year yield can hold above 5 per cent.

Source:StockTitan（PPI）、MarketScreener (Reuters Closing)、Investing.com、Yahoo Finance

3. Renminbi lending in China fell by approximately 100 billion yuan in October, whilst M2 growth slowed to 7.1 per cent

On 13 November, the People’s Bank of China released financial statistics for October. According to the central bank’s report, the M2 balance stood at 359.06 trillion yuan at the end of October, representing a year-on-year increase of 7.1 per cent; the M1 balance stood at 116.9 trillion yuan, up 4.4 per cent year-on-year; the balance of renminbi loans was 283.33 trillion yuan, up 4.7 per cent year-on-year; and the stock of total social financing was 469.16 trillion yuan, up 7.2 per cent year-on-year. In the first ten months of the year, RMB loans increased by 11.42 trillion yuan, whilst the cumulative increase in social financing stood at 28.61 trillion yuan, which was 2.29 trillion yuan less than in the same period last year.

Based on a retroactive calculation using data from the first ten months compared with the first nine months, RMB loans fell by approximately 100 billion yuan in October, marking the third instance of negative monthly growth this year (following April and July). According to a report republished by Sina Finance, household loans fell by 430 billion yuan, loans to enterprises and public institutions decreased by 160 billion yuan, whilst loans to non-bank financial institutions increased by 347 billion yuan; total social financing increased by approximately 1.15 trillion yuan during the month. According to a Reuters survey cited by Hong Kong’s *The English Tiger*, the market had originally expected new loans in October to total around 20 billion yuan; the growth rate of the loan balance hit a record low of 4.7 per cent, whilst M2 growth was the slowest in 19 months, and M1 growth rebounded from 3.2 per cent in September to 4.4 per cent.

Official and market interpretations differ. Experts quoted in the report emphasised that enterprises are increasingly raising funds through bond issuance, which serves as a substitute for loans; furthermore, the approximately 1.4 trillion yuan in special refinancing bonds used to replace loans has also suppressed the total volume of credit. Xing Zhaopeng of ANZ Bank, however, pointed out that the shift towards net repayment of medium- and long-term loans by households and enterprises indicates that demand remains weak. According to the 21st Century Business Herald, Wang Qing of Orient Credit Rating and Wen Bin of China Minsheng Bank, amongst others, believe that with strong external demand and structural policy tools already in effect, the likelihood of a comprehensive cut in the reserve requirement ratio or interest rates before the end of the year has diminished. This is consistent with the stance outlined in the central bank’s third-quarter monetary policy report, which stated that ‘interest rates are already low, and the focus is on smoothing transmission’. Key developments to watch: the forthcoming October data on industrial output, retail sales and fixed-asset investment; the November LPR quote; and the policy direction set at the Central Economic Work Conference in December.

Source:The Paper (Central Bank Report)、Sina Finance、The English Tiger News、21st Century Business Herald

4. Iran claims an LNG tanker was blown up after striking a mine in the Strait of Hormuz; the IEA says the oil market has returned to a surplus for the first time since the outbreak of the war

According to a Reuters report republished by gCaptain, the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy stated via official media on 13 November that a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas had struck a mine and exploded whilst following an “unauthorised and dangerous route” through the Strait of Hormuz; the vessel’s name, flag and details of casualties have not yet been disclosed. This is yet another incident following the explosions involving four oil tankers on Thursday. According to a live summary by *The Gulf Times*, the Revolutionary Guards also claimed to have struck a US early-warning radar station in Bahrain, a claim denied by the US; the US stated that it had shot down two Iranian one-way attack drones heading for Bahrain. According to the same newspaper, Turkey and Qatar signed a defence agreement on the same day.

In contrast to the maritime tensions, supply figures paint a different picture. According to BNN Bloomberg, the International Energy Agency (IEA) stated in its monthly oil market report on 13 November that exports from Gulf states are recovering, and that global supply in the fourth quarter of this year will average 105 million barrels per day, exceeding demand by approximately 1.7 million barrels, marking the first instance of a supply surplus since the outbreak of the war in Iran; the IEA had previously forecast that a shortfall would persist in the fourth quarter. The IEA forecasts that global demand will fall by 2.7 million barrels per day to 102.3 million barrels per day in 2026, before rebounding by 2 million barrels per day in 2027, when there will be a surplus of approximately 3.8 million barrels per day. According to reports, the resumption of exports from the Gulf has partly relied on oil tankers switching off their positioning signals to ‘sneak through’ the straits; OPEC data shows that OPEC+ production in October stood at 41.23 million barrels per day, an increase of 2.04 million barrels per day month-on-month, returning to 97 per cent of pre-war levels in February.

Oil prices showed little fluctuation on the day. According to BNN Bloomberg, Brent stood at US$95.71 per barrel following the release of the IEA report; according to Investing.com, the WTI December contract closed at US$89.07, up 0.37 per cent. The Gulf Times noted that oil prices are expected to record their second consecutive weekly decline this week. Key points to watch going forward include whether attacks in the Strait of Hormuz continue to escalate, the US’s next steps regarding Iran, and whether OPEC+ will adjust the pace of its production increases.

Source:gCaptain (Reuters)、The Gulf Times、BNN Bloomberg、Investing.com

5. A-shares fell across the board: the ChiNext Index dropped by 3.32 per cent, with over 4,400 individual stocks falling, whilst banking and baijiu stocks bucked the trend to rise

According to the 21st Century Business Herald, on 13 November, the three major A-share indices all closed lower: the Shanghai Composite Index fell by 0.59 per cent to close at 3,634.35 points; the Shenzhen Component Index fell by 2.05 per cent to close at 10,968.79 points; the ChiNext Index fell by 3.32 per cent to close at 2,426.08 points; and the STAR 50 Index fell by 2.30 per cent to close at 1,279.66 points. Trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.66 trillion yuan, down from the previous day’s figure of approximately 1.93 trillion yuan; 744 stocks rose whilst 4,415 fell, with 38 hitting the daily upper limit and 39 hitting the daily lower limit.

The decline was concentrated in computing hardware. According to The Paper, telecommunications equipment, semiconductors and precious metals fell sharply, whilst CPO suffered heavy losses; PCB stocks such as Huatong Electronics and Jingwang Electronics hit their daily lower limits; meanwhile, cinema chains, baijiu, the livestock farming sector, city commercial banks and rural commercial banks bucked the trend to rise. The top five stocks by trading value were, in order: Zhongji Xuchuang (25.874 billion yuan), New Ease (19.651 billion yuan), Cambricon (16.038 billion yuan), Dongshan Precision (15.333 billion yuan) and Tianfu Communications (10.112 billion yuan). Capital continued to be concentrated in optical modules and domestically produced computing power, though trading volumes were generally lower than the previous day.

This marks the first full reaction of the A-share market to the management changes at Nvidia and US Treasury yields rising above 5.1 per cent. Among companies in Nvidia’s supply chain, those specialising in optical modules and printed circuit boards (PCBs) have seen the highest valuations and the greatest volatility, whilst capital has shifted towards defensive sectors such as banking and baijiu. As the A-share market closed before the central bank released its financial data, the impact of negative loan growth will not be reflected until next Monday. Key points to watch going forward include: how the A-share market will react next Monday to the credit data and the continued decline in US AI hardware stocks; volatility in the optical module sector ahead of Nvidia’s earnings report; and whether trading volume can hold above 1.5 trillion yuan.

Source:21st Century Business Herald (Market Close)、The Paper

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Friday’s market action demonstrated that the pressure on AI-related trading is no longer driven solely by interest rates. The PPI figure, which came in slightly below expectations on a month-on-month basis, failed to further stoke expectations of an interest rate hike, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average even rose slightly; however, the Nasdaq and AI hardware stocks continued to fall, with AMD, Dell and Broadcom all recording steeper declines than Nvidia. There are three underlying reasons for this: the succession at Nvidia remains unresolved, with only one trading day left before its earnings report; Bessent has publicly shifted the blame for the Megaspeed issue onto the Department of Commerce, suggesting that export controls may be tightened; and Anthropic’s IPO next week may also divert capital. The combination of these factors has led the market to adopt a more cautious stance towards the valuations of AI hardware stocks.

From a macroeconomic perspective, the signals from the US and China are moving in opposite directions. In the US, inflation remains high and the Federal Reserve is preparing to raise interest rates, with the 10-year yield standing at over 5.1 per cent; in China, credit demand is weak and lending is contracting, but the authorities are more inclined to use structural tools and bond financing to compensate, leaving little scope for across-the-board interest rate cuts in the short term. As for oil prices, the IEA’s assessment of a supply surplus coincides with ongoing attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. Provided the situation in the Strait does not deteriorate significantly, the recovery in supply will keep oil prices in check, which represents one of the few positive factors for US inflation expectations.

  • Will the yield on 10-year US Treasuries hold above 5 per cent? Comments from Federal Reserve officials on the scale of the December rate rise
  • China’s October economic activity data and November LPR rates: will weak credit growth prompt new policy measures?
