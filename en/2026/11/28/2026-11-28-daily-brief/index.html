Daily Briefing | 28 November 2026: US stocks surge on Black Friday, with chips leading the gains; Chinese shares soar; Central Huijin to act as a quasi-stabilisation fund; Saudi Arabia’s Jizan refinery attacked; Brent crude nears US$97; S&P places Nvidia on review for downgrade

Today's main theme

This edition covers the US stock market close from 26 November (Thursday, when markets were closed for the US Thanksgiving holiday) to 27 November (Friday). On the first trading day after Thanksgiving, global markets rebounded in unison: US shares surged during the half-day ‘Black Friday’ session, with chip stocks leading the gains, whilst the three major indices recorded their best weekly performance in over a year; Chinese equities soared on the same day, with the Shanghai Composite Index rising by 3.7 per cent and the STAR 50 Index surging by 15 per cent, triggered by a State Council executive meeting which clarified that Central Huijin should better fulfil its role as a ‘quasi-stabilisation fund’. On the same day, China’s industrial enterprise profits for October rose by 20.1 per cent year-on-year, marking the fastest growth in five months, though sectoral divergence remained pronounced. In the energy sector, a projectile struck the Jizan refinery in Saudi Arabia on 26 November, killing one worker; Brent crude approached US$97, whilst OPEC+ is set to meet on 29 November. Meanwhile, in the AI sector, S&P placed Nvidia’s AA rating on review for downgrade, stating that Jensen Huang’s return to the company had exposed shortcomings in corporate governance – whilst the share price has rebounded, questions regarding governance remain unresolved.

Key News

1. US shares surged on ‘Black Friday’: the Dow rose by 1.19 per cent and the Nasdaq by 2.31 per cent, with chip stocks leading the gains; the three major indices recorded their best weekly performance in over a year.

According to Reuters, US shares closed sharply higher on 27 November during the half-day trading session following Thanksgiving, as investors bought back AI-related shares that had been battered earlier this month. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 597.69 points, or 1.19 per cent, to close at 50,780.64; the S&P 500 rose by 115.56 points, or 1.61 per cent, to close at 7,298.34; and the Nasdaq Composite gained 543.70 points, or 2.31 per cent, to close at 24,129.50. All three indices posted substantial gains for the week, marking their best weekly performance in over a year.

Leading the gains were chip stocks that had previously suffered the heaviest losses: Micron and Marvell both rose by more than 7 per cent, whilst SanDisk climbed by around 16 per cent; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose in tandem; AI beneficiaries such as Dell and Lumentum also rose, whilst Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle and Tesla all closed higher, with Amazon up by around 4 per cent and Tesla by over 5 per cent. NVIDIA edged up by around 0.8 per cent, though it remains down by approximately 38 per cent from its early October high. However, trading volumes were light, suggesting that the sustainability of this rally will need to be tested once normal trading resumes after the holiday.

Consumer data has added fuel to the rebound. According to Adobe Analytics, US online spending totalled US$82.5 billion between 1 and 22 November, representing a year-on-year increase of 9.1 per cent; Adobe estimates that online sales on Black Friday alone reached US$12.9 billion. Key events to watch: the US November non-farm payrolls report on 4 December, the November CPI on 10 December, and the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy meeting on 8–9 December. The market is currently almost fully pricing in a 25-basis-point interest rate hike in December.

Source:Reuters (reprinted by Kitco)、XTB、Candid Dragon (Adobe data)

2. Chinese stock markets recorded their biggest rise in over two years: a State Council executive meeting clarified that Central Huijin would act as a ‘quasi-stabilisation fund’, with the Shanghai Composite Index rising by 3.7 per cent and the STAR 50 Index surging by 15 per cent

According to Reuters, on 27 November, the Chinese stock market rebounded sharply from a 17-month low, recording its largest single-day gain in more than two years. The Shanghai Composite Index closed up 3.7 per cent, whilst the CSI 300 rose 4.2 per cent—both marking their largest single-day gains since the announcement of a major stimulus package in October 2024; the STAR 50 index soared by 15 per cent, marking its largest single-day gain in two years; the semiconductor sub-index surged by 19 per cent, recording its largest single-day gain on record. The Hang Seng Index rose by 2.1 per cent, whilst the Hang Seng Tech Index climbed by over 6 per cent. The renminbi also strengthened against the US dollar, approaching its strongest level in four years.

The trigger was Beijing’s clear signal to prop up the market. According to Xinhua News Agency, Premier Li Qiang presided over a State Council executive meeting on 27 November, calling on state-owned ‘national team’ investors such as Central Huijin to play a greater role, optimise Central Huijin’s function as China’s quasi-‘stabilisation fund’, and provide ample liquidity during market turbulence. The meeting also emphasised that the capital markets are crucial to the overall economic development, and that efforts must be made to channel long-term capital into the market and address external risks. As previous measures, such as the relaxation of margin trading rules, had failed to boost confidence, this statement was viewed by the market as the clearest signal to date of a commitment to propping up the market.

Prior to this rally, A-shares had been on a steady downward trend since July, dragged down by factors including weak domestic demand, a prolonged slump in the property sector, and the possibility that trade tensions could impact exports—the economy’s bright spot. Key points to watch: whether Central Huijin will inject real capital into the market; the policy direction for 2027 to be set at the Central Economic Work Conference in December; and the official manufacturing PMI for November (to be released on 1 December, with a Reuters survey forecast of 49.4, still within the contractionary range).

Source:Reuters (reprinted by WDSM)、Reuters (Cabinet meeting)

3. Profits at Chinese industrial enterprises rose by 20.1 per cent year-on-year in October, marking the fastest growth in five months, though the recovery showed marked disparities

According to a report by Reuters, data released by the National Bureau of Statistics on 27 November showed that profits at industrial enterprises above a certain scale rose by 20.1 per cent year-on-year in October, up from 14.4 per cent in September, marking the fastest growth in five months; profits for the period from January to October rose by 16 per cent year-on-year, an acceleration from the 15.5 per cent recorded for January to September.

Divergence is the central theme of these figures. Profits in the computer, communications and other electronic equipment manufacturing sector doubled between January and October, with profit growth in AI-related and upstream industries outpacing that of downstream sectors; by contrast, profits in the furniture manufacturing sector plummeted by 52.4 per cent. Song Lin, Chief Economist for Greater China at ING, said that the recovery remains uneven, sending mixed signals regarding the overall health of the economy. Although export demand remains a key pillar of support, weak domestic demand continues to weigh on most enterprises.

At the policy level, Beijing has in recent months introduced interest rate cuts, injected liquidity and provided targeted financing support for the technology, manufacturing and infrastructure sectors. Analysts expect that, as the annual growth target of 4.5 per cent to 5 per cent is likely to be met, there will be no large-scale stimulus measures this year. Key points to watch: whether the official Manufacturing PMI on 1 December and the Caixin Manufacturing PMI on 2 December will confirm a stabilisation, and the policy signals from the Central Economic Work Conference.

Source:Reuters (reprinted by WDSM)

4. A refinery in Jizan, Saudi Arabia, was attacked, leaving one worker dead; Brent crude approached US$97; OPEC+ is due to meet on 29 November

According to a Reuters report, the Saudi Ministry of Energy stated that on 26 November, a projectile struck Saudi Aramco’s Jizan refinery (with a daily processing capacity of 400,000 barrels) on the Red Sea coast, killing one Nepalese worker and injuring several others, and leading to the temporary suspension of part of the refinery’s operations. This marks the third attack on a Saudi refinery in five days, following strikes on the Rabigh and Rastanura refineries.

Oil prices rose in response. On 26 November, Brent crude closed at US$96.57, marking its highest closing price since 6 November; it rose as high as $97.63 during trading on 27 November and is set to rise by around 4.5 per cent for the week, marking the first weekly gain in November; however, it remains down by around 20 per cent for the month. A series of attacks on oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, coupled with strikes on Saudi energy facilities, has prompted the market to reassess supply risks in the Middle East, despite the International Energy Agency’s earlier assessment that crude oil supply had begun to outstrip demand.

OPEC+ is due to meet on Sunday 29 November. According to Reuters, citing sources familiar with the matter, the group of oil-producing nations may maintain its production targets until at least the first quarter of 2027. Key points to watch: the duration of the shutdown at the Jizan refinery, the state of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, and whether the OPEC+ meeting will, as expected, take no action.

Source:Reuters (reprinted by LaPost)、El-Fondo (Reuters)

5. S&P places Nvidia’s ‘AA’ rating on review for downgrade: Huang Renxun’s return exposes governance shortcomings; rating may be downgraded by one notch within 90 days

According to a Reuters report, S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia’s AA issuer credit rating and senior unsecured debt rating on ‘Credit Watch’ with a negative outlook on Wednesday 25 November, citing the departure and subsequent reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang as evidence of weaknesses in the company’s management and board oversight. S&P stated that it may downgrade the ratings by one notch to AA- within 90 days, unless an investigation or new disclosures damage the company’s reputation or business outlook.

The specific issues highlighted by S&P include: potential compromises to the independence of long-serving board members; inadequate oversight of the code of conduct for senior executives; a lack of succession planning; and the company’s heavy reliance on Jensen Huang personally, known as ‘key person risk’. S&P also stated that it has not yet seen the board take any remedial measures.

On the same day, Reuters also reported that, according to three sources familiar with the matter, the US Department of Justice had not formally opened a case against Huang before Trump pushed for his reinstatement, but had concluded, based on investigations into two of Nvidia’s clients (Advanced Micro Devices and Megaspeed), that there was no evidence to suggest he had broken the law; This conclusion may change as the investigation progresses. NVIDIA’s share price closed 3.91 per cent lower at US$149.30 on Wednesday, before rebounding slightly on Friday. Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear for a closed-door hearing before the relevant House committee on 1 December. Key points to watch: whether the board will introduce governance reforms during the 90-day review period, and the content of his testimony before Congress.

Source:Reuters (reprinted from The Hindu BusinessLine)、Reuters (reprinted by Business Today)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Friday’s global rally had a common underlying theme: a rebound following a sharp sell-off, coupled with signals of policy support. As for US equities, AI stocks had previously fallen too sharply, and the ‘Black Friday’ rebound was more a technical correction than a fundamental reversal – Nvidia rose by just 0.8 per cent and remains nearly 40 per cent below its October high, the Nasdaq 100 had previously come close to the 20 per cent bear market threshold relative to its earlier high, whilst a Fed rate hike in December has been almost fully priced in, and rising interest rates remain the overarching factor weighing on valuations. In China, the statement from Central Huijin regarding its “quasi-stabilisation fund” represents the clearest signal of market support so far this year. The semiconductor index’s 19 per cent single-day surge indicates that capital is first flocking to the most oversold sectors; however, October’s industrial profit data reminds us that the recovery remains uneven and that weak domestic demand has yet to be resolved. a single day’s sharp rally is not sufficient to confirm a trend reversal. As for oil prices, the geopolitical premium is returning, but Brent crude is still down by around 20 per cent for the month; the fundamental assessment of oversupply remains unchanged, and it is highly likely that the OPEC+ meeting on Sunday will take no action.

Checklist:

  • Will the US stock market rally continue? Non-farm payrolls on 4 December, CPI on 10 December, and the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy meeting on 8–9 December (interest rate hike magnitude and dot plot)
  • Has Central Huijin actually stepped in to prop up the market? Will the A-share market be able to hold on to Friday’s gains? The official manufacturing PMI for 1 December
  • The Central Economic Work Conference in December set the tone for policy in 2027
  • The duration of the Jizan refinery shutdown, the state of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, the outcome of the OPEC+ meeting on 29 November, and whether Brent crude will break the $100 mark
  • Progress on NVIDIA’s 90-day rating review and board governance reforms; Jensen Huang to appear before a closed-door congressional hearing on 1 December
