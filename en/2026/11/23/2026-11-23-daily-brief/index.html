Today's main theme

This edition covers the period from 21 November (Saturday) to 22 November (Sunday), whilst the Riemann Hypothesis series began on 20 November. There was no trading over the weekend, but there was no shortage of news. Last week, the Shanghai Composite Index fell to around 3,500 points at one point, on the evening of 22 November, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) approved the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges’ reduction of the minimum margin requirement for margin trading from 100 per cent to 80 per cent. At the same time, the Shenzhen Stock Exchange sought public comments on market-making rules for the ChiNext board, whilst the exchanges and China Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation introduced fee reduction measures; these three policy measures were announced on the same day. In the field of AI, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, published a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis developed in collaboration with Claude. Written in Lean, it comprises approximately 17 million lines of code. On 21 November, the Lean and Mathlib communities issued a joint statement certifying the correctness of this formal proof; However, no human-readable paper has yet been published, and the peer-review process within the mathematical community has only just begun. In the Middle East, Iran attacked approximately 25 oil tankers in the vicinity of the Strait of Hormuz over the past week and claimed to have struck a US military radar station in Bahrain, whilst Trump stated his intention to reach a ‘permanent’ agreement with Iran. The COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, collapsed over the issue of fossil fuels, failing to adopt a final agreement. Domestically in the US, the FBI’s National Crime Information Centre (NCIC) was reportedly hacked, with Trump stating in an interview that “this is not a good thing”.

Key News

1. The minimum margin requirement for margin trading on the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges has been reduced to 80 per cent; the ChiNext board is set to introduce market makers; and the exchanges have rolled out a new round of fee reduction measures

According to the Shenzhen Stock Exchange website (as reported by The Paper), on 22 November, following approval by the China Securities Regulatory Commission, the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges issued a notice lowering the minimum margin ratio for investors’ margin purchases of securities from 100 per cent to 80 per cent, with the measure taking effect on 30 November. In January this year, the three stock exchanges raised this ratio from 80 per cent to 100 per cent in response to market conditions at the time; the notice states that, as the activity in margin trading has recently declined significantly and the overall level of leverage remains low, the ratio has been appropriately reduced in accordance with counter-cyclical adjustment measures. For margin contracts that remain open prior to the adjustment, securities firms may recalculate investors’ available margin balances in accordance with the new regulations. The notice also requires securities firms to strictly apply the margin ratio and conversion rate to investors engaging in abnormal trading behaviour; in serious cases, margin trading and short selling services may be restricted, suspended or terminated.

According to a report by Xinhua News Agency (reprinted by China Youth Net), the margin requirement has been reduced from 100 per cent to 80 per cent, the leverage ratio for margin trading has been raised from 1:1 to 1.25:1, and the scale of margin purchases has increased by 25 per cent, which could theoretically bring in nearly 400 billion yuan in additional capital. This is merely a theoretical estimate; the actual amount of capital entering the market will depend on investors’ risk appetite and the credit limits granted by securities firms.

Two further measures were announced on the same day. According to the *Daily Economic News*, the Shenzhen Stock Exchange has issued a public consultation on the *Detailed Rules for Market-Making Transactions in ChiNext Stocks*: securities firms that have already obtained market-making qualifications for listed securities must apply to act as market makers for ChiNext stocks via a filing procedure, and must use dedicated market-making securities accounts; the Shenzhen Stock Exchange will conduct regular evaluations of market-making services. With regard to fee reductions, according to Xinhua News Agency, the Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges will waive trading unit usage fees and extend the measures implemented at the start of the year—including the waiver of listing fees and bond transaction handling fees—for a further two years until 2028; China Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation will continue to temporarily waive issuer service fees and settlement fees for green, science and technology innovation, and private enterprise corporate bonds (excluding convertible bonds) from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2028. Key points to watch: whether A-shares will be able to halt their decline on Monday on the back of these policies; changes in margin trading balances following the implementation on 30 November; and the official implementation date of the market-making rules.

Source:The Paper (Shenzhen Stock Exchange)、China Youth Net (Xinhua News Agency)、Daily Economic News、Beijing Business Daily (Sina Finance)

2. Anthropic mathematicians claim to have proven the Riemann Hypothesis with Claude; the Lean and Mathlib communities have verified the formal proof, and the mathematical community’s review has only just begun (20–22 November)

According to a compilation by Pasquale Pillitteri of the Italian technology media, on 20 November, Levent Alpöge—a mathematician at Anthropic and a Young Fellow of the Harvard Society of Scholars—published a four-page statement on his personal website claiming that he had “collaborated with Claude to discover a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis”, a method that could also be extended to the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis. The proof was published in the form of Lean code, comprising approximately 17 million lines and divided into 14 compressed files. First proposed in 1859, the Riemann Hypothesis is one of the seven ‘Millennium Problems’ set by the Clay Mathematics Institute; of these seven problems, only the Poincaré Conjecture has been resolved to date.

On 21 November, according to Lean’s official account, Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement certifying the proof as correct. According to the statement, dozens of people independently compiled the code and obtained the same proof output files; two independent tools confirmed that the proven theorem was the formulation of the Riemann Hypothesis already peer-reviewed in Mathlib; the proof also passed verification by the official Lean kernel and multiple third-party verifiers. According to the verification page maintained by MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland, the confirmed scope covers the Riemann Hypothesis itself and the generalised Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions; other classes of L-functions are not included.

It is important to recognise the limitations of this certification. Whilst it confirms that the logical chain is sound and the theorems are correctly stated, this does not mean that mathematicians have yet grasped the reasoning behind the proof. Alpöge stated that the machine-generated, human-readable version exceeds 250,000 pages; he has not published it, but has promised to write a formal paper at a later date; There is currently no preprint, nor has it been submitted to any journal. The Clay Institute’s rules require publication in a qualified journal, followed by a two-year waiting period and widespread recognition; it is therefore too early to discuss the prize. The announcement coincided with the day after Anthropic’s IPO; in his statement, Alpöge acknowledged that the timing was “very poor” but denied any connection to the IPO; At the time of going to press, Anthropic itself had not issued a statement. Points to watch for in the future: Alpöge’s formal paper, the assessment of the proof’s approach by number theorists, and the unpublished results on the Navier–Stokes equations, released on the same day by Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster of New York University, which have not yet undergone external peer review.

Source:Pasquale Pillitteri、Lean（X）

3. Iran attacked around 25 oil tankers in a single week, claiming to have struck US military radar installations in Bahrain, whilst shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz continued to decline; Trump said he wanted to reach a ‘permanent’ agreement

According to a report by Al Jazeera on 22 November, the Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed to have launched a combined missile and drone strike on two US military bases in Bahrain—Riffa and Awali—destroying two ‘Patriot’ air defence radars, describing the action as retaliation for the US military’s strike on a radar facility on Iran’s Hengam Island; Bahraini authorities said that one of the Iranian attacks struck a civilian industrial facility, causing a fire. The Iranian military also claimed to have damaged a US F-16 fighter jet, a claim denied by US Central Command, which stated that all US military aircraft were “safe and undamaged”. Al Jazeera’s correspondent in Tehran reported that approximately 25 oil tankers had been struck in the Strait of Hormuz area since 14 November; CNN, citing data from the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), reported that as of Sunday, at least 22 attacks had been reported in November, whilst October had seen the number exceed 30 for the first time. As the two organisations use different methodologies, there are discrepancies in the figures.

Shipping has already been affected. According to Al Jazeera, citing the UKMTO, a total of 263 vessels passed through the Strait between 12 and 18 November, 15 per cent fewer than the previous week; of these, 37 were oil tankers, compared with 52 the week before. Prior to the conflict, approximately 135 vessels passed through daily. Insurance premiums for shipping in the Gulf have risen from 0.10 per cent to 0.25 per cent before the war to between 3.5 per cent and 7.5 per cent. The International Energy Agency estimates that the conflict has resulted in cumulative supply losses of 2.8 billion barrels of oil and 60 billion cubic metres of natural gas in the Gulf region. Oil prices, which had previously exceeded US$100 per barrel, currently stand at around US$83.

According to CNN, the US blockade of Iranian ports remains in place; shipping data firm Kpler previously estimated that Iran’s oil revenues could fall to zero by mid-December, and analysts believe this is precisely why Iran has stepped up its attacks in an attempt to force the US to make concessions. Iranian President Pezeshizian has stated that Iran is facing a “silent economic war”. Earlier this month, Mr Trump said the US “did not need” to reach an agreement with Iran, but according to Al Jazeera, he said on Saturday that he planned to reach a “permanent” agreement with Iran to prevent it from acquiring nuclear weapons. Key points to watch: the reaction of oil prices on Monday, whether the US will respond militarily to the attacks, and whether there is any progress in the Qatar-mediated negotiations.

Source:Al Jazeera、CNN

4. COP31 broke down over the issue of fossil fuels and failed to adopt a final agreement; the UN Secretary-General expressed his “deep disappointment”.

According to CBS News, on Sunday 22 November, the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31), held in Antalya, Turkey, concluded without a final agreement being reached. Negotiations involving nearly 200 countries ran over time, making it the longest COP in history. The conference president, Turkey’s Environment Minister Kurum, stated that the draft had not been adopted due to a lack of consensus; COP rules require unanimous consent, meaning that opposition from a single country can bring the entire process to a standstill. UN Secretary-General Guterres said in a statement that he was “deeply disappointed”, adding that “there may be no consensus in the negotiating room, but there is no ambiguity in the science”.

The disagreement centred on fossil fuels. According to CBS News, the initial draft stated that action would be “accelerated to phase out fossil fuels”, but this wording was removed a few days later; the EU refused to accept a document that “did not even mention fossil fuels”, aligning itself with climate-vulnerable small states in opposition to oil-producing nations such as Saudi Arabia. Previously, a coalition led by France and Panama had rejected the previous draft on the grounds that it was “too weak”. Australian Climate and Energy Minister Bowen, who co-chaired the session with Turkey, said the negotiations had broken down due to “unbridgeable red lines”. For the second year running, the United States did not send a representative to the conference.

The draft had proposed targets such as capturing billions of tonnes of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere annually by 2035, and electricity accounting for more than a third of global energy supply, but these were all shelved along with the draft. According to the draft, if all countries’ existing climate plans were fully implemented, global warming would be slightly above 2.5 degrees Celsius. Key issues to watch: the hosting arrangements for COP32 next year, and whether the EU and oil-producing nations can reach a compromise on the wording regarding fossil fuels.

Source:CBS News、GoLocalProv (The Guardian)

5. The FBI’s National Crime Information Centre has reportedly been hacked, with a series of incidents affecting US government systems within a week; Trump said, “This is not a good thing.”

According to Al Jazeera, CNN and NBC reported on 20 November, citing two sources familiar with the matter, that the FBI’s National Crime Information Centre (NCIC) had been hacked. This database is used by law enforcement agencies across the United States to share information on wanted persons and background checks. On 21 November, Trump said in an interview with Fox News: “ ‘We’ve just heard about this; it’s not good news, and I don’t like it.’ However, he went on to say that there could be a ‘positive side’ to the incident, adding, ‘Perhaps we’ll find out things we would never have known otherwise’, and referred to the ‘Russia investigation hoax’. Neither the FBI nor the Department of Agriculture responded to Al Jazeera’s requests for comment, and FBI Director Patel has not made any public statement.

This is not an isolated incident. According to Al Jazeera, this would be the second time the FBI has been breached this year: in September, the FBI stated it was investigating an incident in which a cybercriminal group claimed to have stolen sensitive information belonging to 38,000 of its employees; NBC also reported that the US Department of Agriculture had allegedly suffered a cyberattack, and over the past week, a number of breaches and system disruptions within the US government have raised concerns amongst officials.

AI is changing the pace of cyber attacks and defences. According to *The New York Times* (as reported by GoLocalProv), security researcher Paulos Yibelo used AI to discover a vulnerability known as ‘Upload2Shell’: By uploading a file containing a custom font, it is possible to exploit Ghostscript—a 38-year-old image conversion programme—to execute malicious code, potentially affecting software from a large number of companies including Google and Zoom. The developers and relevant companies have already released patches. The report cites the views of intelligence and security experts, who believe that AI will make serious technological disruptions more common. Key points to watch: the FBI’s official statement on the NCIC incident, the scope of the affected data, and whether Congress will hold hearings on government cybersecurity.

Source:Al Jazeera、GoLocalProv (The New York Times)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

The most immediate factor affecting Monday’s market is the A-share market. The reduction in margin financing ratios, the introduction of market makers on the ChiNext board and fee cuts were all rolled out over the weekend; the policy intent is clear: to inject liquidity and stability into the market at a time when trading volumes are shrinking and the index is approaching the 3,500-point mark. The previous adjustment in January this year, which raised the ratio from 80 per cent to 100 per cent, was intended to cool down overheating; this reversal indicates that regulators’ assessment of the market has shifted. However, margin trading funds are pro-cyclical in nature; as the new regulations do not come into effect until 30 November, any increase in capital remains purely theoretical. Should overseas technology shares continue to fall, leveraged investors may not be keen to enter the market immediately. Bank shares, securities firm shares and those related to market-making operations are likely to react first.

External risks remain tight. Whilst attacks in the Strait of Hormuz have increased and the volume of shipping and the number of oil tankers have fallen, oil prices are hovering around US$83, indicating that the market still considers a full-scale war unlikely; should the US mount a military response, oil prices would be repriced, introducing a new inflationary variable for global markets already under pressure from interest rate rises. News regarding the Riemann Hypothesis may boost sentiment towards Anthropic and the AI sector, as it demonstrates the models’ capabilities in cutting-edge mathematics; however, it will not alter the market’s primary concerns from last week, namely the return on AI capital expenditure and the accounting investigation into Nvidia. The inconclusive outcome of COP31 provides a short-term boost to fossil fuels whilst dampening expectations for new energy policies; with US government systems suffering a series of cyber-attacks, the cybersecurity sector is likely to attract attention.

  • Will the attacks in the Strait of Hormuz continue to escalate? Brent and WTI oil prices, and shipping insurance premiums
  • The US response to the attack on Bahrain; US-Iran negotiations mediated by Qatar
  • Alpöge’s formal paper and the mathematical community’s assessment; has Anthropic issued a statement?
  • This week’s US PCE and revised GDP figures, pre-Thanksgiving trading, the SEC investigation into Nvidia and updates on its 10-Q filing
  • How has the Anthropic leveraged ETF performed since its listing on Monday? Will ANT hold onto its $83 issue price?
  • The FBI’s statement on the NCIC incident: Is the US government’s cyber security incident continuing to escalate?
