Daily Briefing | 24 November 2026: NVIDIA’s board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO; share price rises 31 per cent in a single day, the Nasdaq rose 6.92 per cent, marking its largest single-day gain since April 2025; the ChiNext Index rose 3.08 per cent, whilst regulators issued a notice on counter-cyclical risk management for margin trading and short selling; Iran stated it would use all means to counter US sanctions; and Oracle confirmed that all funds under its contract with OpenAI have been fully disbursed

Today's main theme

This issue covers the period from the Asian trading session on Monday 23 November to the close of US markets, with a separate section on Oracle beginning after the close of US markets on 20 November. Following last week’s sharp fall in AI stocks, this Monday saw a vengeance rally, spearheaded by Nvidia: the board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as President, CEO and Director, less than two weeks after his resignation, with Donald Trump subsequently posting a message of support. NVIDIA closed up by approximately 31 per cent, marking its best single-day performance since 2003, with its market capitalisation increasing by around US$954 billion in a single day; the Nasdaq rose by 6.92 per cent and the S&P 500 by 5.03 per cent, the Dow rose by 3.21 per cent, with all three indices recording their largest single-day gains since April 2025. Oracle announced over the weekend that all the funds required to fulfil its OpenAI contract had been secured, and its share price also surged on Monday. In the A-share market, on the first trading day following the introduction of new margin trading regulations over the weekend, the Shanghai Composite Index briefly fell to around 3,476 points during the morning session before closing up 0.15 per cent, whilst the ChiNext Index rose 3.08 per cent, with trading volume expanding to approximately 1.55 trillion yuan; According to the Securities Times, regulators also issued a notice to securities firms, requiring them to handle margin trading defaults in a prudent and measured manner and to avoid concentrated selling. In the Middle East, Iran’s Foreign Ministry stated it would use “all available means” to counter the US maritime blockade; whilst tensions have not eased, oil prices actually fell on Monday.

Key News

1. NVIDIA’s board of directors has unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO; Trump described it as “the right decision”, whilst the internal investigation continues

According to a press release issued by NVIDIA on 23 November and filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the Board of Directors voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang to his positions as President, Chief Executive Officer and member of the Board, with immediate effect. The Board stated that it was satisfied with Huang Renxun’s candour and leadership abilities, and noted that it had received advice from “senior US government officials” on the basis of which it concluded that his return “does not pose a national security risk”. Chairman Harvey Jones remains in his post; interim CEO Corette Kress has returned to her role as Chief Financial Officer. In a statement, Huang Renxun said he accepted this responsibility with “the utmost humility”.

The investigation did not end there. The press release stated that the board had previously established a committee comprising solely independent directors to investigate reports that Huang Renxun had been the target of foreign intelligence operations; the committee had engaged independent legal counsel, and the investigation would be ‘pursued to the end’, with Huang Renxun expressing his full support. NVIDIA has also established a new National Security and Export Control Committee, is recruiting a Chief Compliance Officer, and has stated that it will continue to cooperate with government investigations and enquiries. According to Al Jazeera, Huang resigned on 12 November following revelations of at least two extramarital affairs (one of which involved a suspected spy); At the time of his reinstatement, multiple investigations into NVIDIA by the SEC and the Department of Justice were still ongoing. According to 24/7 Wall St, NVIDIA’s quarterly report (10-Q) for the quarter ending in October remains delayed.

The White House’s stance is clear. According to Al Jazeera, Mr Trump stated on social media that the Nvidia board had made a “correct and appropriate decision”, and that the US government had “no reason to believe” that Mr Huang’s return would pose a national security risk. A petition calling for his return had previously garnered over 180,000 signatures, including one from Mr Musk. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives stated in a report that the board had “no choice”. Key points to watch going forward include the findings of the independent committee’s investigation, the timing of the 10-Q filing, and whether the House of Representatives’ closed-door hearing with NVIDIA executives in early December will proceed as scheduled.

Source:NVIDIA Press Release (SEC)、Al Jazeera、24/7 Wall St

2. US shares stage a sharp rebound: the Nasdaq rises 6.92 per cent, Nvidia surges 31 per cent, adding approximately US$954 billion to its market capitalisation in a single day

According to TipRanks’ closing report, on 23 November, the Nasdaq rose by 1,563 points, or 6.92 per cent, whilst the S&P 500 gained 5.03 per cent, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose by 1,556 points, or 3.21 per cent; all three indices recorded their largest single-day gains since April 2025, when Trump eased tariffs. Last Friday, the Nasdaq fell by 5 per cent, erasing all of its year-to-date gains, whilst the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average entered a technical correction last week.

NVIDIA was undoubtedly the star of the show. According to 24/7 Wall St, NVIDIA closed at US$165.05, up approximately 31 per cent from last Friday’s US$125.53, with its market capitalisation increasing by around US$954 billion in a single day; This marked its best single day since 2003 and its third-largest single-day gain since 1999. However, the share price remains approximately 31 per cent below its record closing high of $239.24 on 6 October. The AI hardware sector staged a collective rebound: According to TipRanks and 24/7 Wall St, Micron rose by approximately 19 per cent, CoreWeave by approximately 20 per cent, Dell by 20.46 per cent, HPE by 17.38 per cent, AMD by approximately 6 per cent, whilst Alphabet and Meta both rose by over 5 per cent. Funds flowed out of defensive sectors, with Chevron, UnitedHealth, Johnson & Johnson and Coca-Cola all falling, whilst CrowdStrike dropped by 8.92 per cent. According to Beinsure, Anthropic (ANT) did not follow the broader market rally, trading at $87.69 around 2.00 pm Eastern Time, down 1.37 per cent (intraday data).

Signals from interest rates and commodities are relatively subdued. According to a pre-market report by JournalArta, Brent crude fell by 4.55 per cent to US$88.36 per barrel, as the resumption of oil exports from the Middle East and market expectations of a global supply glut overshadowed regional tensions; the VIX fear index retreated to around 30. Data cited by Beinsure showed that market expectations of a Federal Reserve rate rise on 9 December remained at around 90 per cent, down slightly from 98 per cent on 12 November; Goldman Sachs believes that a sustained market bottom requires three conditions: confidence in AI earnings, a more accommodative Federal Reserve and lower investor positions, whilst Nomura and others pointed out that short-covering and option hedging amplified Monday’s gains. US stock markets will have a shortened trading week: October’s PCE and the revised third-quarter GDP figures will be released on Wednesday 25 November; markets will be closed on 26 November for Thanksgiving, and will close early at 1.00 pm on 27 November. Key points to watch: whether the rally can continue beyond the PCE data release, and AI server order figures in the earnings reports from Dell and HP Enterprise.

Source:TipRanks、24/7 Wall St、Beinsure、JournalArta

3. A-shares bottomed out and rebounded, with the ChiNext Index rising by 3.08 per cent; regulators issued a notice on margin trading and short selling, calling for a prudent and measured approach to the handling of defaults to avoid concentrated selling.

According to Sina Finance’s closing summary, on 23 November, the Shanghai Composite Index closed up 0.15 per cent at 3,538.95 points, having fallen to around 3,476 points during the morning session—a new low in the current correction; the Shenzhen Component Index rose by 1.93 per cent to close at 10,454.37 points; the ChiNext Index rose by 3.08 per cent to close at 2,280.2 points. According to FX168, trading volume across both markets totalled approximately 1.55 trillion yuan, an increase of around 130.7 billion yuan compared with the previous trading day; more than 4,600 individual stocks rose, with 151 hitting the daily upper limit. The humanoid robots, gearboxes, cinema chains, healthcare services and electric motors sectors led the gains; Sina’s data showed the humanoid robots sector rose by 5.91 per cent; Insurance, oil and gas extraction, coal, automotive manufacturing and shares held by the China Securities Finance Corporation led the declines, whilst the banking and white spirit sectors were also consolidating. Compared with last week, the market sentiment shifted from blue-chip stocks propping up the market to a rebound in growth stocks.

In addition to the three measures announced at the weekend, the authorities have introduced a further policy measure. According to a report by the Securities Times, as cited by Sina, the regulatory authorities have issued a ‘Notice on Implementing Counter-Cyclical Adjustment Measures for Margin Trading and Short Selling and Enhancing the Effectiveness of Risk Management’ to securities firms: during periods of market volatility, securities firms may, after fully communicating with clients and obtaining their confirmation, dynamically adjust the minimum maintenance margin ratio and margin call and liquidation arrangements, thereby ‘buying time’; The handling of defaults must be conducted in a prudent and measured manner, with due attention paid to the amount, timing and pace of actions, to avoid concentrated selling during the early or late trading sessions that could disrupt the market or individual shares. The full text of the notice and specific figures have not yet been made public. The new regulation reducing the margin requirement for margin trading from 100 per cent to 80 per cent will still come into effect on 30 November.

Funds from margin trading and short selling had previously been flowing out. According to the Securities Times website, as of 18 November, the balance of margin trading and short selling on the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing stock exchanges stood at 2,237.83 billion yuan, having fallen for four consecutive trading days, with a total reduction of 65.035 billion yuan. Furthermore, according to a summary by Sina, the first batch of 18 actively managed ETFs were listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges that day, raising a total of approximately 36 billion yuan. Key points to watch going forward include whether the margin trading balance can halt its decline and rebound, changes in margin buying following the implementation of new regulations on 30 November, and whether the Shanghai Composite Index can hold steady above the 3,500-point mark.

Source:Sina Finance (16:00)、Sina Finance (21:00)、FX168、East Money (Securities Times Online)

4. Iran says it will use “all available means” to counter the US naval blockade, whilst Trump says strikes may resume after the New Year

According to Al Jazeera, citing AFP and Reuters, on 23 November, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Baghaei stated at a press conference that Iran would use “all available means” to counter US “aggression”. He described US sanctions and the naval blockade as “acts of military aggression” and asserted that Iran had the right to defend itself under Article 51 of the United Nations Charter. The US blockade began on 13 April, was temporarily lifted in June following a provisional understanding, and was reinstated on 14 July after the agreement collapsed. Baghaei also stated that if the US ceased hostile actions, lifted the blockade and granted sanctions exemptions, the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened to shipping within two days. Al Jazeera’s correspondent in Tehran reported that military action targeting US assets in the vicinity of Iran is under discussion, and that “escalation is currently the main option”.

The US tone was equally firm. According to a summary of live broadcasts by Iran’s International TV, Trump said on Fox News on 22 November that, should no agreement be reached, he would be inclined to resume strikes against Iran ‘after the New Year’, adding that any Iranian vessels attempting to breach the blockade would be sunk to the bottom of the sea. Israeli media reported that US Central Command is on high alert, but the US has postponed any potential strikes by at least a week. On the Iranian side, the Deputy Commander of the Revolutionary Guards stated that Iran’s response would have “no geographical limits”.

The avenues for mediation are narrowing. According to Iran’s International TV, the US State Department had previously announced that Secretary of State Rubio would meet with Mohammed, the Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs of Qatar, in Washington on 23 November; However, on the same day, Iran summoned Qatar’s chargé d’affaires ad interim to protest against a joint German-Qatari statement condemning Iran’s attacks, with some Iranian MPs stating that Qatar could “no longer act as an effective mediator”. On the oil front, according to Al Jazeera, visible exports of Iranian crude have been close to zero in recent months. Last week, TankerTrackers detected a tanker loading Iranian crude via ship-to-ship transfer in the Gulf, which may have been the first export since July; Baghaei described the relevant reports as “speculation”. Key points to watch: the outcome of Senator Rubio’s talks with the Qatari Prime Minister; whether there will be further attacks in the Strait of Hormuz; and whether the US will take military action before the end of the year.

Source:Al Jazeera、Iran International TV (Live)

5. Oracle stated that all the funding required for the OpenAI contract had been secured, and its share price surged by around 12 per cent on Monday (20–23 November)

According to The Next Web, on 20 November (Friday), one minute after the US stock market closed, Oracle posted on X stating that the company had secured all the funding required to fulfil its contract with OpenAI, making it “the first hyperscale cloud provider to have fully secured funding in this investment cycle”. It added that media reports to the contrary were “incorrect”, though it did not specify which reports it was referring to. Oracle stated that the financing arrangements announced at its Investor Day (including supplier financing) had been finalised, and that further discussions with suppliers were merely “additional options” upon which fulfilment did not depend. The statement also noted that the Abilene campus in Texas is nearing completion, the next gigawatt-scale Shackelford campus has secured all necessary permits, and NVIDIA’s Vera Rubin racks will be delivered in the coming weeks; the company expects free cash flow to turn positive in the second half of the 2028 financial year and to exceed US$30 billion in the 2029 financial year.

The ‘final round of funding earlier this month’ referred to in the statement—according to the pricing announcement and 8-K compiled by Ultrathink—was a funding round of approximately US$20 billion, priced on 2 November and completed on 4 November: Approximately 86.21 million ordinary shares were issued at US$116 per share, alongside US$10 billion in senior notes across five tranches with coupon rates ranging from 7 per cent to 8.65 per cent. However, the 8-K states that the proceeds are to be used for general corporate purposes and are not specifically earmarked for the OpenAI project; Nor did the statement provide details of the contract value, duration or total computing power. According to The Next Web, citing Reuters, investors have consistently questioned Oracle’s capital requirements and its reliance on OpenAI, as its data centre expenditure has already exceeded quarterly revenue; Microsoft has also begun to phase out its agreement to lease AI computing power from Oracle this month.

The market took this news in its stride. According to MarketScreener (MT Newswires), Oracle’s share price rose by around 12 per cent on Monday. Key points to watch going forward: changes in capital expenditure and free cash flow in Oracle’s December quarterly report, and whether the Vera Rubin racks can be delivered on schedule.

Source:The Next Web、Ultrathink、MarketScreener、Oracle Bones (X)

Market Correlation and Today's Observation

Monday’s rebound was largely a reaction to two concerns from last week. Jensen Huang’s reinstatement resolved the ‘management vacuum’ at Nvidia, whilst Oracle’s statement addressed the question of ‘where the funding for AI infrastructure will come from’. However, last week’s most pressing issues remain unresolved: the SEC’s investigation into Nvidia’s accounting issues, the delayed 10-Q filing and the independent committee’s inquiry have yet to reach a conclusion, and Nvidia’s share price remains approximately 31 per cent below its all-time high. Such a substantial single-day gain was largely driven by short-covering and option hedging; whether the rebound can be sustained will depend on this week’s PCE data and AI server orders from Dell and HP Enterprise. Anthropic edged lower amidst a broad market rally, suggesting that capital has largely returned to established AI hardware stocks.

Interest rates and oil prices have provided some temporary relief. Brent crude has fallen to around $88, and the VIX has retreated, but the probability of a Fed rate rise in December remains at around 90 per cent; should the PCE figures come in on the high side, Monday’s optimism could be dampened. Iran’s rhetoric continues to escalate, and the market is currently focusing more on expectations of a supply glut; should there be further attacks in the Strait or US military action, oil prices would be repriced. In the A-share market, Monday saw increased trading volumes, with growth stocks rebounding whilst blue-chip and dividend-yielding shares consolidated. The margin trading circular has incorporated the requirement to ‘avoid concentrated liquidation’ into regulatory guidelines, aimed at preventing a stampede among leveraged investors; following the implementation of the new regulations on 30 November, whether the margin trading balance can rebound will be key to gauging the policy’s effectiveness.

Checklist:

  • Will Nvidia be able to hold onto Monday’s gains? SEC investigation, 10-Q filing deadline and the findings of the independent committee’s inquiry
  • 25 November: US October PCE and revised third-quarter GDP figures; market pricing for an interest rate rise on 9 December
  • AI server orders and guidance in Dell and HPE’s financial reports: can Oracle’s share price hold its ground?
  • Will Anthropic hold onto its $83 IPO price? MSCI inclusion on 1 December
  • Will the Shanghai Composite Index hold steady above 3,500 points? Changes in margin trading balances; new regulations come into force on 30 November
  • Outcome of Rubio’s talks with the Qatari Prime Minister, new attack in the Strait of Hormuz, Brent crude oil prices
  • The impact on liquidity of the market closure on Thanksgiving (26th) and the early close on 27th
